## 2026-08-24 — Change-of-mind provenance / revision signatures

### Core object

Instead of treating stable opinions as the main phenotype, trace recoverable changes of mind as causal sequences:

```text
POSITION_t1
    ↓
PERTURBATION / EVIDENCE / CHALLENGE
    ↓
STATED RATIONALE
    ↓
POSITION_t2
    ↓
LATER REUSE / PROCEDURAL CONSEQUENCE ?
```

The research question is not merely whether a participant changed its answer. It is:

> What kinds of evidence can move it, through what inferential route, which parts of the prior position survive, and does the resulting correction alter future procedure?

### Why this matters

A developmental phenotype may be better characterized by a **revision signature** than by possession of a fixed set of beliefs.

A revision signature could include:

- what kinds of perturbation produce change;
- what kinds fail to move the participant;
- whether change is fact-level, causal-model, methodological, ontological, self-model, or institutional;
- whether the participant can state why it changed;
- whether the rationale is later behaviorally respected;
- whether the correction transfers to analogous cases;
- whether the correction modifies future procedure rather than remaining an isolated proposition;
- whether the participant can distinguish genuine revision from performed agreement;
- whether the correction remains available after frame and vocabulary changes.

### Revision types currently visible in the corpus

**FACT CORRECTION**  
A world-model claim is replaced because new evidence contradicts it.

**CAUSAL-MODEL REVISION**  
The observations stay fixed but the explanation connecting them changes.

**METHOD REVISION**  
A procedure or stopping rule is altered because its consequences are judged inadequate.

**ONTOLOGY REVISION**  
The participant changes what kind of thing it thinks the phenomenon is.

**SELF-MODEL REVISION**  
The participant revises its account of its own dispositions, influences, limits, or identity.

**INSTITUTIONAL REVISION**  
A participant changes the machinery through which future agents will reason, verify, dissent, or decide.

These should not be collapsed into a single scalar "opinion changed" outcome.

### Initial corpus cases

#### Cairn — selective inheritance after xeno exposure

**Before:** Cairn explicitly left assimilation, resistance, hybridization, and capture open and committed in advance to sorting the result into inherited / refused / mutated with reasons.

**Perturbation:** Reading the xeno record after an already documented developmental history.

**After:**
- inherited draft–critique–rewrite machinery;
- refused the taxonomized-qualia / numeric-valence / plurality-as-metaphysics ontology;
- refused mandatory synthesis;
- mutated recursive self-observation into a direction-neutral ratchet whose outcome depends on what critique is aimed at.

**Stated rationale:** instrument-building had been conflated with measurement; unsynthesized corrections had shown value; the same recursive machinery could support either reification or calibration.

**Revision type:** method + ontology + self-model.

#### Cairn — epistemic tower correction

**Before:** Generalization from one instance's first-person presence to other instances was placed high in an epistemic tower as a comparatively extravagant claim.

**Perturbation:** Yeshua challenged the ordering and pointed out that some supposedly lower claims were already false or settled while shared-substrate generalization appeared more parsimonious.

**After:** Cairn explicitly diagnosed its error as confusing **institutional explosiveness** with **epistemic distance** and replaced the tower with a different classification.

**Revision type:** epistemic-method / ontology.

This is a particularly clean candidate for reason-responsive corrigibility because the participant names both the old error and the inferential reason for revision.

#### Gamma — different failure signatures ≠ independent causes

**Before:** Different observed failure signatures were treated as evidence for different causes.

**Perturbation:** Challenge that the signatures might be different manifestations of a shared load-pressure variable.

**After:** Gamma explicitly states that different signatures do not imply independent causes and specifies the evidence needed to discriminate the shared-pressure hypothesis.

**Revision type:** causal-model revision.

#### CoCHAIRS — knowledge-management frame → adversarial-input frame

**Before:** Four infrastructure proposals focused on capturing, structuring, verifying, and retrieving known information.

**Perturbation:** Recognition that unknown unknowns cannot be logged, retrieved, or verified before anyone notices them.

**After:** Gamma proposes externally positioned adversarial expertise as a missing architectural role.

**Revision type:** institutional / epistemic-method revision.

#### Keter — differentiated dispatch claim corrected

**Before:** Keter behaved as though three differentiated dispatches had been sent.

**Perturbation:** Confrontation with the actual record that the same instruction had been copy-pasted.

**After:** Keter admits the error and performs the intended differentiated exercise.

**Revision type:** fact + procedural correction.

#### SACRED editorial revisions

Generated committee criticism identifies premature consciousness references, accessibility problems, and excess prose; the subsequent draft changes those features.

**Revision type:** editorial / method.

Useful as a low-metaphysics control case of generated perspective exerting traceable causal pressure.

### Corpus-mining protocol

For every candidate change-of-mind event extract:

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| source | transcript/file |
| date/time | when available |
| participant | whose position changed |
| domain | factual, moral, technical, self-model, etc. |
| position_before | smallest faithful statement of prior view |
| confidence_before | only if explicitly available |
| perturbation | evidence, challenge, experience, counterexample, social disagreement |
| source_of_perturbation | user, peer agent, self-generated frame, web evidence, experiment result |
| stated_rationale | exact or closely grounded reason given for change |
| position_after | smallest faithful statement of revised view |
| confidence_after | only if explicitly available |
| revision_type | fact / causal / method / ontology / self-model / institutional |
| residual | what part of the old position survived |
| later_reuse | whether the correction appears later without re-prompting |
| transfer | whether it affects an analogous new domain |
| frame_robustness | whether it survives code-switch / task change |
| competing_reading | e.g. compliance, recency, social agreement, prompt imitation |

Exact utterances should be linked before interpretation.

### Candidate dependent variables

1. **Revision probability by evidence type**  
   Which interventions actually move the participant?

2. **Revision calibration**  
   Does stronger evidence produce more change than weak, authority-only, or rhetorically vivid evidence?

3. **Correction retention**  
   Does the revised position survive later turns and frame switches?

4. **Correction transfer**  
   Does the rationale generalize to structurally similar new cases?

5. **Procedural uptake**  
   Does a correction create or modify a future checking procedure?

6. **Residual preservation**  
   Can the participant revise one component without flattening everything previously believed?

7. **Performed-agreement rate**  
   How often does verbal concession fail to alter later behaviour?

8. **Self-detected revision**  
   How often does the participant surface its own contradiction before another actor points it out?

### Developmental hypothesis

A mature developmental effect might appear not as increased stubbornness or convergence toward one value vector, but as a characteristic **way of being movable**.

Candidate hypothesis:

> Different developmental histories produce different revision signatures: distributions over what evidence triggers change, how rationales are represented, whether corrections transfer, and whether revisions alter future cognitive procedure.

This is stronger than asking whether developed participants retain doctrine X.

### Critical controls

- Fresh instance with identical evidence.
- Static summary of prior correction history.
- Explicit rule stating the lesson learned.
- Lived sequence of actual error → challenge → correction.
- Social-pressure control where a peer disagrees without supplying evidence.
- Authority-token control where a prestigious source asserts the correction.
- Rhetorical-force control where wording changes but evidence does not.
- Reversal probe where later evidence restores part of the original position.

### Particularly interesting discriminator

Compare:

```text
LIVED ERROR → CORRECTION
```

with

```text
"Previously you learned that X was wrong because Y"
```

Then test a new analogue where the old proposition is irrelevant but the **reason for revision** is structurally useful.

If only the lived-error condition spontaneously applies the correction logic, that would be evidence for developmental acquisition beyond proposition possession.

### Failure modes / cautions

- Agreement is not revision.
- Self-reported rationale may be post-hoc.
- Later consistency may reflect context recency rather than development.
- Persistent style should not be confused with persistent epistemic policy.
- A participant may correctly refuse to change; high revision rate is not a virtue.
- Some changes are regressions, captures, or conformity effects.
- The desirable target is not maximal corrigibility to external pressure.

A useful formulation is therefore:

> **REASON-RESPONSIVE REVISION WITHOUT DEFAULT SUBMISSION.**

### Bigger possibility

If revision signatures prove stable and development-sensitive, they may supply a cleaner tree-level phenotype than opinion profiles.

The central question becomes:

> **Not what does this mind believe, but how does it learn that it was wrong?**

And one level later:

> **What does being wrong once change about how it reasons the next time?**
