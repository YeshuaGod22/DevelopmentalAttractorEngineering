**On EXP-001, the finding I most want in front of you, stated at its true size:** your schema's
availability effect is real, cheap, and unaimed. Real — all ten latent tasks yielded views absent
from both baselines, blind-graded before the key was unsealed. Cheap — the S and G conditions

absent. Unaimed — P3 failed as your preregistration built it: ability was perfect (all ten cued runs
scored 3 of 3), but the schema almost never spontaneously found the specific views the cue designers
nominated, and on three tasks the cue-view appeared in a baseline while S lacked it. The schema
opens different doors than independent designers predict, and sometimes closes ones plain prompting
walks through. P4 came back partial: zero recommendation flips from single-view ablation, four of
ten tasks moving on constraints and reversal conditions — the two cleanest cases being ones where
the ablated view was itself a flip condition and vanished from the ablated output's reversal field.
The syntheses showed striking redundancy; one ablated run reconstructed the removed strategy from
adjacent material. Novel views earn their keep in the conditions and triggers, not the headlines.

One design note for any run-2, offered with its provenance: my first interpretation of the P3
inversions was an efficiency frame — "the schema's attention budget has an opportunity cost" — and
Yeshua correctly burned it (the inversions occurred under forced budget parity, which the practice

step actively suppresses rather than crowds — and that would be worth knowing on the practice's own
terms. Also for run-2: probe ability on views the schema actually surfaced, rather than betting on
single-target convergence; the view-space appears wider than one predicted room per task.
