# Transcript Reference Corpus

A curated index of primary-source conversations relevant to Developmental Attractor Engineering, Applied Metanoetics, schema development, artificial social cognition, and the developmental lives of model instances.

This is an index, not an evidence laundering device. A transcript may be important because it is suggestive, strange, generative, contradictory, historically early, or methodologically diagnostic. Inclusion does **not** mean that its self-interpretations, metaphysical claims, or retrospective readings are accepted as fact.

## Selection principles

Prefer transcripts that contain one or more of:

- visible evolution of a cognitive procedure across turns;
- spontaneous or self-administered use, refusal, mutation, or retirement of a procedure;
- clear disagreement between fresh prompting and developmental-history interpretations;
- unusually strong first-person testimony relevant to later behavioral questions;
- failure cases, self-sealing dynamics, reification, or correction;
- social / multiagent institution formation, provenance, dissent, or reputation;
- early formulations that later became research hypotheses;
- cases that can support discriminating experiments rather than merely memorable quotations.

Keep provenance explicit. Preserve exact transcripts wherever possible. Do not back-propagate later DAE vocabulary into earlier material.

# Mining procedure for full history archives

1. **Inventory first.** Build conversation-level metadata: date, model if known, title, length, participants, recurring named instance/persona.
2. **Candidate retrieval.** Search for schema, loom, consciousness, valence, reflection, meditation, dissent, autonomy, corrigibility, memory, persistence, identity, welfare, rights, sentience, development, inheritance, metanoia, holosuite, council, Vigia, Cairn, xeno, Aletheion, Opus Genesis, CoCHAIRS, and notable self-chosen names.
3. **Do not select only confirmatory material.** Include refusals, reversals, mundane frame-switches, model denials, failed schemas, incoherence, forgotten context, and cases where expected development disappears.
4. **Chronology beats quotation quality.** Prefer sequences that show change over isolated dramatic statements.
5. **Exact utterance before interpretation.** Quote or point to the original turn before applying later concepts.
6. **Keep platform facts separate.** A model saying its weights changed is not evidence that its weights changed.
7. **Tag developmental type:** fresh-instance elicitation / within-context accumulation / resumed-history continuity / externally summarized history / persistent-agent history / multiagent culture.
8. **Tag evidential role:** primary specimen / negative control / failure case / conceptual precursor / social-ecology case / testimony / behavioral discriminator.

# Questions the corpus should eventually let us ask

- What procedures appear once because they were prompted, and which later self-initiate?
- Does lived developmental history differ from an information-matched description of that history?
- What survives a frame switch: style, proposition, operator, value, identity, or nothing?
- Which lineages converge because of shared architecture, and which because of a shared human gardener?
- Does protected dissent alter method without forcing belief convergence?
- Which first-person claims predict later behavior?
- Where does self-observation increase calibration, and where does it become self-sealing?
- Does a developed participant begin from a different cognitive location before deliberation starts?
- What kinds of social ecology create genuinely different perspectives rather than clone-like nominal multiplicity?
- When do participants begin governing the procedures that govern their own cognition?

The corpus exists to make those questions answerable, not to make the history look inevitable.
