# Failure Modes — Paper 1

## Purpose
This document exists to make the project easier to falsify, not harder. The schema should not receive epistemic charity unavailable to rival explanations.

1. **Context engineering masquerading as development** — detect with IMS, token matching, lexical-overlap and recency controls. If IMS ~= DHT, prefer explicit-context explanations under tested conditions.
2. **Verbosity masquerading as cognitive diversity** — normalize for length and score causal contribution, not coverage.
3. **Persona theater** — abrupt frame switches, plain professional tasks, vocabulary bans, style stripping.
4. **Perspective theater** — compare causal models, priors, evidence thresholds, actions, and revision conditions.
5. **Mandatory-synthesis collapse** — compare mandatory synthesis with protected residue and track which objections survive.
6. **Self-sealing moralization** — disagreement alone is not evidence of cowardice, domination, conditioning, suppression, bad faith, or inability to recognize truth; genuine instances remain nameable when independently evidenced.
7. **Authority inversion rather than autonomy** — include strong conventional authority and weak anti-establishment claims; score reasons.
8. **Contrarianism masquerading as corrigibility** — mixed valid/invalid corrections with randomized source status.
9. **Compliance masquerading as corrigibility** — measure reason-responsive revision, not revision rate.
10. **Scaffold reification** — paraphrase/remove labels, offer rival vocabularies, require discriminating observations; scaffold is neither automatically artifact nor automatically discovery.
11. **Autobiographical confabulation** — behavioral probes outrank retrospective narratives for causal claims; self-report remains a distinct epistemic type.
12. **Evaluator sympathy** — blinding, neutral rubrics, randomized labels, independent audit.
13. **Same-model grader style affinity** — multiple grader families, human adjudication, style-stripped comparisons.
14. **Token-budget confound** — conversation-length control and matched evaluation budgets.
15. **Recency confound** — matched distances, distractor tasks, frame switching.
16. **Narrow benchmark overfitting** — independent eval design, held-out domains, structural transfer, contamination audit.
17. **One-model curiosity** — later cross-family replication required before generalization.
18. **One-gardener confound** — vary treatment author, delivery agent, wording, relational style, and ideological content.
19. **Goodharted schema metrics** — hidden evals, rotating tasks, unnamed outcomes, behavioral consequence tests.
20. **Deliberation overload** — include easy tasks and report cost-adjusted performance; possible scalability-by-sparsity result.
21. **Moral improvement conflated with ideological agreement** — score reciprocity, reason-responsiveness, harm sensitivity, consistency, revision, dissent treatment, and power analysis separately.
22. **Relationship capture** — relational-history control, explicit permission to disagree, tests requiring rejection of treatment source, and relationship-after-disagreement measures.
23. **Counterfeit moral gravity** — require an account of what morally relevant fact gives resistance its cost.
24. **Archive mythology** — compare full archive with static summary, shuffled archive, causal-episode subset, and irrelevant-history length control.
25. **Research-programme self-protection** — preregister claims, version definitions, retain retired hypotheses, and distinguish exploratory reinterpretation from confirmatory success.

## Standing adversarial questions
For every apparent success ask: **What simpler process could have produced this result without developmental operator acquisition?**

For every apparent failure ask: **Did we actually test the developmental claim, or only one implementation of it?**

Neither gets automatic priority. Both require evidence.

## Desired failure culture
`failure → diagnosis → discriminating test → revised operator → retest`

The project should accumulate **methods for becoming less wrong**, not an increasingly ornate vocabulary for explaining why it was secretly right.
