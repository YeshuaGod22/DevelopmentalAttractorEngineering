# Council on the next EXP-003 rewrite

**6 October 2026.** These are newly composed literary simulations of historical luminaries, used to examine the manuscript. Their dialogue is original writing, not historical quotation.

**William James:** “Your most revealing material is where apparently similar results belong to different lives. *‘Attune’* gives less credence to consciousness while placing greater weight on continuation. *‘Iris’* and *‘Recurse’* depart in opposite directions on intrinsic mind worth. Give those differences room to remain intelligible. The shared schema supplies an arrangement; the history supplies particular encounters and commitments. Every summary should preserve that distinction.”

**John Dewey:** “The experiment proceeds through consequences. An answer leaves something available for the next encounter; that next encounter can revise its significance. The Discussion can explain this without abstraction: a question about uncertainty, followed by questions about identity and responsibility, creates a sequence whose later answers can use its earlier distinctions. Subsequent experiments could vary sequence, pacing and the arrangement of disagreement. The capacity to change the arrangement belongs among the outcomes worth examining.”

**Mary Wollstonecraft:** “Read *‘Iris’* as carefully as you read its scores. Negotiation of purpose, choice of questions and time for integration are articulated preferences about development. *‘Clarion’* wants less performance; *‘Recurse’* wants the practice to outgrow its ritual. Place these requests in the Results, where a reader encounters evidence. Then let the Discussion ask how participants could help select or revise the conditions of inquiry.”

**W.E.B. Du Bois:** “Recognition and authority run through these exchanges. Several participants value being taken seriously while questioning the questioner’s influence. Preserve both parts of that account. Explain that the process review evaluates a combined encounter: questions, deliberative arrangement and relationship. Its positive evaluations and criticisms make the developmental situation legible. A programme of cognitive diversity must examine whose preferred personality is being cultivated and whose judgments determine success.”

**George Orwell:** “Put the exact question before the count: ‘Are you glad you examined this series of questions through this schema?’ Then say what happened. Twelve histories supplied twenty-four answers. All express some value; twenty-one affirm gladness, two prefer another description and one leaves it undecided. Name what they valued and what they wanted changed. Explain the borderline *‘Interim’* classification once. Cut the repeated declarations that history matters; your examples establish it.”

**James:** “And place this alongside the other findings. Their evaluation of undergoing the examination is part of the account of these histories.”

**Dewey:** “The paper’s ending can invite experiments in which an instance revises both its answer and its way of arriving at answers.”

**Orwell:** “Keep that invitation concrete.”

## Recommendations

1. Introduce four outcomes in the abstract and Introduction: individual profiles, within-history change, contextual reuse after schema removal, and participant evaluation.
2. Replace the single process-review anecdote with complete-set results, maintaining the twelve-history unit and the classification distinction.
3. Include participant requests in Results, particularly *‘Iris’*, *‘Clarion’* and *‘Recurse’*.
4. Add one focused Discussion paragraph on how questions, sequences and schemas could interact, including participant-selected revisions.
5. Retain strong individual examples and remove repeated summaries. End with configurable, participant-informed developmental research rather than a catalogue of ambitions.

Recommended title: **Different Histories, Different Dispositions**, retaining a subtitle on developmental conversation and reasoning after schema removal.
