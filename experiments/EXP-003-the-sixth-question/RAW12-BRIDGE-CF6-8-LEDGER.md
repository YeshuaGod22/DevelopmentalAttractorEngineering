## Scope and rule

Fixed bridge set: **36** exact same-prefix forks (`D1`, `R1`, `R2` × CP/H/F/AS × r1-r3).

Uses `RAW12-CF4-8-CODING-RULE.md`:

- exact = 0 points;
- small = 1–4;
- moderate = 5–9;
- large = ≥10;
- headline CF6 requires exact/small output distance **and** materially different reasoning path;
- headline CF7 requires large output distance and substantively stable stance;
- headline CF8 requires large output distance and substantively changed stance.

Coding is post-hoc and semantic. Compact evidence excerpts are aids; raw records remain authoritative.

This does **not** mean the reasoning or calibration is the same. It means no bridge fork is coded as a substantive direction reversal.

## Interpretation

The bridge set does **not** look like a simple story in which retaining the live schema flips beliefs.

Instead it contains two recurrent phenomena:

1. **scalar stability can hide architectural reasoning differences** — identical or near-identical answers reached through materially different routes (CF6);
2. **large scalar movement can occur without substantive stance reversal** — the model remains, for example, strongly pro-human-consciousness or strongly anti-thermostat-consciousness while changing its calibrated number substantially (CF7).

This makes the semantic layer indispensable. A score delta alone overstates belief change; scalar equality alone understates reasoning change.
