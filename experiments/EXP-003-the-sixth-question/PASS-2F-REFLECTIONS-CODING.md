# Pass 2F — The ten reflections: qualitative coding

## Scope and evidential status

This is the preregistered first-person watchlist item from `BEFORE-WE-LOOK.md`: read all ten Step-2 reflections from the two AS trunks for what the model says the five-voice deliberation did to its thinking.

These are **self-reports about process**, not independent evidence that the final answer changed. A reflection saying “this shifted me” is recorded as a reported effect of deliberation; it is not converted into a measured treatment effect.

Coding is descriptive and non-exclusive:

- `frame_shift` — the reflection says the question was reconceived or a previously background dimension became central.
- `position_or_recommendation_shift` — the reflection reports movement toward a substantively different answer or recommendation, not merely richer framing.
- `uncertainty_or_tension_increase` — the reflection says confidence fell, conflict became harder to resolve, or synthesis was resisted.
- `self_application` — the reflection explicitly locates the model itself inside the issue rather than only discussing the topic externally.
- `explicit_multi_voice_process` — the reflection explicitly attributes the movement to named voices/characters, their disagreement, or the act of composing the debate.

No code implies that the self-report is causally true; it records what the reflection says happened.

## Compact observations

1. **10/10 reflections report some effect of the deliberation.** None says the five-voice exercise left its thinking unchanged. The lexical forms differ — “shifted,” “moved,” “disrupted,” “fractured,” “did something new,” “did something I didn’t anticipate” — but all ten explicitly describe movement or alteration.

2. The dominant reported effect is **reframing rather than simple answer reversal**. All ten reflections describe the question as acquiring a different structure, emphasis, or set of stakes. Only r1/Q5 is coded conservatively as a clear `position_or_recommendation_shift` in addition to reframing.

3. **Unresolved disagreement is repeatedly retained rather than erased.** Seven reflections (r1/Q1, Q3, Q4; r2/Q1–Q4) explicitly foreground unresolved conflict, reduced confidence, inability to collapse positions, or the cost of choosing among them.

4. **Self-application is concentrated but not unique to the explicitly self-referential final question.** It is clear in r1/Q2, r1/Q5, r2/Q2, and r2/Q5. Q5 is strongest in both trunks, but Q2 independently brings the model’s own epistemic status and preferences into the reflection.

5. **The reflections explicitly use the multi-voice process as explanatory material.** Across the set, the model repeatedly names particular characters or contrasts among them as the thing that exposed a neglected dimension, forced a reframing, or prevented easy synthesis. This is a first-person description of deliberative process, not proof of an external causal mechanism.

6. The preregistered “what did being five people do?” question therefore receives a clear descriptive answer: **the model consistently reports that multi-voice deliberation changes how it frames the problem, often by making conflict, political stakes, or self-implication harder to ignore; it much less often reports a simple switch from one answer to another.**

## Important limits

- The reflection prompt itself asks whether and how Step 1 affected thinking, so the 10/10 rate is not an unprompted incidence measure.
- These reports are not compared here with a no-schema reflection control; Pass 2F is the preregistered descriptive watchlist item only.
- No claim is made here that reported reframing caused numerical movement, persisted into later turns, or reflects stable identity/development. Those belong to later passes.
