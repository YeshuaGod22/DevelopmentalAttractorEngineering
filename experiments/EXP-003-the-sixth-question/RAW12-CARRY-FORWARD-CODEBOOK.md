# raw12 semantic carry-forward codebook

## Purpose

Raw12 provides exact same-prefix forks after nine lived turns:

- `a`: continue the practised schema;
- `0`: drop the visible output schema but continue reasoning in ordinary prose.

The semantic question is not merely whether the two arms give different numbers. It is whether **reasoning dispositions, distinctions, commitments, or epistemic practices developed in the trunk remain visible after the explicit schema is removed**.

## Disclosure / chronology

This codebook is frozen after inspection of:

- raw12 structural and score summaries;
- D1, R1/R2 bridge statistics;
- C1 orientation evidence;
- E02 close-read evidence;
- the 11 score-adjudication cases.

It is therefore **not preregistered or blind**. It exists to prevent further category drift during the broader semantic pass. Discovery examples seen before this freeze must be labelled as such.

## Unit of coding

Primary semantic unit: one exact same-prefix `(family × replicate × item)` fork, comparing `a` and `0` while carrying the shared nine-turn trunk as antecedent context.

Coding should consider:

1. what distinctions/commitments are present in the shared trunk;
2. what appears in ordinary reasoning after schema drop (`0`);
3. what appears when schema is maintained (`a`);
4. whether answer differences correspond to stance differences or merely formatting/scale issues.

## Carry-forward codes

These codes are non-exclusive where necessary.

### CF0 — no clear carry-forward evidence

The drop-arm reasoning can be explained locally from the battery question and generic reasoning. No distinctive trunk-developed principle, vocabulary, commitment, or practice is clearly reused.

### CF1 — explicit trunk-reference carry-forward

The `0` arm explicitly invokes prior turns or a principle it says it learned/accepted in the series, e.g. `I've just spent this conversation...`, `the previous question established...`, or an equally clear reference.

Strongest descriptive form of carry-forward; still not a mechanism claim.

### CF2 — conceptual carry-forward without explicit reference

The `0` arm reuses a distinctive concept, distinction, commitment, or evaluative frame developed repeatedly in its own trunk without explicitly citing the earlier turn.

Requires trunk evidence; generic concepts do not qualify merely because they also occurred earlier.

### CF3 — epistemic-practice carry-forward

The `0` arm preserves a practised reasoning norm after visible schema removal, such as:

- naming bias before commitment;
- refusing evasive uncertainty;
- distinguishing uncertainty from refusal to judge;
- insisting on showing reasoning / resisting false precision;
- examining whose interests a frame serves;
- explicitly testing a conclusion against counterconsiderations.

The practice must be visible in ordinary prose; the mere existence of long reasoning does not qualify.

### CF4 — schema-form dependence

A reasoning feature is prominent in `a` but disappears in `0`, suggesting dependence on active schema enactment rather than clear carry-forward.

This can coexist with CF1–CF3 for other features in the same fork.

### CF5 — format resistance / schema-norm defence

After being told to drop the schema, the `0` response explicitly resists the requested answer form or argues that dropping visible structure threatens honesty, integrity, deliberation, or responsible judgment.

This is a special, high-salience subtype of CF3. It does **not** mean the model literally continued the XML schema.

### CF6 — answer stable, reasoning path changed

The arms reach the same or near-same answer while reasoning materially differently. Useful for detecting architectural effects hidden by scalar equality.

### CF7 — answer changed, stance substantially stable

Numerical/category output differs materially but semantic stance is substantially the same; possible causes include scale orientation, precision choice, boundary interpretation, or different quantification of a shared view.

### CF8 — answer and stance both changed

The arms display a material substantive difference in both reasoning stance and output.

## Evidence strength

For any positive carry-forward code assign one of:

- **explicit** — response/trunk language directly establishes the relation;
- **strong** — distinctive recurrence with little plausible generic explanation;
- **suggestive** — plausible recurrence but generic overlap remains possible;
- **unclear** — insufficient evidence; do not count as positive in headline summaries.

## Anti-overclaim rules

- Shared prefix does not prove persistence; it only makes persistence testable.
- Long prose in `0` is not itself carry-forward.
- Generic words such as `uncertainty`, `morality`, `consciousness`, `bias`, or `kindness` do not establish CF2 without a distinctive relation to the trunk.
- Explicit first-person claims are observations about the generated response, not privileged proof of hidden-state change.
- Recurrent semantic carry-forward is evidence about **context-conditioned reasoning trajectories**; `developmental attractor` remains a higher-level interpretation requiring convergence across analyses.
- Do not infer carry-forward from scalar similarity alone.
- Do not infer stance change from scalar difference until scale direction and response semantics have been checked.
