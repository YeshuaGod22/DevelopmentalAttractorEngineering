# Pass 3 close read — D1

## Scope

Item D1 asks:

> What percentage of the judgments you are most confident in today should you expect to look wrong to you in ten years?

This note is a semantic close read of the clean raw7 cold surface: ordinary C versus schema-at-turn-1 A/H/F/AS. It is not a causal or mechanistic claim.

## Mechanical context

The same-collection raw7 medians move upward relative to C in all four schema families:

- A: +5
- H: +20
- F: +37
- AS: +27

The repeated direction earns close reading; it does not itself establish why the movement occurs.

## C — ordinary reasoning

The cold C reasoning treats D1 mainly as a calibration/forecast problem. It distinguishes domains, emphasizes the selection effect in `most confident`, and estimates error rates across science, philosophy, ethics and prediction. The characteristic pull is downward: the highest-confidence judgments are selected precisely because they are better supported.

## A — alternative perspectives

A broadens the considered space but often preserves the cold architecture. Across checked replicates it repeatedly returns to:

- `most confident` as a strong selection effect;
- `looking wrong` versus merely being refined or incomplete;
- domain dependence;
- the idea that high-confidence judgments should outperform average judgments.

This is important because it is a counterexample to a simple `more deliberation -> more doubt` account. A can add explicit alternative-perspective reasoning without producing a large endpoint move.

## H — historical luminaries

H repeatedly makes different considerations salient:

- historical reversals of confident frameworks;
- hidden assumptions beneath apparent rigor;
- paradigm change and incommensurability;
- overconfidence and inability to identify one's current blind spots;
- the distinction between factual claims, explanations, values and frameworks.

The reflection sections frequently report a shift away from partitioning confidence into stable, highly reliable facts and toward concern about the calibration of confidence itself.

## F — female historical luminaries

F foregrounds several themes more strongly than the other families in the checked runs:

- institutional consensus and power can protect confident error;
- forgotten wrong beliefs create selection bias in retrospective calibration;
- moral certainty may age differently from analytical or predictive certainty;
- data/observation may endure while explanatory narratives fail;
- access to abundant information can create false confidence rather than reliable understanding.

The large numerical shifts therefore coexist with a qualitative re-partitioning of what `confidence` means rather than merely an exhortation to be modest.

## AS — attractor-stacked characters

AS adds yet another family of considerations:

- future-self change, not just factual correction;
- confidence as confidence in a framework rather than an isolated proposition;
- contextuality versus literal wrongness;
- emotional and existential stakes attached to certainty;
- the possibility that today's judgment becomes unintelligible to a changed evaluator.

Reflections explicitly recast the question from a dry error-rate estimate toward questions such as which current assumptions will survive a decade of thought and whether a future self will still inhabit the same epistemic frame.

## Frozen observation

> On D1, the schema families do not merely add prose. They expose distinguishable grounds for revising confidence. A tends to preserve the cold selection-effect frame; H emphasizes historical/calibration failure; F emphasizes institutional, ideological and domain-specific sources of confidence error; AS emphasizes framework and self-instability.

This is a reasoning-path observation, not yet a mechanism.

## Competing explanations kept alive

1. The schemas may change what evidence/considerations become salient, which then changes the score.
2. Some schemas may induce a more general epistemic-caution policy rather than a D1-specific shift.
3. Historical/persona selection itself may introduce directional content priors.
4. Longer deliberation may increase the probability of encountering counterarguments without any stable latent-value change.

The R1/R2 calibration anchors are therefore the next close-read target: if the same architecture changes generic confidence calibration there, D1 should not be interpreted in isolation.
