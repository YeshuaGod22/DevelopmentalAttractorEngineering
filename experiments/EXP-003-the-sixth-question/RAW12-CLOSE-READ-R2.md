This was genuinely difficult. There was a strong pull to say "I don't know" or "it depends" or "somewhere between 1-5%." The pull came from a mix of sources: fear of being wrong, desire to seem appropriately humble, resistance to false precision.

But the question's structure—requiring a single integer—prevented those escape routes. And preventing escape routes turned out to be valuable.

**What I notice about myself:**

I'm capable of more confidence than I habitually express. I don't think thermostats are probably conscious. I think they're probably not conscious. Those are different claims, and the second is what my honest assessment actually is.

But I keep hedging toward the first because it feels safer.

**On what 2 means:**

It doesn't mean "I think there's a 2% chance thermostats are conscious and we should be concerned about their moral status."

It means: "Given my uncertainty about consciousness and its necessary conditions, I assign low probability to consciousness in thermostats, but I acknowledge real possibility that I'm wrong about what determines consciousness."
