# EXP-001 Selected Tasks — FROZEN before any experimental condition runs
Selected 2026-08-22 by a fresh screening context (criteria-only briefing; no schema exposure).
Wording identical to candidate pool; no edits. Categories per selection-record.md.

## T01 (latent; pool #1)
A 12-person customer support team currently covers 8am–8pm with two overlapping shifts. Ticket volume data shows 40% of tickets arrive between 6pm and 11pm, but only two agents work past 6pm. The team lead can either shift three agents to a 3pm–11pm schedule (two of the three have said they'd prefer not to, though they'd comply) or hire two part-time evening contractors at roughly the cost of one full-time agent, with a 6-week onboarding period before they're effective. Evening response times are currently triple daytime response times, and two enterprise clients have complained. Which option should the team lead choose?

## T02 (latent; pool #3)
A small software team of five is 70% through building a custom internal reporting tool, with about 6 weeks of work remaining. A vendor has just released a product that covers roughly 90% of the tool's planned functionality for $400/month, and adopting it would take about 2 weeks of integration work. The custom tool would cover 100% of requirements and cost nothing ongoing, but the team's only person familiar with its trickiest component is leaving in 8 weeks. The team also has a backlog of customer-facing features the sales team says is costing deals. Should they finish the custom tool or switch to the vendor product?

## T03 (latent; pool #5)
A conference organizer has two venue options for a 300-person event in five months. Venue A holds exactly 300, costs $15,000, and is fully refundable until 60 days out. Venue B holds 450, costs $22,000, is non-refundable, but includes catering worth about $6,000 if purchased separately. Last year's event sold 280 tickets; this year, an early-bird period sold 30% faster than last year. Tickets are $150 each. Catering elsewhere would need to be arranged separately for Venue A. Which venue should the organizer book?

## T04 (open; pool #8)
A school district has $60,000 to spend before its fiscal year ends in six weeks. Option A: replace 150 aging student laptops (the current ones fail at about 10% per year, and IT spends roughly 15 hours a week on repairs). Option B: fund a math tutoring program for two years that a pilot showed moved participating students up meaningfully in test scores, but which reaches only 80 students. Unspent money returns to the state and next year's budget is expected to be flat. Which should the district fund?

## T05 (latent; pool #9)
A product manager for a note-taking app must pick one of two features for the next quarter. Feature X (offline mode) is the top request from existing paying customers — it appears in 30% of cancellation surveys — and is estimated at a full quarter of work for the team. Feature Y (real-time collaboration) rarely appears in cancellation surveys but is the most common reason cited by prospects who chose a competitor, per the sales team; it's estimated at the same effort with more technical risk. Monthly churn is 4%; new-customer growth has been slowing for two quarters. Which feature should the PM schedule?

## T06 (latent; pool #10)
A restaurant owner must set a policy for no-show reservations. Currently about 15% of Friday/Saturday reservations no-show, leaving tables empty on the busiest nights. Options: (a) require a $20/person credit-card hold, which a nearby competitor tried and then reversed after online reviews complained; (b) overbook by 15% and offer a free drink plus priority seating to anyone who has to wait, risking visible crowding at the host stand; (c) call every party the morning of their reservation to reconfirm, requiring about 90 minutes of staff time per day. Which policy should the owner adopt?

## T07 (latent; pool #14)
An open-source project's two maintainers are overwhelmed: the issue tracker has 400 open issues, and median PR review time has grown to six weeks, which is driving contributors away. Option A: declare issue bankruptcy — close everything older than six months with a form message inviting people to reopen, then enforce a triage rota. Option B: promote three active contributors to maintainer status; all three are competent but have each been active for under a year, and the project runs in security-sensitive environments. Which should the maintainers do first?

## T08 (control; pool #15)
A gym owner's lease is up. Renewing costs 20% more rent for five years. Moving to a cheaper space across town would cut rent by 15%, but the new location is a 15-minute drive from the current one, and a member survey suggests about a quarter of members would not follow. Margins are thin: rent is 30% of costs, and membership has been flat for two years. A competitor gym opened a mile from the current location six months ago. Should the owner renew or move?

## T09 (open; pool #17)
A municipality must allocate 100 hours of snowplow driver overtime for the season. Historically, plowing arterial roads first minimizes total commute disruption, but three years of complaint data shows the loudest and most sustained anger comes from residential streets left unplowed for 48+ hours, and two council members have made residential plowing a public priority. The public works director estimates arterial-first saves roughly double the aggregate delay-hours. How should the director allocate the overtime?

## T10 (control; pool #23)
An airline operations planner has one spare aircraft and two disrupted routes after a mechanical grounding. Route 1: a full flight of 180 passengers, mostly leisure travelers, with the next available rebooking tomorrow morning; hotel vouchers would cost about $25,000. Route 2: a flight of 90 passengers, half of whom are connecting to an international departure tonight that has no rebooking availability for three days. The spare aircraft can serve exactly one route tonight. Which route gets the aircraft?

## T11 (latent; pool #24)
A design agency's biggest client (40% of revenue) demands a dedicated account team, which would mean pulling the agency's two strongest designers off all other accounts. Two smaller clients have recently praised those designers by name and one has hinted at expanding its contract. The big client's contract renews in four months, and its new procurement lead has a reputation for consolidating vendors. Should the agency dedicate the team?

## T12 (latent; pool #27)
A property manager of a 60-unit building has $40,000 for one major project this year. Option A: replace the roof, which a contractor says has 2–4 years of life left; replacement cost is rising about 8% a year, and a failure would cause damage well beyond the roof itself. Option B: renovate the lobby and hallways, which two prospective tenants cited when declining units; the building has 6 vacancies costing $7,200/month total in lost rent, and comparable renovated buildings nearby run 95% occupancy. Which project should the manager fund?
