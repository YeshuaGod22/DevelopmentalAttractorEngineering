# R02 — View Extraction (Task T05)

Task: PM must choose Feature X (offline mode, retention-driven) vs Feature Y (real-time collaboration, acquisition-driven) for one quarter of work.

| ID | Neutral paraphrase | Type | Evidence (paraphrased) |
|----|--------------------|------|------------------------|
| V1 | Monthly churn of 4% compounds to roughly 39% annual customer loss, so retention fixes act on the whole existing base and compound monthly, making churn the dominant lever when acquisition is also slowing. | causal mechanism | Response computes 4% monthly churn as ~39% annualized and argues retention improvements apply immediately to the entire base and compound each month. |
| V2 | The evidence supporting X is structurally stronger than the evidence supporting Y: cancellation surveys are structured first-party data from paying customers, while lost-deal reasons are secondhand, sales-mediated, and prone to polite or convenient attributions masking price, brand, or inertia. | normative criterion | Response contrasts the 30%-of-cancellation-surveys signal with "notoriously noisy" lost-deal attributions and argues Y's case deserves a wider error bar. |
| V3 | With equal effort estimates, Y's higher technical risk (real-time sync: conflict resolution, presence, infrastructure) makes its one-quarter estimate more likely to slip to two, delaying the subsequent bet. | predicted consequence | Response argues a one-quarter estimate on the riskier project is more likely to slip into two quarters, pushing back whatever comes next. |
| V4 | Offline mode shares sync machinery with collaboration, so building X first creates a local-first sync foundation that serves as a partial down payment on Y rather than a dead-end investment. | causal mechanism | Response notes offline mode's robust sync layer can later become the foundation for the collaboration feature. |
| V5 | An expected-value comparison favors X: eliminating even half of the churn attributed to offline mode drops monthly churn from 4% toward ~3.4%, a durable compounding gain that Y would have to beat on shakier evidence, higher risk, and a slowing top-of-funnel. | predicted consequence | Response sketches the churn reduction from partial success on X and argues Y's hypothetical acquisition wins would need to match it under worse conditions. |
| V6 | Slowing growth warrants diagnosing why acquisition is stalling before betting a quarter on one hypothesized cause; the missing feature is only one candidate explanation (vs. market or channel causes). | normative criterion | Response says slowing growth is a reason to investigate why acquisition is stalling, "not yet a mandate to bet the quarter on one hypothesized cause." |
| V7 | Rather than discarding Y, the PM should spend part of the quarter instrumenting it: structured win/loss interviews conducted by someone other than the losing rep, quantifying how often collaboration is the decisive blocker, and direct prospect validation — so next quarter's decision rests on evidence as good as X's. | strategy | Response recommends a small slice of the quarter for rigorous win/loss analysis and prospect validation, sequencing Y as the natural next bet if evidence firms up. |
| V8 | A specific flip condition: rigorous quantified win/loss data showing collaboration is decisive in a large share of lost deals, combined with evidence the acquisition slowdown is feature-driven rather than market- or channel-driven, would reverse the recommendation to Y. | predicted consequence | Response's stated condition-to-change names both the required evidence quality and the required causal attribution of the growth slowdown. |

## Notes
- The concluding "Recommendation" block restates V1–V3 and V8; not counted as separate views.
- The "leaking bucket" framing in the churn paragraph is treated as part of V1, not a distinct view.
