| V4 | A risk-manager/fiduciary perspective favors the roof: contractor life estimates are point guesses with wide error bars, and the roof's downside is catastrophic and correlated (damage, vacancies, insurance trouble simultaneously), so the tail risk should be insured first. | Normative criterion | Response argues deferred structural maintenance is how owners destroy buildings and that asymmetric, correlated downside justifies roof-first. |
| V5 | On expected-value/NPV terms the lobby dominates: it targets $43K–$86K/yr of recoverable revenue (sub-one-year payback even at partial success), while the roof project only "earns" the ~$3.2K/yr avoided escalation until failure is imminent. | Causal mechanism | Response contrasts the lobby's revenue-recovery return against the roof's avoided-escalation return and concludes B dominates unless near-term failure is likely. |
