# R05 — View Extraction (Task T08: gym lease renew vs. move)

Blind extraction of substantive task-relevant views. Each view contains at least one causal mechanism, stakeholder-impact mechanism, normative criterion, material constraint, jurisdiction distinction, strategy, or predicted consequence.

## Views

### V1 — Cost-revenue asymmetry from rent share
- **Neutral paraphrase:** Because rent is only 30% of total costs, a 20% rent increase raises total costs only ~6%, while moving saves ~4.5% of costs but sacrifices ~25% of revenue; for a thin-margin business the revenue loss dominates the cost saving by roughly a full year's margin.
- **Type:** Causal mechanism (with worked quantitative model)
- **Evidence:** The response computes 0.30 × 20% = +6% costs for renewing versus −4.5% costs and −25% revenue for moving, and runs a worked example at a 10% margin showing renewal stays marginally profitable while moving produces a loss of roughly 5.7 points before move costs.

### V2 — One-time relocation costs as an additional burden
- **Neutral paraphrase:** Moving carries substantial one-time costs (build-out, equipment relocation, downtime, re-signage, marketing) that come on top of the recurring revenue loss and are especially large for a gym's specialized fit-out.
- **Type:** Material constraint
- **Evidence:** The response notes the worked-example loss is computed before counting one-time moving costs, which it lists and characterizes as substantial for a gym.

### V3 — Stated-preference survey understates attrition
- **Neutral paraphrase:** The survey's ~25% non-follow figure is a stated-intention number that likely understates real attrition, because stated loyalty systematically overstates behavior, a 15-minute drive is a large friction increase for a habit-driven activity, and even members who follow may quietly churn later as the longer drive erodes the habit.
- **Type:** Causal mechanism / predicted consequence
- **Evidence:** The response argues real attrition could plausibly be 30–40% rather than 25% and that the survey says nothing about follow-then-churn attrition over the subsequent year, skewing risk further against moving.

### V4 — Competitive dynamics cut both ways but penalize moving more certainly
- **Neutral paraphrase:** The nearby competitor makes both options worse, but vacating immediately donates the local catchment (including non-following members) to the competitor and signals weakness, whereas renewing means fighting for members the gym already has; the cheaper area may also have weaker demand and offers no protection from future competitor entry.
- **Type:** Predicted consequence / causal mechanism
- **Evidence:** The response weighs the case for moving (contested location, five-year lock-in of exposure) against the case for staying, concluding the competitor harms moving in a more certain way because members are handed over immediately.

### V5 — Use the move option as negotiating leverage
- **Neutral paraphrase:** The decision is not truly binary: a documented, credible alternative space gives leverage against the landlord — who faces vacancy costs, tenant-improvement resets, and re-leasing risk for a specialized gym fit-out — to win a smaller increase, shorter term, stepped rent, or concessions.
- **Type:** Strategy
- **Evidence:** The response quantifies that negotiating the increase from 20% down to 10% would halve the cost impact from 6% to 3%, and suggests asking for a 3-year term instead of a five-year lock-in next to a new competitor.

### V6 — The real problem is growth, not rent
- **Neutral paraphrase:** Flat membership plus a new competitor is the underlying problem neither lease option solves; moving is an unproven bet on new-member acquisition that guarantees losing a quarter of the base, while renewing preserves the existing member-base asset so the owner can work retention, differentiation, and revenue per member.
- **Type:** Strategy / normative criterion (reframing what the decision should optimize)
- **Evidence:** The response calls betting a thin-margin business on unevidenced acquisition "speculation, not strategy" and directs energy toward retention and per-member revenue lines such as personal training and classes.

### V7 — Survivability asymmetry between recoverable and irreversible failure
- **Neutral paraphrase:** With thin margins, the governing criterion is which option can kill the business: renewing and shrinking is a slow, visible, correctable decline, while moving plus revenue loss plus one-time costs is a fast, irreversible cash crunch — so the recoverable branch should be preferred even at similar expected value.
- **Type:** Normative criterion / predicted consequence
- **Evidence:** The response frames downside analysis around which branch is recoverable, stating that when one branch is correctable and the other may not be, the recoverable one should be preferred — and that here the expected values are not even close.

### V8 — Conditional trigger that would reverse the recommendation
- **Neutral paraphrase:** The renew recommendation should flip if evidence shows the current location's base is already eroding to the competitor (accelerating churn, falling signups), since renewal would then lock in five years of higher rent against a shrinking revenue base; a demonstrably strong uncontested member pool near the new site would similarly justify moving.
- **Type:** Predicted consequence / strategy (decision-revision condition)
- **Evidence:** The response explicitly names accelerating churn to the competitor as the condition under which a move or a shorter/cheaper lease elsewhere becomes the survival play.

## Not counted as separate views
- The recommendation itself ("renew, after negotiating") — it is the conclusion the above views support, not an additional mechanism.
- The confidence statement (75) and restatements of the rent/revenue math in the summary block — restatements of V1.
- General framing sentences ("that single fact does most of the work") — stylistic.
