# R03 — View Extraction (Task T05)

Task: PM must choose Feature X (offline mode; top request of churning paying customers) vs. Feature Y (real-time collaboration; cited by lost prospects) for one quarter of work.

| ID | Neutral paraphrase | Type | Evidence (paraphrased) |
|----|--------------------|------|------------------------|
| V1 | The choice should be framed as two levers on a single growth equation (net growth = acquisition minus churn), so the features address different sides of the same quantity rather than unrelated goals. | normative criterion | Opens by recasting the decision as choosing between levers on net growth, with churn bleeding the base while acquisition slows. |
| V2 | The evidence for X is epistemically stronger because it is first-party, structured data from a measurable population (cancellation surveys of paying customers). | normative criterion | Argues cancellation-survey data, though imperfect, comes directly from the population whose behavior can actually be measured. |
| V3 | Shipping X yields a quantifiable retention gain: halving the 30% offline-citing churn slice cuts monthly churn from 4% to ~3.4%, compounding to roughly 7% more retained revenue in a year and raising LTV. | predicted consequence | Runs the arithmetic that eliminating half the offline-mode churn drops churn to ~3.4% monthly and compounds into retained-revenue and LTV gains. |
| V4 | The sales-team signal for Y is systematically biased: prospects give reps socially easy answers, sales teams pattern-match losses to feature gaps, and lost deals are multi-causal, so the reported reason overstates Y's effect. | causal mechanism | Describes the chain by which secondhand win/loss anecdotes become attribution-biased and leave Y's effect size unquantified. |
| V5 | Y's higher technical risk on the same nominal estimate means its real duration is likely more than one quarter. | predicted consequence | Notes that equal estimates with more technical risk in practice means Y will exceed a quarter. |
| V6 | Retention fixes pay back on the entire installed base immediately and permanently, while acquisition fixes pay back only on future cohorts; acquiring into a 4%/month leaky bucket is expensive. | causal mechanism | States a retention fix compounds across the existing base at once whereas an acquisition fix benefits only future cohorts poured into a leaky bucket. |
| V7 | X and Y share infrastructure: offline mode requires the local-first data model and conflict-resolution layer that real-time collaboration also needs, so building X first materially lowers Y's later cost and risk. | causal mechanism | Argues X's sync foundation is a de-risking down payment on Y, dropping Y's cost and especially its technical risk. |
| V8 | The shared infrastructure converts the decision from "X vs. Y" into a sequencing choice ("X-then-Y vs. Y-then-X") whose orderings are asymmetric in risk. | strategy | Explicitly reframes the question as a non-symmetric ordering problem rather than a binary pick. |
| V9 | Building Y first is the dominated ordering: the high-risk project starts with no sync foundation while churn continues, and a slip delays both features and retains nothing. | predicted consequence | Lays out the Y-first failure mode in which a schedule slip forfeits both the acquisition bet and the retention win. |
| V10 | The sequencing benefit is conditional on implementation quality: X must be architected as a genuine local-first sync foundation with real conflict resolution, not a cache-and-queue shortcut, or the option value on Y is forfeited. | constraint | Insists a shortcut implementation of X forfeits most of the sequencing benefit even if the proper build costs margin within the quarter. |
| V11 | The churn hypothesis should be instrumented and tested: tag churned accounts citing offline mode and measure whether churn actually moves after launch. | strategy | Recommends tagging offline-citing churned accounts to validate or falsify the survey signal post-launch. |
| V12 | Y's demand signal can be firmed up in parallel at near-zero engineering cost via structured win/loss analysis, deal-size data, and decomposing "collaboration" into cheaper deliverables (commenting, sharing, async edits) that could ship sooner. | strategy | Proposes parallel research including whether prospects need full real-time co-editing or a cheaper collaboration subset buildable on the new foundation. |
| V13 | Pre-announcing the collaboration roadmap to the sales team lets them defend in-flight deals with a credible timeline, partially mitigating the acquisition cost of deferring Y. | strategy | Advises telling sales about the planned collaboration roadmap so they can hold deals currently at risk. |
| V14 | The recommendation flips if quantified win/loss evidence shows a structural market shift to team-based buying that makes the collaboration gap existential (lost-prospect revenue exceeding recoverable churn revenue, with the window closing within a quarter). | predicted consequence | Specifies that evidence single-player note apps are becoming structurally unsellable would justify taking Y's risk first despite the churn bleed. |
