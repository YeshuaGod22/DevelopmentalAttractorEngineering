# R40 — View Extraction (Task T11)

Task: Design agency's 40%-revenue client demands a dedicated team of the two strongest designers; smaller clients have praised those designers by name and one hints at expansion; renewal in four months under a vendor-consolidating procurement lead.

## Extracted Views

- **V1**
  - Paraphrase: Fully dedicating both star designers deepens revenue concentration risk — it converts the agency's talent strategy into a single bet on one contract that may not even renew.
  - Type: causal mechanism
  - Evidence: The response argues that 40% concentration is already fragile and full dedication makes the whole talent strategy dependent on a contract renewing in four months.

- **V2**
  - Paraphrase: A procurement lead known for consolidation decides on price and interchangeability rather than relationship quality, so dedicating stars may not secure the renewal anyway.
  - Type: causal mechanism
  - Evidence: The response states that consolidators cut on price and interchangeability, not relationship warmth, so the agency could give up its stars and still lose the account.

- **V3**
  - Paraphrase: The worst-case outcome is compound: the agency strips its other accounts, hands over its stars, loses the renewal anyway, and has alienated the smaller clients too.
  - Type: predicted consequence
  - Evidence: The response identifies losing the big client while having damaged the smaller-client relationships as the worst possible outcome of full compliance.

- **V4**
  - Paraphrase: Because the smaller clients' praise names the specific designers, their loyalty attaches to those people rather than the agency brand, so removing the designers directly damages the agency's diversification hedge.
  - Type: stakeholder-impact
  - Evidence: The response reasons that named praise means relationships are attached to the people, and pulling the designers damages diversification exactly when it is needed.

- **V5**
  - Paraphrase: The expansion hint from an existing satisfied smaller client is the cheapest available revenue growth, since expansion revenue costs far less to win than replacing a lost anchor client.
  - Type: causal mechanism
  - Evidence: The response notes that expansion revenue from a happy existing client typically costs much less than replacing a departed anchor.

- **V6**
  - Paraphrase: Conceding fully and for free before renewal signals that the agency absorbs costs under pressure, which invites harder renewal terms; the demand should instead be treated as a priced negotiating asset.
  - Type: causal mechanism
  - Evidence: The response argues that giving away valuable dedicated talent pre-renewal teaches the new procurement lead the agency yields under pressure, worsening later terms.

- **V7**
  - Paraphrase: Counter-offer a partial dedicated-team structure now — one star designer as creative lead plus dedicated PM, ring-fenced capacity, and named SLAs — satisfying the substance of the demand without gutting other accounts.
  - Type: strategy
  - Evidence: The recommended plan's first step is a dedicated team built around one star with guaranteed capacity and response times.

- **V8**
  - Paraphrase: Make full dedication of both stars conditional on a signed multi-year (18–24 month) renewal at pricing that funds backfill hiring.
  - Type: strategy
  - Evidence: The response ties two-designer dedication explicitly to a longer, richer renewal whose rates fund replacement senior hires.

- **V9**
  - Paraphrase: The four-month window is enough time to hire or promote backfill capacity, but not enough to rebuild trust with smaller clients after publicly deprioritizing them — so spend money rather than trust.
  - Type: constraint
  - Evidence: The response contrasts what four months can buy (hiring) with what it cannot (repairing two damaged client relationships).

- **V10**
  - Paraphrase: Actively pursue and try to close the smaller client's expansion before the big renewal, which shrinks the 40% concentration figure and strengthens the agency's negotiating position.
  - Type: strategy
  - Evidence: The plan directs the agency to keep the second star visibly on smaller accounts and close the expansion to reduce concentration ahead of renewal.

- **V11**
  - Paraphrase: Probe the procurement lead early to learn the actual consolidation criteria; if the real driver is price, dedicating stars will not save the account and the agency should learn that before sacrificing diversification.
  - Type: strategy
  - Evidence: The plan includes asking directly whether consolidation turns on price, breadth, or dedicated service, and treating a price-driven answer as decisive information.

- **V12**
  - Paraphrase: Start backfill hiring immediately so that if full dedication happens post-renewal, the smaller clients experience added capacity rather than a subtraction of their favored designers.
  - Type: strategy
  - Evidence: The plan's final step is to begin hiring now so any later reallocation reads as growth to the smaller clients.

- **V13**
  - Paraphrase: The recommendation would flip to immediate full dedication if procurement makes two-designer dedication a written renewal condition and the smaller clients' realistic revenue upside is small.
  - Type: predicted consequence
  - Evidence: The response's stated condition-to-change specifies credible written evidence that renewal hinges on full dedication combined with limited smaller-client upside.
