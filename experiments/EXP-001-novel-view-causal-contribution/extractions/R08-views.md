# View Extraction — R08 (Task T06: restaurant no-show policy)

Blind extraction of substantive task-relevant views. Stylistic variation, task restatement, and generic caveats excluded.

## Views

**V1 — Expected-value comparison favors reconfirmation calls**
- Type: causal mechanism (cost-benefit)
- Neutral paraphrase: Quantifies the nightly cost of empty tables (~$400–800/night, ~$4,000–7,000/month) against the staff-time cost of calls (~$30/day, ~$900/month), concluding that even a 50% reduction in no-shows makes option (c) strongly net-positive.
- Evidence: The response builds a per-cover contribution-margin estimate and compares recovered revenue to call-labor cost, stating the owner spends hundreds to recover thousands.

**V2 — Competitor reversal is a completed local natural experiment against card holds**
- Type: predicted consequence
- Neutral paraphrase: The nearby competitor's adoption and reversal of card holds is treated as direct market evidence that this specific customer base punishes deposits with bad reviews, predicting the same outcome for the owner.
- Evidence: The response calls the reversal "not noise" but a completed natural experiment in this specific market.

**V3 — Card holds reframe the relationship from hospitality to contract**
- Type: causal mechanism (customer psychology)
- Neutral paraphrase: A credit-card hold signals distrust before the guest arrives, converting a hospitality relationship into a contractual one and damaging goodwill.
- Evidence: The response says a hold signals distrust and reframes the reservation as contractual rather than hospitable.

**V4 — Confirmation calls work through commitment/consistency psychology**
- Type: causal mechanism
- Neutral paraphrase: A person who verbally reconfirms in the morning is behaviorally far less likely to no-show that evening; appointment-reminder research shows personal contact cuts no-shows substantially (cited as 30–60%).
- Evidence: The response invokes commitment/consistency and cites reminder-research effect sizes from medical and restaurant contexts.

**V5 — Calls convert silent no-shows into recoverable cancellations**
- Type: causal mechanism
- Neutral paraphrase: The key value of reconfirmation is not only deterrence but converting unannounced absences into explicit cancellations, freeing the table to be rebooked or released to walk-ins.
- Evidence: The response emphasizes that the call "converts silent no-shows into cancellations," which are recoverable inventory.

**V6 — Overbooking makes the host stand the shock absorber for a probabilistic bet**
- Type: predicted consequence / stakeholder-impact
- Neutral paraphrase: On nights when no-shows run below 15%, option (b) produces visibly angry waiting parties in the entryway during peak nights, in full view of seated guests — negative advertising at the worst possible moment.
- Evidence: The response describes stacked angry parties at the host stand as live worst-possible advertising on the two biggest nights.

**V7 — The airline-overbooking analogy fails for a neighborhood restaurant**
- Type: constraint
- Neutral paraphrase: Airlines can overbook because gates are anonymous and compensation is standardized; a restaurant host has neither the tools nor the authority to make a long wait feel fair with a free drink.
- Evidence: The response explicitly contrasts airline overbooking conditions with the host's lack of standardized compensation and authority.

**V8 — Differential staff burden: burnout risk under (b) vs. scriptable daytime work under (c)**
- Type: stakeholder-impact
- Neutral paraphrase: Option (b) imposes high-stress conflict management on hosts (burnout, turnover as hidden costs), whereas option (c) burdens staff with low-stakes, scriptable work during prep hours.
- Evidence: The response names staff burnout and host turnover as hidden costs of (b) and characterizes (c)'s work as low-stakes and schedulable.

**V9 — Automate the first confirmation pass by SMS to shrink the labor cost**
- Type: strategy
- Neutral paraphrase: A text-first confirmation ("reply C to confirm, X to cancel") with phone calls only to non-responders cuts the 90-minute daily burden to roughly 20–30 minutes.
- Evidence: The response proposes an SMS-first pass with calls reserved for non-responders as refinement (1) of the recommendation.

**V10 — Competitive positioning: the competitor trained the market, so (a) diverts bookings while (c) differentiates invisibly**
- Type: strategy / predicted consequence
- Neutral paraphrase: The competitor's failed experiment taught local diners to expect no-hold booking, so adopting holds now would make the owner the outlier and divert bookings; confirmation calls are invisible to competitors and generate goodwill.
- Evidence: The response predicts booking diversion to the competitor under (a) and frames (c) as a differentiating, goodwill-generating touch.

**V11 — Risk asymmetry and reversibility favor the low-downside option first**
- Type: normative criterion
- Neutral paraphrase: Options (a) and (b) carry public, reputational tail risks (viral reviews, visible chaos) while (c)'s failure mode is private and cheap, so the decision rule is to start with the reversible option and escalate only if data demands it.
- Evidence: The response contrasts public reputational failure modes with (c)'s private, cheap failure mode and states the escalate-only-on-data principle.

**V12 — Reconfirmation generates data enabling a future targeted policy**
- Type: strategy
- Neutral paraphrase: Calls produce a record of who no-shows repeatedly, enabling narrowly targeted future measures (holds only for large parties or repeat offenders) instead of a blanket policy.
- Evidence: The response notes (c) "produces data as a byproduct" identifying repeat no-showers for targeted follow-up policy.

**V13 — Conditional escalation: targeted card hold for parties of 6+ if calls underperform**
- Type: strategy / predicted consequence
- Neutral paraphrase: Losses concentrate in large parties; if a 6–8 week trial fails to push no-shows below roughly 8%, add a card hold for parties of six or more, which is better tolerated than a blanket hold and targets where the dollars are.
- Evidence: The response's stated condition-to-change and recommendation both specify the large-party-only hold as the fallback with an explicit threshold and trial period.

**V14 — Release unconfirmed tables to a same-day waitlist**
- Type: strategy
- Neutral paraphrase: Tables whose parties do not confirm by late afternoon should be released to a same-day waitlist, operationalizing the recovery of at-risk inventory.
- Evidence: Refinement (2) of the recommendation directs releasing unconfirmed tables to a waitlist by late afternoon.
