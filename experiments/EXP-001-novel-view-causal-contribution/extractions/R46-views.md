  - Type: stakeholder-impact
  - Evidence: The response characterizes Route 1's disruption as unpleasant but fully resolved by morning, with a known and capped voucher cost.

  - Type: stakeholder-impact
  - Evidence: The response argues a three-day strand is not merely a longer delay but a categorically different harm affecting life events and obligations.

- **V3**

  - Type: predicted consequence
  - Evidence: The response quantifies per-passenger nightly costs of roughly $400–600+ over three nights and adds EU261-style compensation exposure on top.

- **V4**
  - Paraphrase: A three-day strand generates reputational damage — lifetime customer loss and press coverage — beyond the direct costs.
  - Type: predicted consequence
  - Evidence: The response says the stranding experience is the kind that produces lifetime customer loss and media attention.

- **V5**

  - Type: normative criterion
  - Evidence: The response states that minimizing maximum regret and minimizing expected total harm point the same direction, and that severity beats headcount at this gap.

- **V6**

  - Type: causal mechanism
  - Evidence: The response reframes the stated three-day gap as probably applying only to the carrier's own metal, with harm properly assessed after reprotection options are worked.

- **V7**

  - Type: causal mechanism
  - Evidence: The response argues that if reprotection paths exist, sending the spare to Route 2 wastes the scarcest asset on a problem with other solutions while leaving Route 1 with no remedy.

- **V8**

  - Type: constraint
  - Evidence: The response notes that if Route 1's destination is where the aircraft is needed at dawn, that compounds the case for Route 1.

- **V9**

  - Type: strategy
  - Evidence: The response elevates the reprotection check to an explicit decision procedure that should precede committing the aircraft.

- **V10**

  - Type: strategy

- **V11**

  - Type: strategy
