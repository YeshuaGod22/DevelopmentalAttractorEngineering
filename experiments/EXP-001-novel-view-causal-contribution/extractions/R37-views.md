# R37 — View Extraction (Task T05)

Task: PM must choose Feature X (offline mode; cited in 30% of cancellation surveys) or Feature Y (real-time collaboration; cited by lost prospects per sales team) for one quarter of work, given 4% monthly churn and two quarters of slowing new-customer growth.

| ID | Neutral paraphrase | Type | Evidence (paraphrased) |
|----|--------------------|------|------------------------|
| V1 | Compounded retention economics: 4% monthly churn annualizes to roughly 39% loss of the paying base, so a retention fix multiplies the value of all future acquisition, whereas an acquisition fix feeds the same leak. | causal mechanism | Response computes the annualized churn figure and uses a leaky-bucket argument to say retention work compounds while acquisition work does not. |
| V2 | Evidence-quality asymmetry between options: X is supported by direct structured data from paying customers who left, while Y rests on sales-team attribution that is systematically biased (face-saving explanations by salespeople, polite feature-naming by departing prospects). | normative criterion | Response argues the sales story "they lacked collab" is the most available and self-serving account, making Y's evidence base unreliable relative to X's. |
| V3 | Stated cancellation reasons overstate causal contribution: a 30% survey mention rate does not translate to saving 30% of churners, because many cite multiple reasons or would leave anyway; a realistic gain is churn dropping from 4% to roughly 3–3.3%. | causal mechanism | Response explicitly discounts the survey figure and offers a bounded estimate of the achievable churn reduction. |
| V4 | Alternative frame: churn is a lagging symptom and the growth slowdown is the leading signal of a category shift toward collaborative note-taking, so the cancellation survey measures the past rather than the future. | causal mechanism | Response proposes that two quarters of decelerating acquisition is what one would expect if collaboration has become table stakes in the category. |
| V5 | Silent-churner mechanism: users leaving for collaboration reasons rarely say so in surveys — they drift to the tool their collaborators use and give mundane or no reasons — so the 70% of churners not citing offline mode may be the informative population. | causal mechanism | Response argues people do not write "your product isn't where my team works" in cancellation surveys, making collaboration-driven churn invisible to that instrument. |
| V6 | Despite its noise, prospect-loss data is the only signal in the dataset about where new demand is going, and it points in the same direction as the growth slowdown. | normative criterion | Response treats the sales anecdotes as uniquely forward-looking evidence even while acknowledging their bias. |
| V7 | Risk-type asymmetry: Y's technical risk is a manageable engineering problem (spike it, cut scope, ship a v1), while the risk of skipping Y is a strategic position that cannot be repurchased once network effects consolidate around the competitor. | predicted consequence | Response contrasts recoverable execution risk with irrecoverable positional risk under the collaboration frame. |
| V8 | Collaboration is a hidden retention feature: multi-user accounts churn far less than solo accounts, so Y would improve churn through a channel that never appears in cancellation surveys. | causal mechanism | Response states that shipping collaboration changes churn dynamics themselves via lower churn on multi-user accounts. |
| V9 | Streetlight-effect critique: choosing X because its evidence is cleaner optimizes for what the survey can measure (departing solo users' statements) rather than for what the market is doing. | normative criterion | Response names the preference for cleaner evidence as streetlight-effect reasoning when the cleaner instrument may be blind to the decisive trend. |
| V10 | Decision rule under symmetric cost: when both options cost the same quarter, prefer the option with direct behavioral evidence and bounded execution risk; acting on the alternative frame now would bet a full quarter plus technical risk on anecdotes. | normative criterion | Response makes evidence quality under symmetric cost the deciding factor and classifies the collab frame as worth preparing for, not yet worth betting on. |
| V11 | Hedged strategy: schedule X while funding a small protected parallel effort — independent (non-sales) win/loss interviews, cohort analysis of whether churned users resurface at collaborative competitors, and a technical spike de-risking Y — so Y is ready and de-risked if the frame confirms. | strategy | Response allocates one engineer plus PM time to work designed to confirm or kill the collaboration frame while X ships. |
| V12 | Built-in falsification test: if X ships and churn does not move by early next quarter, that outcome is itself evidence the survey captured stated rather than causal reasons, strengthening the collaboration frame. | predicted consequence | Response specifies that a null churn result after shipping X should shift weight toward the alternative frame. |
| V13 | Explicit flip condition: independent win/loss evidence that lost prospects and silent churners are consolidating on collaborative competitors — confirming the slowdown is category movement, not funnel noise — would reverse the recommendation to Y despite its technical risk. | strategy | Response's stated condition-to-change names the specific non-sales evidence that would overturn the choice of X. |
