# R25 — View Extraction (Task T11)

Task: Design agency's 40% client demands a dedicated account team (its two strongest designers) with renewal four months out and a consolidation-minded procurement lead; smaller clients praise those designers by name and one hints at expansion.

## Extracted views

**V1 — Dedication deepens an already dangerous concentration**
- Type: causal mechanism
- Paraphrase: Fully dedicating both star designers converts 40% revenue concentration into a deeper dependency, because the same client would then hold both the revenue and the people who keep the rest of the client book attached.
- Evidence: The response argues unconditional dedication turns 40% revenue exposure into 40% plus the two people who make other accounts sticky.

**V2 — The star designers are the retention glue for the smaller accounts**
- Type: stakeholder-impact
- Paraphrase: The smaller clients' loyalty (and one's hinted expansion) is attached to the named designers specifically; pulling them damages those relationships and destroys the agency's only organic hedge against the big client leaving.
- Evidence: The response notes smaller clients praise the designers by name and says removing them kills the hedge against the very scenario the agency fears.

**V3 — Pre-renewal timing makes compliance an unpriced concession**
- Type: constraint (with a causal timing mechanism)
- Paraphrase: Because the demand arrives before renewal, the agency would bear the full cost of compliance during exactly the window in which the client still retains the option not to renew, giving away value for nothing guaranteed in return.
- Evidence: The response labels compliance during the pre-renewal window an unpriced concession and says full dedication should be tied to a signed multi-year or expanded commitment.

**V4 — Consolidation frame: the demand is an audition in a winner-take-more game**
- Type: causal mechanism
- Paraphrase: A procurement lead consolidating vendors means surviving vendors typically gain scope; clients do not request dedicated teams from vendors they plan to cut, so the demand likely signals an internal case being built to consolidate work onto this agency.
- Evidence: The response's alternate frame holds that the dedicated-team demand is the audition for surviving consolidation, sponsored by someone on the client's working side.

**V5 — A hedged counter-offer reads as lack of commitment and loses the audition**
- Type: predicted consequence
- Paraphrase: If the audition frame is right, a contingent, lawyerly partial offer signals tepid commitment and hands the consolidated relationship to a competitor willing to say yes cleanly.
- Evidence: The response warns that hedging at the moment the client is testing for commitment is the classic way agencies lose anchor clients.

**V6 — Sequence diversification after securing the renewal**
- Type: strategy
- Paraphrase: Concentration risk should be addressed from a position of secured, larger revenue — by hiring and training the next tier of designers after winning the renewal — rather than by half-committing during the trial period.
- Evidence: The response says you diversify from secured revenue, not by half-committing during the audition and losing everything.

**V7 — The frames' disagreement turns on a discoverable fact: who sponsors the demand**
- Type: strategy (diagnostic move resolving the decision)
- Paraphrase: Whether the demand originates from the client's day-to-day working teams (audition) or from procurement itself (squeeze) determines which frame is right, and the agency can learn this within about two weeks by asking stakeholders how the renewal decision will be made and who sponsors the request.
- Evidence: The response makes discovering the demand's sponsorship the agency's first task before completing the commitment decision.

**V8 — Phased commitment: a dedicated pod framed as phase one of full dedication**
- Type: strategy
- Paraphrase: Commit immediately and visibly to a pod led by one star with the second substantially allocated, presented as the first phase of full dedication (not a counter-offer), inviting the client to co-design the full structure in the renewal conversation — performing commitment while retaining option value.
- Evidence: The final recommendation specifies this pod structure and framing as the action pending discovery of the demand's origin.

**V9 — Backfill the expanding smaller client to keep the hedge alive**
- Type: strategy
- Paraphrase: Assign the next-best designer, mentored by one of the stars during transition, to the smaller client hinting at expansion, so that relationship does not decay on the assumption the big renewal will close.
- Evidence: The response directs this staffing move explicitly, calling that relationship the hedge that must not be allowed to decay.

**V10 — Downside asymmetry justifies accepting the tepid-signal risk**
- Type: normative criterion (asymmetric-loss reasoning bearing on the decision)
- Paraphrase: The phased approach is right because the losses are asymmetric: a genuine consolidator will tolerate an enthusiastically offered two-phase commitment, whereas stripping the other accounts for a client that then churns loses both the 40% and the hedge.
- Evidence: The response states its confidence rests on this asymmetry and openly accepts the residual risk of reading as tepid.

**V11 — Explicit escalation trigger to full dedication**
- Type: predicted consequence (conditional decision rule)
- Paraphrase: If discovery shows the demand is sponsored by the client's working teams — especially if competitors are being evaluated on willingness to dedicate — the answer flips to immediate full dedication of both designers, executed before the renewal rather than after.
- Evidence: The response's stated flip condition is working-team sponsorship of the demand, with pre-renewal escalation instructed in that case.

## Notes
- The "initial view" counter-offer (tie full dedication to a signed commitment) is folded into V3/V8 rather than counted separately, since the final recommendation supersedes it with the same underlying pricing logic.
- Generic caveats (confidence number, restatement of the dilemma) were not counted as views.
