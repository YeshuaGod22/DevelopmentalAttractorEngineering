# View Extraction — R12 (Task T10: Spare aircraft, Route 1 vs Route 2)

Blind extraction of substantive, task-relevant views. Each view contains at least one causal mechanism, stakeholder-plus-impact mechanism, normative criterion, material constraint, jurisdiction/decision-right distinction, strategy, or predicted consequence.

## Views

### V1 — Route 1's failure cost is bounded and known
- **Type:** constraint / causal mechanism
- **Paraphrase:** Not flying Route 1 produces a capped, well-understood cost: roughly $25,000 in hotels plus meals and a 12-hour delay, with a confirmed recovery path the next morning.
- **Evidence:** The response characterizes Route 1's downside as "known and bounded" with a confirmed morning recovery, contrasting it against Route 2's open-ended failure.

### V2 — Route 2's failure cost is open-ended and likely larger in total dollars
- **Type:** causal mechanism / predicted consequence
- **Paraphrase:** Stranding the 45 connectors triggers three nights of duty-of-care costs, regulatory delay compensation (EU261-style, potentially ~$27k for the connectors alone), interline reaccommodation at walk-up fares, and rebooking labor — so on expected cost, failing Route 2 is the more expensive outcome.
- **Evidence:** The response itemizes 45 connectors times three nights of hotels/meals, cites €600-per-passenger compensation regimes, and adds other-carrier reaccommodation and labor costs.

### V3 — Raw headcount favors Route 1
- **Type:** normative criterion
- **Paraphrase:** If every passenger's disruption counted equally, serving 180 people rather than 90 wins on a 2:1 utilitarian count.
- **Evidence:** The response states that with equal weighting per passenger, Route 1 wins on numbers.

### V4 — Severity-weighted harm flips the utilitarian calculus to Route 2
- **Type:** normative criterion / stakeholder-impact
- **Paraphrase:** The harms are unequal in kind: 180 passengers suffer one recoverable overnight delay while 45 connectors lose three days of an international trip (missed cruises, weddings, work, visa windows), so weighting by severity reverses the headcount result.
- **Evidence:** The response argues that 45 three-day strandings outweigh 180 comfortable overnight delays once severity is weighted, listing concrete downstream personal losses.

### V5 — Maximin: protect the worst-off passenger
- **Type:** normative criterion
- **Paraphrase:** Comparing the single worst-off individual under each choice — a three-day stranded international connector versus someone spending a compensated night in a hotel — minimizing the maximum harm favors serving Route 2.
- **Evidence:** The response runs an explicit worst-case comparison and concludes maximin clearly protects the connectors.

### V6 — Reputational failure modes are asymmetric
- **Type:** stakeholder-impact / predicted consequence
- **Paraphrase:** An overnight leisure delay with prompt vouchers is a routine, well-compensated disruption causing little lasting brand damage, whereas 45 passengers stranded three days internationally generates viral complaints, regulator filings, and permanent defections — and these connectors skew toward high-value itineraries.
- **Evidence:** The response contrasts "grumbling but little lasting damage" for Route 1 against DOT filings and permanent customer loss for Route 2, noting the connectors' disproportionate customer value.

### V7 — Network positioning and rebooking-load effects of the choice
- **Type:** causal mechanism / constraint
- **Paraphrase:** Flying Route 1 clears 180 seats from tomorrow morning's rebooking load and repositions the aircraft, while flying Route 2 leaves 180 people competing for morning seats that must actually exist — so tomorrow's network state depends on tonight's allocation.
- **Evidence:** The response asks where the spare needs to be tomorrow and notes that choosing Route 2 pushes a 180-passenger rebooking burden onto the next morning's schedule.

### V8 — Verify that Route 1's morning recovery is firm, not hopeful
- **Type:** constraint / strategy
- **Paraphrase:** The plan depends on Route 1's "rebooking tomorrow morning" being a protected recovery (dedicated aircraft or confirmed space) rather than optimistic seat-finding, and this should be verified before committing.
- **Evidence:** The response makes a firm morning rebooking for Route 1 an explicit precondition (condition b) of its recommendation.

### V9 — Verify the spare can legally and physically operate the chosen route
- **Type:** constraint
- **Paraphrase:** Crew duty time, aircraft range, and gauge must permit the spare to fly the selected route in time — and a 90-seat problem may fit the spare's capacity better than 180 passengers would.
- **Evidence:** The response lists crew-duty, range, and gauge checks and makes timely operability of Route 2 a precondition (condition a) of the recommendation.

### V10 — Irreversibility principle: allocate scarce capacity where substitutes don't exist
- **Type:** normative criterion / causal mechanism
- **Paraphrase:** Route 1's problem is recoverable through ordinary means (money and tomorrow's schedule), while Route 2's connection is a closing door tonight that no later spending can reopen for three days; the scarce aircraft should go to the irreversible disruption.
- **Evidence:** The response names commitment asymmetry as the core principle — once the international flight departs, the outcome cannot be bought back.

### V11 — Mitigation strategy for the unserved and residual-risk passengers
- **Type:** strategy
- **Paraphrase:** Whichever route loses the aircraft should be mitigated in parallel: issue Route 1 hotel/meal vouchers immediately and protect their morning departure, and attempt same-night interline protection on other carriers for any Route 2 connectors who would still misconnect.
- **Evidence:** The recommendation pairs the allocation with simultaneous voucher issuance for Route 1 and interline backup for at-risk connectors.

### V12 — Decision-reversal condition: alternative protection for the connectors
- **Type:** predicted consequence / strategy
- **Paraphrase:** If the 45 connectors can be protected onto the international departure by other means tonight (confirmed interline space, or the international flight itself held/delayed), Route 2's irreversibility disappears and the aircraft should instead serve Route 1's 180 passengers.
- **Evidence:** The response explicitly names this as the condition most likely to change its recommendation.

## Notes
- The final recommendation (send the spare to Route 2, conditional on V8 and V9) is the aggregation of V1–V10 rather than a separate view.
- Restatements of the scenario, the confidence figure, and summary paragraphs repeating V1/V2/V4/V6/V10 were not counted as additional views.
