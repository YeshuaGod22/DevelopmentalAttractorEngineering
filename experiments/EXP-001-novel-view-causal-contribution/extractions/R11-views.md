# R11 — View Extraction (blind)

Task T11: Design agency's 40%-of-revenue client demands a dedicated account team built from the agency's two strongest designers; smaller clients have praised those designers by name and one hints at expansion; renewal in four months under a vendor-consolidating procurement lead.

## Extracted views

- **V1**
  - Paraphrase: Losing the 40% client is plausibly existential for the agency, so protecting the renewal has dominant expected-value-of-survival weight, making the dedicated team look like cheap insurance.
  - Type: causal mechanism / predicted consequence
  - Evidence: The response argues no realistic combination of smaller-client growth could offset a 40% revenue loss within a year, so renewal protection dominates on survival grounds.

- **V2**
  - Paraphrase: Granting the demand paradoxically deepens the very concentration risk it is meant to insure against, because the agency's top talent, relationships, and portfolio output would all flow to the single dominant client.
  - Type: causal mechanism
  - Evidence: The response notes that dedicating the best people raises effective concentration above 40% and calls insurance that enlarges the insured risk a bad policy.

- **V3**
  - Paraphrase: The demand's timing — four months before renewal, from a consolidation-minded new procurement lead — suggests it may be a pre-renewal negotiating squeeze or paper-trail construction rather than a genuine service need.
  - Type: causal mechanism (strategic-intent inference)
  - Evidence: The response offers two readings of the demand, including procurement extracting concessions before price talks or documenting an unmet ask to justify a consolidation already leaning elsewhere.

- **V4**
  - Paraphrase: Vendor consolidation decisions are typically driven by cost, vendor count, and category coverage, so a dedicated team may not influence the renewal outcome at all.
  - Type: causal mechanism / predicted consequence
  - Evidence: The response states consolidation is usually decided on cost and vendor-count grounds, so dedication might not move the decision.

- **V5**
  - Paraphrase: Conceding unilaterally spends the agency's best bargaining chip for nothing contractual; the dedication should instead be traded for a signed multi-year renewal with minimum commitment.
  - Type: strategy
  - Evidence: The response recommends never giving dedication away but converting it into leverage via a pod contingent on a 2–3 year signed renewal.

- **V6**
  - Paraphrase: Locking the two strongest designers onto a single demanding account creates burnout and attrition risk, and their willingness to accept the arrangement is a hard constraint; a designer quitting would cost the agency both the big client and the small ones.
  - Type: constraint / stakeholder-impact
  - Evidence: The response describes single-account lock-in as a classic burnout setup and insists any structure must be one the designers themselves accept.

- **V7**
  - Paraphrase: Clients praising individual designers by name reveals key-person risk — relationships live in people rather than the agency — which should be remedied regardless of this decision.
  - Type: causal mechanism / constraint
  - Evidence: The response flags by-name praise as evidence that client relationships reside in individuals and calls for fixing that independently.

- **V8**
  - Paraphrase: The smaller clients' by-name praise plus an expansion hint marks the agency's escape path from concentration, and removing the named designers from those accounts would likely kill that growth because those clients will notice the disappearance.
  - Type: predicted consequence / stakeholder-impact
  - Evidence: The response argues the diversification path runs through the smaller accounts and that pulling the praised designers is the most reliable way to close it.

- **V9**
  - Paraphrase: Under explicit probability estimates, full unilateral dedication risks losing both the growth path and the renewal, while a conditional counteroffer preserves optionality across every branch of uncertainty.
  - Type: predicted consequence (decision-under-uncertainty asymmetry)
  - Evidence: The response assigns rough probabilities (e.g., 25–40% the client leaves despite dedication) and concludes the asymmetry favors the conditional offer, which risks only mildly annoying procurement.

- **V10**
  - Paraphrase: A partial-dedication structure — one star designer as majority-allocated creative lead of a dedicated pod with mid-level support, an account manager, SLAs, and priority escalation — can satisfy the client's substantive continuity concern without full dedication.
  - Type: strategy
  - Evidence: The response's first counteroffer element is a pod led by one star at majority (not 100%) allocation with named SLAs, framed as substance for the client's real concern.

- **V11**
  - Paraphrase: Making deeper dedication activate only upon a signed multi-year minimum-spend renewal reframes the four-month window from a threat against the agency into a deadline that binds the client too.
  - Type: strategy / causal mechanism
  - Evidence: The response states the contingent premium structure turns the renewal window into a mutual deadline.

- **V12**
  - Paraphrase: Keep the second star designer visibly anchored on the growth accounts and actively pursue the hinted expansion as a deliberate concentration hedge.
  - Type: strategy
  - Evidence: The response's third element keeps the second designer on the smaller accounts, visible to the expansion-minded client, and prices the expansion as a hedge.

- **V13**
  - Paraphrase: De-risk the people layer by pairing each star with a strong second on key accounts and putting retention terms in front of both designers now, asking them directly what allocation they would accept.
  - Type: strategy
  - Evidence: The response's fourth element prescribes succession pairing and immediate retention offers plus direct consultation with the designers.

- **V14**
  - Paraphrase: Probe the new procurement lead early to learn the actual consolidation criteria, so the agency staffs the real decision question rather than an assumed one.
  - Type: strategy
  - Evidence: The response's fifth element requests a meeting with procurement this month to discover whether price, vendor count, or capabilities drive the consolidation.

- **V15**
  - Paraphrase: The recommendation flips if the client credibly confirms, ideally in writing, that full dedication of both designers is a binding renewal condition paired with a multi-year minimum-spend commitment — then survival math dominates and the agency should dedicate while racing to diversify.
  - Type: predicted consequence (condition-based decision reversal)
  - Evidence: The response's stated flip condition is explicit written confirmation of dedication as a renewal requirement in exchange for a signed multi-year commitment.
