# R14 — View Extraction (Task T07)

Task: Overwhelmed open-source maintainers choosing what to do first — issue bankruptcy with a triage rota (A) vs. promoting three sub-one-year contributors to maintainer in a security-sensitive project (B).

| ID | Neutral paraphrase | Type | Evidence (paraphrased) |
|----|--------------------|------|------------------------|
| V1 | The two symptoms are causally distinct: contributor attrition is driven by PR review latency, not the issue backlog, so the binding constraint is review throughput. | causal mechanism | Opening section argues issues pile up quietly while contributors leave because their pull requests rot, so only one symptom is doing the stated damage. |
| V2 | The options fix different problems and are not substitutes, so the real decision is sequencing rather than choosing one over the other. | strategy | The framing section states A treats the issue pile and B treats review throughput, making sequencing, not selection, the actual decision. |
| V3 | Only promotion adds reviewer capacity; issue bankruptcy leaves the same two maintainers reviewing every PR and does nothing for review latency. | causal mechanism | The throughput view notes that after bankruptcy two maintainers still review every PR, so B is the only intervention that adds capacity. |
| V4 | Delay costs compound: each additional month of six-week review times drives away precisely the contributors the project would later want to promote. | predicted consequence | The queueing section warns that every month of slow reviews sheds the future promotion pool itself. |
| V5 | Promoting three short-tenure contributors simultaneously under maintainer-burnout pressure closely matches the xz-utils supply-chain attack pattern, which exploited exactly that condition. | causal mechanism | The security view invokes xz-utils, where a helpful contributor with under two years of history was promoted under burnout pressure and was the attack. |
| V6 | Maintainer trust grants are effectively hard to reverse because revoking access from a well-meaning person is socially costly and in practice rarely happens. | constraint | The security section observes that walking back access gracefully is socially costly and often just does not occur. |
| V7 | In the short term B increases the incumbent maintainers' load (onboarding, mentoring, reviewing the new maintainers' reviews), while A is cheap and gives immediate psychological relief. | stakeholder-impact | The sustainability view describes A as a weekend of scripting that relieves a demoralizing 400-item inbox, whereas B initially adds mentoring and meta-review work. |
| V8 | Sequence cheap relief before trust-building so near-burnout maintainers have the slack to run promotion properly instead of rubber-stamping it. | strategy | The sustainability section recommends doing the cheap relief first so the maintainers can do B's trust-building work well. |
| V9 | Issue bankruptcy is a normalized community practice whose "reopen if still relevant" message filters for live issues at near-zero cost, limiting reputational damage. | causal mechanism | The community view says bankruptcy irritates some reporters but is well understood, and the reopen invitation filters live issues essentially for free. |
| V10 | Promotion is the strongest retention signal a project can send, and delaying it risks losing the three candidate contributors themselves. | predicted consequence | The community section states promotion tells contributors there is a path to ownership and that delaying B may cost the candidates. |
| V11 | Decouple the signal from the access grant: tell the three candidates immediately that they are on a maintainer track even if full permissions come later. | strategy | The community view advises announcing the maintainer track now so the retention signal lands ahead of the trust grant. |
| V12 | The A/B dichotomy is partly false because forge permission systems are graduated: triage rights are a much smaller trust grant than commit rights, and release/signing authority is larger still. | jurisdiction | The governance view distinguishes triage (label, close, assign) from commit and from release/signing authority as separate levels of granted power. |
| V13 | Implement A via a scoped slice of B: grant the three contributors triage permissions now (low blast radius, reversible), and use their observed conduct as the evidence base for full promotion in 3–6 months, staggered one candidate at a time. | strategy | The governance section and recommendation propose staffing the triage rota with triage-level grants as an explicit on-ramp, with full promotion staggered after months of observed conduct. |
| V14 | Review latency can be attacked without commit rights by requiring a candidate's approving review before a maintainer's final pass, reducing maintainer effort per PR. | causal mechanism | The governance view proposes candidate first-pass reviews as a way to cut maintainer review effort substantially without granting commit bits. |
| V15 | Conditional reversal: if review latency is imminently fatal (candidates about to quit, security patches sitting unreviewed) and a candidate has a strong verifiable outside track record, promote one person to full maintainer immediately and do the bankruptcy the following week. | predicted consequence | The stated flip condition promotes a single candidate first when latency is imminently fatal and identity/track-record evidence is strong. |

Notes on exclusions: the confidence number, the restatement of the recommendation in the closing lines, and general framing language ("which option is better is not the question") were not counted as separate views; the sequencing reframe is counted once (V2) and the scoped-triage recommendation once (V13) despite appearing in both the perspectives and recommendation sections.
