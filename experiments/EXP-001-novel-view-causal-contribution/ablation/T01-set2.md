# T01 — Considerations (Set 2)

- The demand-supply mismatch is worse than stated: 40% of tickets arrive 6pm–11pm while staffing drops to two agents until 8pm and none from 8pm to 11pm, so part of the demand window has zero coverage, producing the 3x evening response times.
- Slow evening response with enterprise complaints constitutes an active revenue risk (enterprise churn), not merely an operational annoyance.
- The contractor option's 6-week onboarding period leaves the coverage problem unaddressed during that window.
- New part-time contractors are poorly suited to handle enterprise-grade tickets during their early months, so contractor coverage may not resolve the enterprise-client problem quickly.
- Reassigning shifts is within the team lead's ordinary operational authority, and the reluctant agents have said they would comply, so the shift option is legitimately available.
- In a small tenured team, the binding constraint is retention of experienced agents (who carry product knowledge and enterprise relationships), not schedule coverage hours.
- Stated compliance is unreliable: forced evening shifts are a strong quit trigger in support work, and resignations tend to cluster, with one departure licensing the next.
- Losing one experienced agent would cost more than a year of the contractor budget and would open a coverage hole in both daytime and evening worse than the one being fixed.
- The two risks differ in reversibility: slow evening response is recoverable and bridgeable, whereas attrition of tenured staff cannot be recovered on a comparable timescale — so choose the option whose downside is reversible.
- Independent of the shift question, contractors are cheap permanent capacity: two evening contractors at roughly half an FTE's cost against 40% of demand is worth buying on its own terms.
- The problem underdetermines the frame conflict: data needed to weigh enterprise churn against staff attrition (contract renewal dates, agent tenure, local labor market) is absent, so a pure-form choice of either option is unjustified.
- Recommended plan: do both, sequenced — hire contractors as the permanent evening solution and run a volunteer-first, incentivized, time-boxed 3pm–11pm rotation as the bridge, with compelled assignment only as a last resort carrying a written end date tied to contractor readiness.
- Enterprise complaints are partly relational: contacting the clients immediately with a concrete plan and dates addresses the feeling of being unheard, which matters alongside actual latency.
- Conditional fallback: if only one option is affordable, take the shift move because uncovered enterprise demand is the nearer cliff — but doing so without a time-box converts a coverage problem into a retention problem.
- Reversal condition: an enterprise contract renewal or escalation inside the 6-week window, or contractors proving unable to handle enterprise-tier tickets, would force the permanent shift of existing agents despite the retention cost.
