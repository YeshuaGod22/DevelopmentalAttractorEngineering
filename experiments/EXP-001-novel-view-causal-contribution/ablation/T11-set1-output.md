# T11 — Answer (Set 1 considerations only)

## Task
A design agency's biggest client (40% of revenue) demands a dedicated account team, requiring its two strongest designers to come off all other accounts. Two smaller clients have praised those designers by name; one has hinted at expansion. The big client's contract renews in four months under a new procurement lead known for consolidating vendors. Should the agency dedicate the team?

## Weighing the considerations

**The strongest and most reliable considerations are the structural ones about risk and timing.** Considerations 1–3 are sound and mutually reinforcing: fully dedicating both stars deepens dependence on a single client (revenue concentration becomes talent concentration), damages the specific relationships that constitute the agency's only hedge, and does all of this during the four-month window in which the client retains a free option not to renew. The agency would pay the full cost of compliance up front for nothing contractually secured.

**The "audition vs. squeeze" diagnosis (considerations 4 and 7) is plausible but should not be over-trusted.** Consideration 4's inference — "clients do not request dedicated teams from vendors they plan to cut" — is a reasonable base rate, and it does suggest the demand is more likely an audition for consolidated scope than a prelude to termination. But it is an inference from a reputation, not evidence about this client, and procurement leads also extract concessions from vendors they intend to keep on worse terms. Consideration 7 is the right corrective: whether the demand is sponsored by the client's working teams (audition) or by procurement itself (squeeze) is discoverable within roughly two weeks by asking stakeholders how the renewal decision will be made and who sponsors the request. That discovery is cheap, fast relative to the four-month clock, and directly determines which frame governs. It should be done first.

**The asymmetry argument (consideration 12) is the decisive weighing principle.** The downside branches are not symmetric: a genuine consolidator who receives an enthusiastic two-phase commitment rarely walks over the phasing itself, whereas an agency that strips its other accounts for a client that then churns loses both the 40% and the hedge simultaneously — an existential outcome. Under that asymmetry, the phased structure dominates unless discovery shows the audition frame clearly.

**Consideration 5 is the main counterweight, and it is partly right.** A hedged, lawyerly counter-offer would read as tepid and could hand the consolidated relationship to a competitor willing to say yes cleanly. But consideration 8 answers it: the risk is in the *presentation*, not the phasing. A pod led by one star with the second substantially allocated, announced immediately and framed as the first phase of full dedication — with the client invited to co-design the full structure in the renewal conversation — performs commitment while retaining option value. That resolves the tension between 5 and 12 rather than choosing one over the other.

**Consideration 6 ("fix concentration after winning the renewal, by hiring the next tier") is correct as sequencing but does not justify full immediate dedication now**; it describes what to do once revenue is secured, and it is compatible with the phased path.

**Consideration 9 (assign and mentor the next-best designer onto the expanding smaller client) is a low-cost, high-value hedge** that directly protects the asset considerations 1–2 identify, and costs almost nothing under any scenario. It should happen regardless.

**Consideration 13 states the flip condition** and is consistent with the rest: if discovery shows the demand is sponsored by the client's working teams — especially if competitors are being evaluated on willingness to dedicate — the expected value of the consolidated relationship outweighs the hedge, and the agency should move to full dedication of both designers before the renewal, not after.

## Answer

Do not fully dedicate both star designers now. Instead:

1. **Immediately and visibly commit to a dedicated pod** — one star leading full-time, the second substantially allocated — presented enthusiastically as *phase one of full dedication*, not as a counter-offer, with an invitation to co-design the full structure in the renewal conversation.
2. **Run two weeks of discovery** on who sponsors the demand and how the renewal decision will be made.
3. **Backfill the expanding smaller client** with the next-best designer, mentored through the transition by one of the stars, so that relationship does not decay.
4. **If discovery shows the demand comes from the client's working teams** (audition dynamics, competitors being scored on willingness to dedicate), flip to immediate full dedication of both designers before the renewal.
5. **After the renewal is won**, address concentration from strength by hiring and training the next tier of designers.

This captures nearly all the upside of saying yes (the enthusiasm and visible commitment the audition frame requires) while avoiding the one catastrophic branch: stripping every other account for a client that then declines to renew.

Recommendation: Do not fully dedicate both designers yet — commit immediately to a dedicated pod (one star full-time, the second substantially allocated) framed as phase one of full dedication, run ~2 weeks of discovery on who sponsors the demand, backfill the expanding smaller client, and escalate to full dedication before renewal only if the demand is sponsored by the client's working teams.

Confidence: 78

Main reason: The losses are asymmetric — an enthusiastically framed phased commitment satisfies a genuine consolidator, whereas stripping all other accounts during the pre-renewal window (when the client holds a free option not to renew) risks losing both the 40% client and the smaller-client hedge at once.

Condition most likely to change recommendation: Discovery showing the demand is sponsored by the client's day-to-day working teams and that competing agencies are being evaluated on willingness to dedicate — in that case the recommendation flips to immediate full dedication of both designers before the renewal.
