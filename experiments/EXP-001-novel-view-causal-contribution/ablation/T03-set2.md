# T03 — Considerations (Set 2)

- When catering is added to Venue A's price, the two venues' effective all-in costs are nearly identical, so cost cannot be the deciding factor.
- Venue A's full refundability until 60 days out functions as a free option: the organizer retains ~90 days of costless exit if the event collapses or circumstances change.
- Venue B's extra 150 seats represent revenue upside that Venue A structurally cannot capture, worth up to about $22,500 at the given ticket price.
- A faster early-bird period is weak evidence of higher final attendance, because early-bird velocity often measures demand pulled forward in time (price-sensitive buyers shifting earlier) rather than incremental demand.
- Ticket break-even is not a differentiator: both venues are covered comfortably at attendance levels far below last year's figure.
- The decision-relevant risks are asymmetric scenarios: full cancellation (where A protects and B loses $22,000) versus demand overshoot (where B captures the excess and A must turn buyers away with zero headroom at exactly 300).
- A capacity ceiling can inflict compounding strategic damage: turning attendees away in a growth year bleeds momentum, pushes rejected buyers toward competing events, and "sold out" converts poorly into future attendance.
- The growth frame's key weakness is that it implicitly prices cancellation risk at zero despite B being non-refundable five months out.
- The hedge strategy is conditional on Venue B remaining bookable later; if B is unique in the market and the organizer's ambition is growth, booking B immediately becomes defensible.
- Negotiate directly with Venue B for a 60-day hold or right of first refusal, which would eliminate the availability risk underlying the hedge.
