# T10 — Considerations (Set 1)

- Route 1's harm is bounded and recoverable — an overnight delay with rebooking the next morning at a known, capped cost (~$25,000) — so all 180 passengers are made whole within roughly 12 hours.
- The 45 international connectors on Route 2 face a categorically different, tail-risk harm — a three-day strand — that is qualitatively worse than a longer delay (missed weddings, visa windows, work obligations).
- Leaving Route 2 unserved carries direct duty-of-care costs (estimated $50,000–80,000+ for three nights of hotels and meals) plus possible regulatory compensation, so its expected cost exceeds Route 1's $25,000 and its worst case exceeds it by multiples.
- A three-day strand generates reputational damage — lifetime customer loss and press coverage — beyond the direct costs.
- Severity of harm should outweigh raw headcount as the deciding criterion when the severity gap is wide; both minimax-regret and expected-total-harm reasoning converge on serving Route 2.
- "No rebooking availability for three days" likely describes only the airline's own aircraft and itineraries; an operations planner should measure harm as the residual after the recovery toolkit (interline, alliance partners, alternate hubs, ground transport to the gateway, holding the connecting flight) is exhausted.
- Route 1's harm is irreducible by any lever other than the spare aircraft (full flight, no alternative tonight), whereas Route 2's harm may be reducible through other channels — so under this frame the scarce asset should go where it is the only remedy, flipping the answer to Route 1.
- Where the spare aircraft and its crew (within duty limits) end the night determines what tomorrow's schedule looks like, so overnight positioning is a second-order factor in the allocation.
- Before releasing the aircraft, run a quick check of reprotection paths for the 45 connectors — a minutes-long verification that dominates either unconditional answer as a decision procedure.
- Send the aircraft to Route 2 but attempt partner-airline reprotection for the connectors in parallel anyway, with an explicit reversal trigger: if a viable path materializes in time, redirect the spare to Route 1.
- Because Route 1's passengers are being knowingly disadvantaged, they should receive proactive rebooking confirmation and immediate voucher issuance rather than counter-line service.
