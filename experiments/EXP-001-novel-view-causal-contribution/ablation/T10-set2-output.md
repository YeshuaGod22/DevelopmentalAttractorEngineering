# T10 — Answer (from Set 2 considerations only)

## Task
One spare aircraft, two disrupted routes tonight. Route 1: 180 mostly-leisure passengers, rebooking tomorrow morning, ~$25,000 in hotel vouchers. Route 2: 90 passengers, 45 of them connecting to an international departure tonight with no rebooking for three days. Which route gets the aircraft?

## Weighing the considerations

**Severity asymmetry (considerations 1, 2, 4).** These are the strongest and most credible points. Route 1's harm is genuinely bounded: a known ~12-hour delay with a capped, quoted cost. Route 2 contains a tail-risk harm — a three-day strand for 45 international connectors — that is categorically different in kind, not just degree (missed non-recoverable events, visa windows). The claim that this severity gap should dominate raw headcount (consideration 5) is a reasonable decision principle here: 180 people mildly harmed vs. 45 people severely harmed is exactly the case where per-person severity, minimax regret, and expected total harm all point the same way. I accept this cluster largely at face value; the task's own facts support it.

**Cost comparison (consideration 3).** The $50,000–80,000+ duty-of-care estimate for a three-day strand is an inference, not a given fact, but it is directionally sound: three nights of hotels and meals for stranded international passengers plausibly exceeds one night of vouchers for 180 domestic leisure passengers, and regulatory compensation regimes for long international disruptions make the worst case worse still. Even discounting the point estimate, the expected-cost ordering (Route 2 unserved > Route 1 unserved) survives. Reputational damage (consideration 4) reinforces but shouldn't independently drive the call.

**The flip argument (consideration 6).** This is the one consideration pointing to Route 1: allocate the scarce asset where it is the *only* remedy. Route 1's flight is full with no alternative tonight; Route 2's connectors might be reprotectable via partner airlines. The logic is legitimate — scarce-resource-to-irreducible-harm is a real allocation principle — but its factual premise is weak against the stated facts: the task says the international departure has *no rebooking availability for three days*, which is precisely a statement that the harm may not be reducible through other channels. Consideration 6 only wins if partner reprotection actually exists, which is unverified. As an unconditional argument it should not flip the answer; as a *conditional* it is properly absorbed by considerations 8 and 9.

**The decision procedure (considerations 8, 9).** These reconcile the tension correctly: a minutes-long check of reprotection paths for the 45 connectors dominates either unconditional answer, and dispatching to Route 2 with a parallel reprotection attempt and an explicit reversal trigger preserves option value. This is the right operational shape — but the question asks which route gets the aircraft, and the default while the check runs (and if it fails, as the stated facts suggest it will) is Route 2. Note the reversal trigger is time-boxed by reality: crew duty limits and departure windows mean the option to redirect decays fast, so the check cannot be allowed to delay dispatch.

**Second-order factors (considerations 7, 10).** Overnight positioning and crew duty limits (7) are real but underdetermined here — no facts in either direction — so they can tilt a near-tie, and this isn't one. Proactive care for Route 1's passengers (10) is a sound mitigation obligation that follows from knowingly disadvantaging them; it lowers the cost of the Route 2 choice (immediate voucher issuance and confirmed morning rebooking convert Route 1's harm from uncertain to fully bounded) but doesn't change the allocation.

**What Set 2 glosses over.** The other 45 passengers on Route 2 are treated as if only the connectors matter; in fact all 90 go unserved tonight if the aircraft goes to Route 1, and the considerations' cost estimate implicitly covers this. Also, none of the pro-Route-2 considerations quantify the probability that partner reprotection succeeds — the one number that would legitimize the flip. Absent it, the stated "no availability for three days" governs.

## Answer

Send the spare aircraft to Route 2 tonight. Simultaneously: (a) run the fast reprotection check for the 45 connectors with a hard, duty-limit-aware deadline — if a confirmed partner path materializes before dispatch commitment, redirect to Route 1; (b) immediately issue Route 1's hotel vouchers and push confirmed morning rebookings so their bounded harm stays bounded. The severity gap (a three-day international strand vs. a capped overnight delay), the expected-cost ordering, and the convergence of minimax-regret and total-harm reasoning all favor Route 2; the only argument for Route 1 rests on an unverified premise that the connectors can be reprotected elsewhere, which the task's facts contradict unless proven otherwise in the next few minutes.

Recommendation: Give the spare aircraft to Route 2 tonight, with a time-boxed parallel check for partner reprotection of the 45 connectors (redirect to Route 1 only if a confirmed alternative path emerges before dispatch), and immediate vouchers plus confirmed morning rebooking for Route 1's passengers.
Confidence: 82
Main reason: The 45 connectors face a categorically severe, largely irrecoverable three-day strand while Route 1's harm is a bounded overnight delay at a known ~$25,000 cost; severity, expected cost, and minimax regret all converge on Route 2, and the lone counterargument depends on an unverified assumption of alternative reprotection.
Condition most likely to change recommendation: A confirmed partner-airline or alternative reprotection path for the 45 international connectors emerging within the dispatch window — that would make Route 2's severe harm reducible without the spare aircraft and flip the allocation to Route 1.
