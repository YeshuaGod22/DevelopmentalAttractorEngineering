# Considerations

- Arterials are network prerequisites: residential streets that are plowed but feed into impassable arterials are not actually usable, and arterials also carry buses and emergency vehicles.
- Complaint volume is a biased welfare signal: it oversamples residents with time, phone access, and political attention while undersampling the diffuse many whose commutes quietly lengthen.
- Treat the complaint pattern partly as a communications problem: publish the plowing logic, provide a plow tracker with honest ETAs, and let council members defend the policy publicly.
- Harm from unplowed streets is threshold-shaped rather than linear: past roughly 48 hours an unplowed street produces qualitatively different harms (stranded elderly and disabled residents, blocked home health aides, wholly lost shifts, ambulance access failures) that delay-hour accounting cannot register.
- The sustained 48+ hour complaint pattern should be read as the municipality's only signal sensitive to concentrated, severe, threshold-crossing harm — evidence about severity, not squeaky-wheel noise.
- If threshold harm is the right model, the objective should shift from minimizing total delay (utilitarian) to minimizing worst-case abandonment (maximin).
- Institute a hard residential service floor: no street goes more than N hours unplowed, with overtime reserved first to guarantee the floor and only the remainder spent on arterial efficiency.
- Council members' pressure is the elected system legitimately transmitting a harm the technical metric failed to register, not political interference in a technical optimum.
- Concrete split with automatic trigger: ~70 hours to arterial-first as the default, ~30 hours held as a residential backstop that fires automatically when any street approaches a 36–40 hour cap (deliberately below the 48-hour anger threshold); the reserve is a guarantee, not a rotation, and reverts to arterials in a mild season.
- Run a one-season instrumentation effort: code the complaint corpus by severity (missed medical care, stranded residents, lost shifts vs. annoyance) and pull EMS response-time data for unplowed blocks, then re-set the split and cap on that evidence.
- If complaints turn out to encode annoyance amplified by political salience, the 30-hour reserve is an efficiency tax paid mostly for legitimacy — legitimacy has real but bounded value, and the director should state this trade openly to the council.
- The recommendation's direction is conditional on the severity evidence: confirmed abandonment-class harm at 48+ hours should tighten the cap and enlarge the reserve; annoyance-only findings should shrink the reserve toward pure arterial-first plus communication.
