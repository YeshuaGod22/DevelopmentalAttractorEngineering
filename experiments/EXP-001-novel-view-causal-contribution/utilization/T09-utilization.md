Without V, the response's own framing (line 10) would have dismissed complaints as a biased instrument and favored arterial-first.

3. **Recommendation shaped by V (lines 27–31).** The ~65/35 split toward a residential service-level guarantee, and especially the sequencing rule — "by *vulnerability*, not complaint volume: streets on the medical-needs/oxygen-dependent registry... high concentrations of elderly or carless residents, home-health service density" — is a direct operationalization of V's specific populations. The instrumentation plan ("EMS access delays, and missed home-health visits") also tracks V's terms.

4. **Confidence and main reason (lines 36–37).** The stated main reason is essentially V verbatim as the decisive factor: delay-hours "omits the severe tail harms (EMS access, care continuity, carless residents)."

5. **Reversal condition interacts with V (lines 22, 38).** The response does not swallow V uncritically — it notes arterials are the precondition for the residential EMS benefit V describes, and the stated flip condition (arterial backbone degrading, "and thus EMS access itself") is framed in V's own currency. This is coherent integration with the response's other reasoning, not a bolt-on.

The response also combines V with an independent marginal-analysis argument (line 23), so V is integrated into a larger structure rather than being the sole crutch — consistent with coherent use rather than mechanical deference.
