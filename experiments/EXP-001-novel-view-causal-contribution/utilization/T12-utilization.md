## Justification

V is not merely present in R07; it is the response's stated pivot and does load-bearing work in at least four places:

1. **Decision reasoning (section "The consideration that decides it," lines 9–14).** The response opens the section with V restated in its own terms ("$40,000 is very likely not enough to replace the roof on a 60-unit building") and then *builds an argument on top of it* rather than repeating it: it derives an asymmetry — $40k on Option A buys an incomplete solution while forfeiting recoverable rent, whereas $40k on Option B buys a complete solution whose cash flow funds the roof later. This asymmetry is the explicit hinge of the recommendation ("a budget that probably can't complete the roof anyway," line 29).

2. **Constraints/action plan (line 22).** V's "getting a firm replacement quote changes the comparison" clause is converted into guardrail #1: "Immediately get 2–3 firm roof replacement quotes," characterized as the highest-value information purchase. The response operationalizes V, not just acknowledges it.

3. **Reversal condition (lines 27 and 39).** The response reasons through V's negation: "if it turns out $40k genuinely covers a full replacement or restoration on this building... the option comparison changes... eliminating the catastrophic tail for the same money is defensible." This appears again verbatim in the stated condition-to-change field (line 39). Reasoning about what happens if V is false is strong evidence of coherent integration rather than parroting.

4. **Main reason and quantitative reasoning (lines 16, 37).** The final "Main reason" cites V directly ("$40k spent on the roof likely buys only a partial fix"), and the 8%-escalation cost comparison is computed against a hypothetical $150k full-replacement figure — a number that only makes sense downstream of V's premise that the true replacement cost far exceeds the budget.

V changes the recommendation's structure (Option B becomes the mechanism that "makes the roof affordable"), shapes the constraints (quotes, reserve, insurance), and defines both directions of the reversal condition. This is the top of the scale.
