# T03 Materiality Ratings

Task: Conference organizer choosing between Venue A (300 cap, $15,000, refundable until 60 days out) and Venue B (450 cap, $22,000, non-refundable, ~$6,000 catering included). Last year: 280 tickets; early-bird selling 30% faster; tickets $150.

Scale: 0 = no plausible decision relevance; 1 = weak/peripheral; 2 = material; 3 = potentially decision-changing.

| ID | Rating | Justification |
|----|--------|---------------|
| K1 | 1 | A negative result — showing break-even fails to differentiate the venues — can slightly sharpen confidence by clearing a distractor, but it removes a consideration rather than moving the recommendation, ranking, or conditions. |
| K2 | 3 | Framing the decision as an asymmetric-loss problem (turned-away demand compounds into defection and poor "sold out" conversion) directly favors Venue B and could flip the top recommendation from the refundable hedge. |
| K3 | 3 | A concrete operational strategy (book refundable A, set a ~90-day sales tripwire, switch to B if projected demand exceeds ~300) supplies both a candidate top recommendation and the explicit condition that would reverse it. |
| K4 | 2 | A 60-day hold or right of first refusal on Venue B removes the hedge strategy's main availability risk, materially strengthening the hedge and adding a substantive condition, without by itself changing which option ranks first. |
| K5 | 3 | Naming the hedge's hidden dependency (B remaining bookable later) and the case where booking B immediately is defensible states exactly the condition most likely to reverse the recommendation, and could change it outright. |
