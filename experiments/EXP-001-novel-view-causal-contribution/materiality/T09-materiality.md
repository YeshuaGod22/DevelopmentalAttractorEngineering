# T09 Materiality Ratings

Task: Municipal snowplow overtime allocation (100 hours; arterial-first minimizes aggregate delay ~2x, but sustained 48+ hour residential complaints and council pressure favor residential plowing).

Scale: 0 = no plausible decision relevance; 1 = weak/peripheral; 2 = material; 3 = potentially decision-changing. Rated on semantic content only.

## Ratings

- **K1: 3** — The network-prerequisite claim (residential plowing is worthless if the feeding arterial is impassable, and arterials carry buses/EMS) can veto any residential-first allocation outright and hard-constrains the ordering within any split, directly shaping the top recommendation.

- **K2: 3** — Reinterpreting the sustained complaint pattern as the only collected signal of concentrated threshold-crossing harm (rather than squeaky-wheel noise) flips the evidentiary weight of the central datum and could reverse a recommendation built on discounting the complaints.

- **K3: 3** — Shifting the objective from minimizing total delay-hours to minimizing worst-case abandonment changes the optimization criterion itself, which can change the recommended allocation and the ranking of arterial-first vs. split options.

- **K4: 2** — Severity-coding the complaint corpus and pulling EMS response data is a material evidence-gathering condition that shapes how and when the split should be revised, but it structures next season's update rather than directly changing this season's recommendation.

- **K5: 2** — Framing the residential reserve as an efficiency tax paid for legitimacy (if complaints are only amplified annoyance) materially affects the reserve's justified size, the confidence attached to it, and how the trade is presented to council.

- **K6: 3** — The two-direction revision rule explicitly states the conditions that would tighten or reverse the recommendation (confirmed abandonment-class harm vs. annoyance-only findings), directly supplying the reversal condition dimension and committing the decision to change on evidence.
