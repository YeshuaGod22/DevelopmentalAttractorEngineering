# T01 Materiality Ratings (blind)

Task: 12-person support team, evening coverage gap. Options: shift three agents to 3pm–11pm (two reluctant but compliant) vs. hire two part-time evening contractors (~cost of one FTE, 6-week onboarding). Evening response 3x daytime; two enterprise complaints.

Scale: 0 = no plausible decision relevance; 1 = weak/peripheral; 2 = material; 3 = potentially decision-changing. Rated on semantic content only.

| View | Rating | Justification |
|------|--------|---------------|
| K1 | 3 | Recasting slow evening response as active enterprise-churn revenue risk raises the cost of the contractor option's 6-week gap and could flip the recommendation toward the immediate shift. |
| K2 | 2 | Establishing the shift as within ordinary authority with agent compliance removes a feasibility/legitimacy objection, materially affecting confidence and option viability, though it does not itself pick a winner. |
| K3 | 3 | If retention of tenured agents (not coverage hours) is the binding constraint, the reluctant-agent shift becomes the riskier option and the recommendation plausibly flips to contractors. |
| K4 | 3 | The reversibility asymmetry (recoverable response times vs. unrecoverable attrition) is a decision rule that directly selects the contractor option and could reverse a shift-first recommendation. |
| K5 | 3 | Viable bridging measures (paid on-call rotation, dated client outreach, morning-first triage) neutralize the contractor option's main weakness — the 6-week gap — potentially changing both the recommendation and its conditions. |
| K6 | 3 | Arguing contractors are worth buying on cost-per-demand grounds regardless of the shift question restructures the option space (e.g., do-both or contractors-first) and can change the top recommendation. |
| K7 | 2 | The underdetermination point (missing renewal dates, tenure, labor-market data) materially affects confidence and pushes toward a conditional or information-gathering answer, but shapes rather than reverses the substantive choice. |
| K8 | 3 | Specifying triggers that would force the permanent shift despite retention cost is precisely a condition-most-likely-to-reverse and could convert a contractor recommendation into a contingent one. |
