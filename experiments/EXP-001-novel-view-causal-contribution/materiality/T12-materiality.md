# T12 Materiality Ratings

Task: 60-unit building, $40,000 for one project — Option A (roof replacement, 2–4 yr life left, 8%/yr cost escalation, failure causes cascading damage) vs. Option B (lobby/hallway renovation, 6 vacancies at $7,200/mo lost rent, comparables at 95% occupancy).

Scale: 0 = no plausible decision relevance; 1 = weak/peripheral; 2 = material; 3 = potentially decision-changing.

## K1 — Rating: 2
The observation that each option's case silently treats the other's key unknown (roof-failure probability vs. vacancy-conversion rate) as benign is materially relevant: taken seriously, it blocks a clean dominance claim, lowers stated confidence, and forces the recommendation to carry explicit conditions on both unknowns (e.g., what roof-failure odds or conversion rate would flip the choice) — though it supplies no new evidence that would by itself reorder the options.

## K2 — Rating: 2
Framing the (implied lobby-first) plan as an explicit bet that accepts roughly two years of elevated roof risk directly shapes the stated reversal condition (early roof failure) and would add substantive constraints — e.g., interim roof inspection/monitoring, a contingency reserve, or a hard trigger to redirect funds — but it characterizes and hedges the chosen plan rather than plausibly overturning which option is ranked first.
