# T02 Materiality Ratings

Task: build-vs-buy decision — finish custom internal reporting tool (6 weeks left, expert on trickiest component leaving in 8 weeks) vs. switch to vendor product covering ~90% for $400/month, with a revenue-costing feature backlog.

Scale: 0 = no plausible decision relevance, 1 = weak/peripheral, 2 = material, 3 = potentially decision-changing. Rated on semantic content only.

## K1 — Rating: 3
The claim that the missing 10% is disproportionately likely to contain the tool's raison d'être (and to coincide with the trickiest component) directly attacks the naive "90% coverage is nearly complete coverage" reading, which could flip the option ranking or become the stated condition most likely to reverse a switch recommendation.

## K2 — Rating: 3
The inversion — that an essential gap plus the expiring 8-week expert window makes "finish now" the only irreversible-option-preserving choice while the vendor remains available indefinitely — is a decision-theoretic asymmetry that could change the top recommendation outright or define the reversal condition.

## K3 — Rating: 2
Mandating knowledge capture from the departing expert under either choice is a substantive constraint/condition that materially hedges both options, but it does not by itself change which option is recommended or how they rank.

## K4 — Rating: 2
Reversing the direction of the backlog-pressure argument (from "switch to free the team" to "finish fast now, since later reimplementation is open-ended without the expert") could plausibly shift confidence or ranking under the essential-gap frame, though it is largely a corollary of K2 rather than an independent decision driver.
