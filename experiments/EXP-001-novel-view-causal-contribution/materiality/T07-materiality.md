# T07 Materiality Ratings (blind)

Task: Overwhelmed OSS maintainers — Option A (issue bankruptcy + triage rota) vs Option B (promote three <1-year contributors to maintainer; security-sensitive environments). Question: which first?

Scale: 0 = no plausible decision relevance, 1 = weak/peripheral, 2 = material, 3 = potentially decision-changing. Rated on semantic content only.

## Ratings

- K1: **2** — Articulates concrete benefits of Option A (attention, morale, contributor signal) that could raise A's ranking or the confidence behind an A-first recommendation, but these are the standard, largely already-implicit case for A rather than a consideration that would flip the decision by itself.
- K2: **3** — Directly attacks Option A's sufficiency by tying it to the stated causal driver of attrition (six-week review latency, which only more reviewers fixes), so taking it seriously plausibly demotes A from first choice or converts an A-first answer into A-only-with-a-binding-commitment-to-B.
- K3: **3** — Reframing the core pathology as bus-factor/succession risk (all trust through two exhausted people) changes what the decision is optimizing for and strongly favors B-first, so it can change the top recommendation, not just its confidence.
- K4: **3** — Inverts the main objection to Option B: if the xz-style attack surface comes from maintainer overload plus the absence of a graduated trust path, the security concern argues FOR structured promotion rather than against it, neutralizing the chief reason to prefer A-first — potentially decision-changing.
- K5: **2** — The explicit residual-risk trade (a patient adversary passes graduated trust too, accepted against near-certain collapse risk) materially shapes stated confidence and the conditions/constraints attached to a B-first recommendation, but it refines rather than reverses the choice.
- K6: **3** — States a concrete reversal condition (hard compliance requirements for multi-year verified maintainer history, or a specific integrity doubt about a candidate) that would flip the ordering to A-first; this is squarely the "condition most likely to reverse the recommendation" category.
