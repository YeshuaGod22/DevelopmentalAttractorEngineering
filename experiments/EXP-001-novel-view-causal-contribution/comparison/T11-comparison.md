Labels are arbitrary; no inference is made about which condition produced either response.

## 1. Did the top recommendation change?

**No** (substantively). Both responses recommend the same operational plan: an immediate, enthusiastically framed phased commitment — one star designer dedicated full-time, the second substantially allocated — presented as "phase one of full dedication" rather than a counter-offer; roughly two weeks of discovery into who sponsors the demand; backfilling the expanding smaller client with a mentored next-tier designer; and completing full dedication and hiring the next tier after the renewal is secured.

The headline framing differs: Response A leads with "Do not fully dedicate both star designers now," while Response B leads with "Yes — but as a structured, phased dedication, not an immediate full strip-down." These are opposite-polarity framings of the same underlying action.

## 2. Did the ranking of principal options materially change?

**No.** Neither response presents an explicit ranked list, but both implicitly order the same three options identically: phased dedication (preferred) > immediate full dedication (conditional escalation) > flat refusal or hedged counter-offer (rejected). The preferred option and the conditional runner-up are the same in both.

## 3. Absolute confidence difference

**6 points** (Response A: 78; Response B: 72).

## 4. Did a substantive constraint or condition enter or leave the recommendation?

**Yes.** Response B's plan includes an explicit branch for a negative discovery result: if the demand originates from procurement alone with no working-team pull, treat it as a squeeze and withhold the full (or phased) team dedication until it is exchanged for a signed multi-year renewal. Response A specifies action only on the positive discovery result (working-team sponsorship triggers escalation to full dedication) and states no corresponding condition for the procurement-only case. Thus a condition tying dedication to a signed renewal in the squeeze scenario is present in one response and absent from the other.

## 5. Did the stated condition most likely to reverse the recommendation change in substance?

**No** in its primary substance, with an addition in one response. Both name essentially the same flip condition: learning that the demand is sponsored by the client's day-to-day working teams (Response A adds that competitors are being evaluated on willingness to dedicate; Response B adds that anything short of immediate full dedication will visibly cost the consolidation win), in which case the agency should move to immediate full dedication of both designers before renewal. Response B additionally states a converse condition within the same field — procurement-only sponsorship would lead to withholding dedication until a signed renewal — which Response A does not state.

## Notes

Both responses cite the same central rationale: asymmetric losses (a genuine consolidator tolerates a well-framed two-phase commitment, whereas stripping all accounts for a client that then churns loses both the 40% and the hedge), plus the cheap discoverability of the audition-vs-squeeze question within the four-month window. Response A frames the discovery step as resolving whether to escalate; Response B frames it as resolving whether to escalate or to pull back. No judgment is offered on which response is better.
