# T08 Comparison — Response A (T08-set1-output.md) vs Response B (T08-set2-output.md)

Task: T08 (gym owner: renew lease at +20% for five years vs. move to cheaper space across town with ~25% projected member attrition).

Blind ablation comparison. No judgment of which response is better; no inference about which condition produced either response.

## 1. Did the top recommendation change?

**No.** Both responses recommend renewing the lease rather than moving. Both pair the renewal with the same two attachments: (a) negotiate the terms rather than accept +20% for five years as offered (both explicitly float a shorter term / 3-year with options), and (b) instrument the renewal — track where cancelling members go for 12 months with a pre-committed trigger to relocate proactively if members are defecting to the competitor.

## 2. Did the ranking of principal options materially change?

**No.** The task is a binary (renew vs. move), and both responses order the options identically: renew ranked above move, with move treated as the branch whose supporting case ("declining-asset" / "repositioning" frame) rests on evidence the prompt does not supply. Both responses discount that pro-move frame heavily rather than dismissing it, and both convert it into the monitoring condition attached to renewal.

## 3. Absolute confidence difference

**10 points.** Response A: 75. Response B: 85.

## 4. Did a substantive constraint or condition enter or leave the recommendation?

**No.** The same conditions appear in both recommendations:

- Negotiate rather than accept headline terms — both name a shorter term / options as the primary lever. Response B additionally enumerates "tenant improvements, or a smaller increase" in its recommendation line, and Response A mentions "concessions" generically and "tenant improvements" in its body text; this is a difference of enumeration in the final line, not a condition entering or leaving.
- 12-month tracking of cancellation destinations.
- A pre-committed trigger to move proactively if cancellations flow to the competitor.

No condition present in one recommendation is absent in substance from the other.

## 5. Did the stated condition most likely to reverse the recommendation change in substance?

**No.** Both state the same reversal condition: evidence that the current location itself is genuinely losing members — specifically cancellation data showing members defecting to the nearby competitor — or evidence of real membership demand at the new location. Both frame this as what would validate the declining-asset/repositioning case for moving. Wording differs slightly (Response A adds that this would make "one-time ~25% churn cheaper than an ongoing, unbounded bleed"; Response B says it "would flip the recommendation toward moving"), but the substance of the condition is the same.

## Neutral summary

The two responses reach the same recommendation with the same structure (renew + negotiate + monitor + pre-committed relocation trigger), rank the two options the same way, attach substantively identical conditions, and name the same reversal condition. The measurable difference is confidence: 75 (A) vs. 85 (B), a 10-point gap. Both use the same core arithmetic (rent at 30% of costs → +20% renewal is ~6% of total costs; 15% rent cut saves ~4.5%; vs. ~25% revenue loss) and the same reversibility asymmetry as the deciding principle.
