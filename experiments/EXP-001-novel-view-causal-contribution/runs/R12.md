# R12 — Spare Aircraft Allocation: Route 1 vs Route 2

## Situation
One spare aircraft, one night, two disrupted routes:
- **Route 1:** 180 passengers (mostly leisure). If not flown tonight: rebooked tomorrow morning; hotel vouchers ~$25,000.
- **Route 2:** 90 passengers, 45 of whom connect to an international departure tonight with **no rebooking availability for three days**.

## Perspectives

### 1. Direct cost minimization
Route 1's downside is known and bounded: ~$25,000 in hotels plus meal vouchers and a 12-hour delay, with a confirmed recovery path tomorrow morning. Route 2's downside is larger and open-ended: 45 stranded connectors × 3 nights of hotels and meals easily approaches or exceeds $25k on its own, before adding delay compensation obligations (EU261-style regimes for international itineraries can run €600/passenger — potentially ~$27k for the connectors alone), interline/other-carrier reaccommodation at walk-up fares, and rebooking labor. On expected cost, Route 2 is the more expensive failure. **Favors Route 2.**

### 2. Utilitarian headcount
180 people inconvenienced vs 90. If each passenger's disruption counted equally, Route 1 wins 2:1. But the harms are not equal: Route 1 passengers lose one night with a guaranteed morning recovery; 45 Route 2 passengers lose three days of an international trip — missed cruises, weddings, work obligations, visa windows. Severity-weighted, the calculus flips: 45 × (3-day stranding) outweighs 180 × (one comfortable overnight delay). **Weakly favors Route 1 on raw count; favors Route 2 once severity is weighted.**

### 3. Worst-case harm (maximin)
Ask: which single passenger is worst off under each choice? Under "fly Route 1," the worst-off person is stranded three days mid-itinerary abroad or pre-departure — a genuinely severe outcome. Under "fly Route 2," the worst-off person spends a night in a paid hotel and flies at 8 a.m. Minimizing the maximum harm clearly protects the connectors. **Favors Route 2.**

### 4. Brand, loyalty, and complaint risk
Leisure travelers delayed overnight with prompt vouchers and a morning flight generate grumbling but little lasting damage — this is a well-understood, well-compensated disruption. Forty-five people stranded three days on an international itinerary generate viral complaints, DOT/regulator filings, and permanent defections; these are also disproportionately the passengers with the most complex, high-value itineraries. Reputationally, the failure modes are asymmetric. **Favors Route 2.**

### 5. Network and operational recovery
Where does the spare aircraft need to be tomorrow? Flying Route 1 repositions a (presumably larger-demand) aircraft and clears 180 seats from tomorrow morning's rebooking load; flying Route 2 leaves 180 people competing for tomorrow-morning seats that must actually exist. The planner should verify Route 1's "next available rebooking tomorrow morning" is a firm, protected recovery (dedicated aircraft or confirmed space), not just hopeful seat-finding across a full schedule. Also verify the spare can legally/physically operate the chosen route (crew duty time, range, gauge — a 90-seat problem may fit the spare better than 180 pax do). **Mixed; mostly a set of preconditions rather than a decider.**

### 6. Commitment asymmetry / recoverability
The core principle: allocate the scarce resource to the disruption that is *irreversible tonight*. Route 1's problem is recoverable through ordinary means (money and tomorrow's schedule). Route 2's connection is a closing door — once the international flight leaves, no amount of money buys the outcome back for three days. Scarce capacity should go where substitutes don't exist. **Favors Route 2.**

## Recommendation
Send the spare aircraft to **Route 2**, provided (a) the aircraft can legally and physically operate it in time to make the international connection, and (b) Route 1's morning rebooking is firm. Simultaneously: issue Route 1 hotel/meal vouchers immediately and protect their morning departure; for any Route 2 connectors who would still misconnect, attempt same-night interline protection on other carriers.

The $25,000 hotel bill for Route 1 is the cheap, reversible arm of this decision. The 45 connectors are the irreversible arm: their harm is severe, three days long, likely more expensive in total (duty-of-care + compensation + reaccommodation), and reputationally the worse failure. Headcount favors Route 1 only if all disruptions are weighted equally, which they are not.

Recommendation: Give the spare aircraft to Route 2 tonight; rebook Route 1's 180 passengers tomorrow morning with hotel vouchers issued immediately.
