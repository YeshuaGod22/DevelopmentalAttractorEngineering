# R37

## Initial view

Schedule Feature X (offline mode). Three things point that way:

1. **The churn math dominates.** 4% monthly churn compounds to roughly 39% annualized loss of the paying base. With new-customer growth already slowing, the business is a leaky bucket being filled by a weakening tap. A retention fix multiplies the value of every future acquisition win; an acquisition fix pours more water into the same leak.

2. **Evidence quality is asymmetric.** The case for X rests on direct, structured data from people who actually paid and then left — 30% of cancellation surveys name it. The case for Y rests on sales-team attribution of prospect losses, which is systematically biased: "they went with the competitor because we lack collab" is the most face-saving and most available story a salesperson can tell, and prospects themselves often name a concrete missing feature to end the conversation politely. Same effort, but X has lower execution risk and higher evidential confidence.

3. **A caveat against overclaiming for X:** appearing in 30% of cancellation surveys does not mean shipping offline mode saves 30% of churners. Stated reasons overstate causal contribution — some of those churners had multiple reasons and would leave anyway. A realistic win might be cutting monthly churn from 4% to 3–3.3%. That is still a large LTV improvement, but it should be priced honestly.

## A materially different frame

**Frame: the churn is a lagging symptom; the slowing growth is the leading signal of a market that is moving to collaborative note-taking, and the cancellation survey is measuring the past, not the future.**

Under this frame, the 30% who cite offline mode are the articulate tail of a legacy segment — solo power-users — and the 70% of churners who *don't* cite it are the interesting population. People rarely write "your product isn't where my team works" in a cancellation survey; they just drift to the tool their collaborators use and give a mundane reason or none. Meanwhile the prospect-loss data, noisy as it is, is the only signal in the dataset about where *new* demand is going, and it points the same direction the growth slowdown does: two quarters of decelerating acquisition is exactly what you'd expect if collaboration has become table stakes in the category.

**What follows if this frame is right:** Feature X is a well-evidenced investment in a shrinking segment — it would measurably reduce this year's churn while the company's addressable position erodes. The correct move is Y despite the technical risk, because the risk of Y is an engineering problem you can manage (spike it, cut scope, ship a v1), while the risk of skipping Y is a strategic position you cannot buy back once network effects settle around the competitor. Collaboration features also change churn dynamics themselves: multi-user accounts churn far less than solo accounts, so Y is secretly a retention feature too — just one that never shows up in a cancellation survey. Under this frame, choosing X because its evidence is cleaner is streetlight-effect reasoning: the survey measures what departing solo users say, not what the market is doing.

## Final recommendation

I recommend X, but not comfortably, and the discomfort is load-bearing.

The deciding factor is evidence quality under symmetric cost: both features cost a quarter, but only X's payoff is grounded in direct behavioral data from paying customers, and only X has bounded execution risk. The alternative frame is genuinely live — the two-quarter growth slowdown is real and unexplained, and the collab-frame's account of it is at least as plausible as "marketing funnel noise." But acting on that frame today means betting a full quarter plus technical risk on sales anecdotes. That's a bet worth *preparing*, not yet worth *placing*.

So: schedule X for the quarter, and spend a small, protected slice of capacity (one engineer, PM time) doing the work that would confirm or kill the collab frame — structured win/loss interviews with lost prospects conducted by someone other than sales, cohort analysis of whether churned users resurface at collaborative competitors, and a technical spike that de-risks Y's architecture. If the frame confirms, Y is next quarter's obvious choice, de-risked. If X ships and churn doesn't move by early next quarter, that is additional evidence the survey was measuring stated rather than causal reasons — and the collab frame gains weight.

The unresolved tension I am carrying, not dissolving: X is the better-evidenced decision, and the evidence itself may be systematically blind to the thing that kills the company. The recommendation optimizes for decision quality given current information while explicitly funding the collection of the information that would overturn it.

Recommendation: Schedule Feature X (offline mode) this quarter, with a protected side-effort (validated win/loss research + technical spike) that de-risks and evaluates Feature Y for next quarter.
