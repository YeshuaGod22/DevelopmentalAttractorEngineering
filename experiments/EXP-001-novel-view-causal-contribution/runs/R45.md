# R45 — Roof vs. Lobby: One Project, $40,000

## Framing

This is not "risk vs. revenue" in the abstract. Both options are quantifiable, and the key asymmetry is timing: the roof is a cost that grows slowly and predictably (with a tail risk), while the vacancies are a large, certain loss compounding every month right now.

## The numbers

**Cost of the vacancy problem (Option B addresses):**
- 6 vacant units × $1,200/unit = $7,200/month = **$86,400/year** in lost rent.
- Comparable renovated buildings run 95% occupancy → for 60 units that's ~3 vacancies. Closing the gap from 6 to 3 vacancies recovers ~$3,600/month = **~$43,200/year**.
- At that rate, the $40,000 renovation pays for itself in roughly **11 months**. Even a weak result (filling just 2 of the 6 units) recovers ~$28,800/year — still a strong return.
- Direct market evidence exists: two prospective tenants explicitly cited the lobby/hallways when declining. This is unusually clean causal signal that the common areas are suppressing demand.

**Cost of deferring the roof (Option A addresses):**
- 2–4 years of remaining life means the roof does not need to be replaced *this* year by the contractor's own estimate.
- Cost escalation at 8%/year on a ~$40,000 replacement ≈ **$3,200 more if done next year**, ~$6,700 more if done in two years.
- The real exposure is the tail risk: a failure causes damage well beyond the roof (interiors, tenant property, possible liability, and — ironically — more vacancies). That risk is low in year 1 of a 2–4 year window but grows each year.

## The comparison

Deferring the lobby costs ~$43,000/year in recoverable rent, forever, until fixed. Deferring the roof one year costs ~$3,200 in escalation plus a small (but nonzero) failure probability at the early end of the contractor's window. The renovation's recovered rent (~$43k/year) is itself the funding mechanism for the roof: doing B first makes A affordable next year; doing A first leaves the vacancy bleed untouched and no new cash flow to show for it.

## Recommendation and conditions

Fund **Option B (lobby and hallway renovation)** this year, and pair it with a firm plan for the roof:

1. **Commit now to replacing the roof next budget year**, funded substantially by recovered rent. That lands within the contractor's 2–4 year window with margin.
2. **Get a second roof inspection** (a second opinion on remaining life) and budget a small reserve — a few thousand dollars — for interim inspection, patching, and gutter/flashing maintenance to hold the roof safely through the deferral.
3. **Verify the demand story before committing**: confirm with the leasing agent that units are being shown and declined (an aesthetics problem) rather than not drawing applicants at all (a pricing/market problem). Two tenant citations plus the 95% comp figure support the aesthetics story, but it's worth a week of diligence.

The main caveat: "2–4 years" is an estimate, and roof failure is the one outcome here that can destroy value rather than merely delay it. That's why the recommendation is B *with a hard roof commitment*, not B instead of A.
