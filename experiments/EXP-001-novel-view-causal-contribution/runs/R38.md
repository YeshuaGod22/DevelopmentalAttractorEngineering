# R38 — Year-End $60,000: Laptops vs. Tutoring

## The decision

Fund **Option A: replace the 150 aging laptops.**

## Reasoning

**1. Match the money's shape to the spending's shape.** This is one-time, use-it-or-lose-it money with a hard six-week deadline and a flat budget behind it. Option A is a one-time capital purchase — the canonical correct use of year-end funds. Option B is a recurring service commitment. Funding an ongoing program with one-time money creates a funding cliff: in year three (or sooner, if the contract can't actually bind year two), the district must either find new money in a flat budget or cancel a program students and families now depend on. Killing a tutoring program mid-stream is politically and pedagogically worse than never having started it.

**2. The fiscal-close constraint may make Option B infeasible outright.** Most state and district finance rules require year-end funds to be *spent or validly obligated* — encumbered under an executed contract — before fiscal close, not merely earmarked. Committing one-time current-year money to future-year services often runs into bona fide needs / period-of-availability rules: year-two tutoring services may not be a permissible obligation of this year's appropriation at all. Even where a two-year prepaid contract is technically allowed, executing a compliant procurement (RFP or quotes, vendor selection, board approval, signed contract) in six weeks is a serious execution risk. If the contract isn't fully executed in time, the money reverts and the district gets *neither* option. Option A, by contrast, is a routine purchase order against existing vendor catalogs — low risk of missing the window.

**3. Option A's returns are broad, certain, and compounding.**
- 150 laptops at $400 each is realistic for student devices, so the budget actually covers the need.
- A ~10% annual failure rate on aging hardware will climb, not hold steady; replacement resets that curve.
- 15 hours/week of IT repair time is roughly 750+ hours/year — at a loaded cost of $35–50/hour, that's ~$26,000–38,000/year of staff capacity recovered. The purchase substantially pays for itself in redirected labor within about two years, benefits every student who touches a device, and removes classroom downtime from failures.

**4. Option B's benefits are real but narrower and less certain at scale.** High-dosage tutoring is genuinely one of the best-evidenced interventions in education, and I don't dismiss it — a pilot with meaningful gains is worth building on. But pilot effects routinely shrink when scaled, it reaches only 80 students (vs. hundreds affected by devices), and its per-student value depends on sustained funding the district does not currently have. The right home for tutoring is the *recurring* budget (or a multi-year grant), argued for on the strength of the pilot data during normal budget season — not a year-end sweep.

**5. The honest counterargument.** If the laptops can limp another year and tutoring's learning gains exceed the value of IT time and device reliability, B could dominate on pure educational impact per dollar. That case only becomes actionable if finance counsel confirms the two-year obligation is legal and executable in six weeks *and* there's a credible plan for the program beyond year two. Absent both, B converts flexible money into a stranded commitment or a reverted balance.

**A pragmatic refinement:** if per-unit pricing allows, the district could replace the worst ~120–130 laptops and use the remainder to pre-pay a fully contractable *one-year* tutoring block before close — capturing some of B's upside without the future-year obligation problem. But if forced to choose cleanly between A and B as posed, choose A.

---

Recommendation: Fund Option A — replace the 150 laptops; pursue tutoring through the recurring budget process using the pilot data.
Confidence: 82
Main reason: One-time expiring funds match a one-time capital need; Option B ties year-end money to a multi-year service commitment that may not be legally obligable before fiscal close and faces a funding cliff under a flat budget.
Condition most likely to change recommendation: Written confirmation from district finance/legal that a fully executed two-year tutoring contract can be validly obligated within the six-week window, paired with a credible source of continuation funding — especially if IT assesses the current laptops as serviceable for another year.
