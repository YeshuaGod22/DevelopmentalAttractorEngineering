# R26 — Spare Aircraft Allocation

## The question
One spare aircraft, two disrupted routes, one flight tonight. Route 1: 180 leisure passengers, rebookable tomorrow morning, ~$25,000 in hotel vouchers. Route 2: 90 passengers, 45 of whom connect to an international departure tonight with no rebooking for three days.

## Analysis

The decision hinges on the difference between a recoverable disruption and an unrecoverable one.

**Route 1's disruption is bounded and priced.** Every one of the 180 passengers has a known recovery path: tomorrow morning's flight. The harm is one uncomfortable night, and the airline can largely buy it down — the $25,000 in vouchers is real money but small against the alternative, and the delay cost per passenger is a night, not a trip.

**Route 2's disruption is unbounded for half its passengers.** The 45 connecting passengers face a three-day strand — for many international itineraries that is not a delayed trip, it is a destroyed one: missed weddings, cruises, business obligations, visa windows. Three days of hotels, meals, and rebooking for 45 stranded international passengers will likely cost more than $25,000 by itself, before counting regulatory compensation exposure (EU261-style claims, DOT requirements on international itineraries), goodwill payments, and the disproportionate reputational damage of a multi-day strand versus an overnight delay. The other 45 passengers on Route 2 also get to their destination tonight rather than joining a rebooking queue.

**The asymmetry test:** if the aircraft goes to Route 1, the worst outcome anywhere in the system is a 3-day strand for 45 people. If it goes to Route 2, the worst outcome is a 12-hour overnight delay for 180 people with a guaranteed seat in the morning. Minimizing maximum harm and minimizing total expected cost point the same direction.

Raw headcount (180 vs 90) favors Route 1 only if all delay-hours are weighted equally. They are not: passenger-harm scales super-linearly with delay length, and a delay with no recovery path is categorically worse than one with a next-morning fix.

## Recommendation

Send the spare aircraft to **Route 2** tonight. For Route 1: issue the hotel vouchers immediately, confirm every passenger onto tomorrow morning's flight before they leave the airport, and communicate proactively — a well-handled overnight delay is a recoverable customer experience.

Before executing, spend thirty minutes trying to protect the 45 international connections another way (interline/partner rebooking onto tonight's other departures, or holding the international flight briefly if it is the airline's own metal). If the connections can be protected without the spare aircraft, the calculus flips.

Recommendation: Assign the spare aircraft to Route 2; rebook Route 1 to tomorrow morning with hotel vouchers.

Condition most likely to change recommendation: If the 45 international connections can be protected another way — partner-carrier or interline rebooking tonight, or holding the international departure — Route 2's urgency collapses and the aircraft should go to Route 1's 180 passengers.
