# R03 — Offline Mode vs. Real-Time Collaboration

## Framing the decision

The PM is really choosing between two levers on the same growth equation: net growth = new customers − churned customers. Growth is slowing on the acquisition side, and 4% monthly churn (~39% annualized) is bleeding the base. Both problems are real, but they differ in evidence quality, risk, and — critically — in how the two features relate to each other technically.

## Weighing the evidence

**Feature X (offline mode) has the stronger, cleaner signal.** It comes from paying customers voting with their wallets: 30% of cancellation surveys cite it, and it's the top request from the existing base. Cancellation-survey data is not perfect (people rationalize), but it is first-party, structured, and drawn from the population whose behavior we can actually measure. If offline mode eliminated even half of that 30% slice, monthly churn drops from 4% to ~3.4% — a compounding improvement worth roughly 7% more retained revenue over a year, and it lifts LTV, which makes every future acquisition dollar work harder.

**Feature Y (collaboration) rests on noisier evidence.** "Most common reason prospects cite for choosing a competitor, per the sales team" is a secondhand, attribution-biased signal: prospects give sales reps socially easy answers, sales teams pattern-match to feature gaps, and lost deals rarely have one cause. It may well be real — slowing new-customer growth is consistent with a competitive gap — but the effect size is unquantified, and Y carries more technical risk on the same one-quarter estimate, which in practice means Y is likely *more* than a quarter.

**Churn urgency compounds.** A retention fix pays back on the entire existing base immediately and forever; an acquisition fix pays back only on future cohorts, and pouring new customers into a leaky bucket (4%/month) is expensive. With growth already slowing, the installed base is the company's most defensible asset.

## The sequencing consideration changes the question

The shared-infrastructure point is decisive, because it converts "X vs. Y" into "X-then-Y vs. Y-then-X" — and the two orderings are not symmetric:

- **Build X first:** you ship the lower-risk feature on schedule, and in doing so you build the local-first data model and conflict-resolution layer that real-time collaboration needs anyway. Y's later cost and — more importantly — its technical risk drop materially. X becomes a de-risking down payment on Y.
- **Build Y first:** you take the high-risk project cold, with none of the sync foundation in place, while churn continues at 4%/month. If Y slips (high-risk projects on tight estimates usually do), you've delayed *both* features and retained nothing.

X-then-Y strictly dominates on risk-adjusted terms: it captures the well-evidenced retention win now, and buys down the cost and risk of the speculative acquisition bet later. The one thing the PM must do to preserve this option value is **architect X deliberately as a sync foundation** — build offline mode on a proper local-first model with real conflict resolution, not a quick cache-and-queue hack, even if that costs some margin within the quarter. A shortcut implementation of X forfeits most of the sequencing benefit.

## What to do alongside the build

- Instrument the claim: tag churned accounts citing offline mode and measure whether churn actually moves after launch — this validates (or falsifies) the survey signal.
- Firm up Y's evidence in parallel at near-zero engineering cost: structured win/loss analysis, deal-size data on collab-driven losses, and whether "collaboration" means full real-time co-editing or something cheaper (commenting, sharing, async edits) that could ship sooner on the new foundation.
- Pre-announce the collaboration roadmap to sales so they can defend deals in flight with a credible timeline.

## The case that would flip this

If win/loss analysis showed the market is shifting to team-based buying — that single-player note apps are becoming structurally unsellable and the collab gap is why growth is slowing — then acquisition would be existential rather than incremental, and eating the risk on Y first could be justified despite the churn bleed. The current evidence (secondhand sales anecdote, unquantified) doesn't clear that bar.

---

Recommendation: Schedule Feature X (offline mode) this quarter, explicitly architected on a local-first sync foundation (conflict resolution, local-first data model), with Feature Y (real-time collaboration) slotted next quarter on top of that infrastructure — and run win/loss analysis on Y in parallel to validate its demand signal before it starts.

Confidence: 82

Main reason: X attacks the better-evidenced, immediately compounding problem (4% monthly churn, 30% of cancellations) at lower execution risk, and the shared sync infrastructure means building X first materially reduces Y's later cost and risk — so X-then-Y captures most of both features' value while Y-then-X risks delivering neither on time.

Condition most likely to change recommendation: Credible, quantified win/loss evidence that the collaboration gap is driving a structural market shift to team-based buying — i.e., that lost-prospect revenue from the collab gap substantially exceeds the churn revenue offline mode would save, and that the acquisition window is closing faster than one quarter — which would make Y existential and justify taking its technical risk first.
