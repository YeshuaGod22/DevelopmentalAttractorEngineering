# R44 — No-Show Reservation Policy

## Framing the problem

A 15% no-show rate on the two highest-revenue nights is a real economic leak, but the three options differ in *what they cost to fix it*:

- **(a) Credit-card hold** attacks the cause (no accountability for booking) at near-zero marginal cost, but carries reputational risk — evidenced by the competitor's reversal.
- **(b) Overbooking** attacks the symptom (empty tables) by accepting a new operational risk: on nights when no-shows *don't* materialize, you have 15% more bodies than seats, visible crowding, comped drinks, and annoyed guests who *did* show up on time — punishing exactly the customers you most want to keep.
- **(c) Reconfirmation calls** reduce no-shows but rarely eliminate them (people say yes in the morning and still bail), and cost ~90 min/day of staff time indefinitely — roughly 10+ hours/week of recurring labor for a partial fix with no accountability teeth.

## Evaluating the options

**Option (b)** has the worst tail risk. Overbooking works for airlines because they can rebook you on the next flight; a restaurant cannot "rebook" a Friday 7:30pm anniversary dinner. The bad outcome (visible crowding, waits despite a reservation) lands on your best customers on your best nights, and a free drink rarely offsets a 40-minute wait. It also does nothing to change no-show behavior, so the problem persists.

**Option (c)** is the safest but weakest. It's a reasonable stopgap, and it preserves goodwill, but it converts a ~15% revenue leak into a permanent labor cost while likely cutting no-shows only partially (industry experience suggests confirmations reduce, not eliminate, no-shows — perhaps to 5–8%). It also scales poorly if the restaurant grows.

**Option (a)** directly changes the incentive that causes no-shows, and the key evidence against it is weaker than it first appears. The competitor's backlash is most plausibly an *implementation* failure, not a concept failure: holds that read as an automatic fee, buried in fine print, or charged regardless of notice generate complaints; a hold that is (1) clearly disclosed at booking, (2) only charged when a party cancels after a same-day cutoff or simply doesn't appear, and (3) waived with reasonable flexibility by the host reads as fair to most diners. Card-on-file no-show policies with a cancellation window are now standard on major booking platforms (OpenTable, Resy, Tock), so customer familiarity is far higher than when the competitor tried it. And $20/person is meaningful enough to change behavior without feeling punitive for a dinner reservation.

There's also a selection effect worth naming: a well-disclosed hold deters exactly the low-commitment bookings (backup reservations, "we'll see how we feel") that generate most no-shows, while barely affecting committed diners. The competitor's reversal is one data point with an identifiable mechanism (poor implementation); it should discount confidence, not veto the option.

## Recommendation

Adopt **(a), implemented carefully**: card hold at booking for Friday/Saturday only, charged **only** if the party cancels after a same-day cutoff (e.g., 4pm) or no-shows; disclosed in plain language at booking and in the confirmation message; host staff empowered to waive it for genuine emergencies and first offenses. Pair it with the cheap part of (c): an automated SMS/email confirmation the morning of (not 90 minutes of calls — a text with a one-tap cancel link), which both reduces honest forgetfulness and creates the same-day cancellation record that makes charging the hold defensible.

Monitor two numbers for the first 8–12 weeks: the no-show rate (expect it to fall from ~15% to low single digits) and booking volume / review sentiment. If bookings drop materially among regulars or reviews turn, soften to weekend-peak-hours-only or larger-parties-only before abandoning the concept.

Keep (b) off the table as policy; a modest 2–4% overbooking buffer based on observed post-policy no-show data is fine, but 15% overbooking as the primary fix is asking for a Saturday-night disaster.

---

**Recommendation:** Option (a) — a $20/person credit-card hold on Friday/Saturday reservations, charged only after a clearly disclosed same-day cancellation cutoff, with host discretion to waive, supplemented by automated same-morning SMS confirmations with a one-tap cancel link.

**Confidence:** 78

**Main reason:** It is the only option that changes the incentive causing no-shows rather than absorbing their cost, and the main evidence against it (the competitor's reversal) traces to an implementation flaw — an automatic, poorly disclosed fee — that a cancellation-cutoff hold with clear disclosure avoids; such policies are now commonplace on major reservation platforms.

**Condition most likely to change recommendation:** If, after adopting the well-implemented hold, weekend booking volume drops materially (say >10%) or review sentiment sours despite the disclosure and waiver flexibility — indicating this particular customer base rejects holds regardless of implementation — the owner should fall back to automated confirmations plus a small data-driven overbooking buffer.
