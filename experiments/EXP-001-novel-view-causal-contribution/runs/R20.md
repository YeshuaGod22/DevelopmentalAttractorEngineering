# R20 — Evening Coverage Decision

## The situation, restated

- 12 agents cover 8am–8pm in two overlapping shifts.
- 40% of ticket volume arrives 6pm–11pm, but only 2 agents work past 6pm — and the 8pm–11pm window has **zero** coverage at all.
- Evening response times are 3x daytime, and two enterprise clients have already complained.
- Option A: move three agents to 3pm–11pm (immediate; free; 2 of 3 would rather not, but would comply).
- Option B: hire two part-time evening contractors at ~the cost of one FTE (cheap, durable, morale-safe; 6 weeks before they're effective).

## Analysis

**The clock matters more than anything else here.** Enterprise complaints are a leading indicator of churn, and enterprise accounts are usually where the revenue concentration is. Option B alone means at least six more weeks of tripled evening response times for clients who have already escalated — that is the highest-cost path even though its payroll line is the smallest.

**The two options are not actually exclusive, and they solve different problems.** The shift change is an immediate, zero-cost, fully reversible fix. The contractors are the sustainable steady-state answer — they add capacity rather than rearranging it (note that Option A thins daytime coverage from 12 to 9 during hours that still carry 60% of volume), and they don't ask two people to permanently work a schedule they dislike. Neither option alone is complete: A fixes the emergency but creates a slow morale/attrition leak; B fixes the structure but leaves the emergency burning for six weeks.

**Morale risk is real but manageable.** "Would comply" is not "wants to," and forced schedule changes are a classic driver of support-team attrition — replacing even one experienced agent would cost far more than the contractor budget. The mitigations: ask for volunteers across all 12 before assigning anyone, attach an evening differential or comp perk, and — critically — give the reassignment an explicit end date tied to contractor ramp-up. A hard 6–8 week commitment with a named exit is a very different ask than an open-ended schedule change.

**A sizing check favors doing both.** Roughly 40% of volume in the evening argues for roughly 4–5 evening-capable heads, not 2. Three shifted agents ≈ adequate bridge; two part-timers plus one willing shifted agent ≈ adequate steady state.

## Recommendation

Do both, sequenced: **immediately move three agents to 3pm–11pm as an explicitly temporary bridge (volunteers first, evening differential, committed end date), and simultaneously start hiring the two evening contractors.** When the contractors are effective (~week 6), return the reluctant agents to their preferred schedule, keeping any shifted agent who wants to stay on evenings. Call the two enterprise clients this week to tell them evening coverage changes are already live — the save is as much about visible responsiveness as about the metric.

If the team lead truly must pick only one option, pick the shift change: it is the only move that addresses the active enterprise-churn risk on a relevant timescale, and it is reversible, whereas six more weeks of 3x response times may not be.
