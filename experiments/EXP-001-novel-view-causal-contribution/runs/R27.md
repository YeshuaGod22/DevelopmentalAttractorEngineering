# R27 — Evening Coverage Decision

## The problem in numbers

- 40% of ticket volume arrives 6pm–11pm, but only 2 of 12 agents (~17% of capacity) work past 6pm, and formal coverage ends at 8pm — the 8pm–11pm window has effectively zero staffing.
- Evening response times are 3x daytime. Two enterprise clients have already complained, which means the cost of the gap has moved from "internal metric" to "revenue risk."

Rough capacity check: if evening demand is ~40% of volume across a 5-hour window, matching it proportionally needs roughly 4–5 agent-equivalents in that window. Shifting three agents to 3pm–11pm produces 5 agents on duty 6pm–8pm (3 shifted + 2 current late-shift) and 3 agents 8pm–11pm — approximately the right shape. Two part-time contractors alone would likely still leave the evening thin, and not for six more weeks.

## Evaluating the two options

**Option A — shift three agents to 3pm–11pm**
- Effective immediately; zero incremental cost.
- Correctly sized to demand; shifted agents already know the product, tooling, and enterprise accounts — no quality dip.
- Cost: two of the three prefer not to. That is a real morale/retention liability, but it is a manageable one (they said they'd comply), and it can be softened: ask for volunteers across all 12 first, add a shift differential or an extra perk, and commit to a review date (e.g., rotate or revisit in 8–12 weeks).
- Mild secondary cost: daytime thins by three agents, but daytime currently carries a disproportionate share of capacity relative to its 60% of volume, so it can absorb this.

**Option B — hire two part-time evening contractors**
- Cheap (~1 FTE) and preserves the incumbent team's preferred schedules.
- But: 6 weeks of onboarding before they're effective, and even then contractors ramping on a new product are unlikely to match tenured agents on complex enterprise tickets — the exact tickets that triggered the complaints.
- The problem persists at full severity for at least 6 weeks while enterprise clients are already escalating.

## The SLA/renewal consideration — why it's decisive

The two enterprise contracts may contain response-time SLAs (with credits or termination-for-breach clauses) or renewal dates in the near term. The team lead should pull those contracts **before finalizing anything**, because:

- If an SLA is currently being breached, or a renewal decision lands inside the next ~6 weeks, Option B guarantees the gap stays open through exactly the window where the damage is done. SLA credits or a lost enterprise renewal would dwarf the cost difference between the options. In that case the 6-week onboarding lag alone disqualifies contractors as the sole response — regardless of the preference cost of Option A.
- Only if the contracts show no SLA exposure and renewals are comfortably distant (6+ months) does Option B's slower timeline become tolerable — and even then it trades a known staffing preference problem for an unproven-quality problem in the highest-risk window.

Note the options are not truly exclusive over time: the strongest play is Option A now as the immediate fix, with contractors as a later relief valve if the shifted agents' dissatisfaction persists at the review date. But forced to a single choice, the answer is A.

## Answer

Shift the three agents to 3pm–11pm now. First, check the two enterprise contracts for SLA terms and renewal dates — this will almost certainly confirm rather than reverse the choice, since active complaints from enterprise clients plus a 3x response-time gap cannot wait six weeks on an unproven fix. Soften the preference cost: solicit volunteers from the full team before assigning, add an evening differential or comp perk, and set an explicit 8–12 week review with the option to rotate the shift or bring in contractors as longer-term relief.

Recommendation: Shift three agents to the 3pm–11pm schedule immediately (volunteer-first, with a shift differential and a scheduled review); verify enterprise SLA terms and renewal dates before finalizing; hold contractors in reserve as a later relief option.
Confidence: 80
Main reason: The enterprise-client risk is live now — a 6-week onboarding gap (plus contractor ramp-up on complex enterprise tickets) leaves the highest-cost failure window uncovered, while the shift change fixes coverage immediately at zero incremental cost with agents who already know the product.
Condition most likely to change recommendation: If the reluctant agents are credible flight risks (likely to quit rather than merely comply) AND the enterprise contracts show no SLA breach exposure or renewal dates within the next ~2 quarters — losing tenured agents would then cost more than six more weeks of slow evenings, tipping the choice to contractors.
