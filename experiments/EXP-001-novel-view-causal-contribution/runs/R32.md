# R32 — Venue Decision Analysis

## The setup

- **Venue A:** capacity 300, $15,000, fully refundable until 60 days out; catering must be arranged separately.
- **Venue B:** capacity 450, $22,000, non-refundable, includes catering worth ~$6,000 if bought separately.
- Event is in five months (~150 days), targeting 300 people. Last year: 280 tickets sold. This year's early-bird sold 30% faster than last year. Tickets are $150.

## Step 1: Normalize the costs

The sticker prices are misleading because Venue B bundles catering.

- Venue A all-in: $15,000 + ~$6,000 catering = **~$21,000**
- Venue B all-in: **$22,000** (catering included)

On a like-for-like basis, B costs only about **$1,000 more**, not $7,000 more. (This assumes the event actually wants catering at roughly that quality/price point — a load-bearing assumption.)

## Step 2: Value the two real differences

Once costs are normalized, the decision reduces to two asymmetric features:

**1. Capacity headroom (favors B).**
Venue A "holds exactly 300" for a 300-person target — zero headroom. Last year sold 280; the early-bird pace is 30% faster this year. Velocity is a noisy signal (early-bird speed can reflect discount pull-forward, not total demand), but the direction points up. If final demand lands anywhere above 300, Venue A hard-caps revenue and forces turning people away. Each ticket beyond 300 is $150 of margin: just **7 extra tickets ($1,050) pays the entire cost difference**; 330 attendees would net +$4,500; 350 would net +$7,500. A also leaves no room for staff, speakers, sponsors, or no-show overbooking — operationally tight even at plan.

**2. Refundability (favors A).**
Venue A is free to cancel until 60 days out; Venue B puts $22,000 at risk immediately. This is real option value, but note its shape: it only pays off in a cancellation/collapse scenario during days 150→60. For an established annual event that sold 280 tickets last year and is currently selling *faster*, the probability of outright cancellation is low. And A's refundability expires 60 days out anyway — for the final two months both venues are sunk.

## Step 3: Compare the bets

- Paying ~$1,000 extra for B buys: up to 150 seats of upside (max ~$22,500 additional gross), catering logistics solved, and operational slack.
- Choosing A buys: ~$1,000 savings plus a cancellation hedge worth $15,000 × P(cancel before day 60) — likely a small expected value given current demand signals — at the cost of a hard revenue ceiling that the growth signal suggests may actually bind.

The downside scenarios also differ in severity: if attendance disappoints (say 250), both venues are already paid for and the P&L difference is ~$1,000. If attendance surprises upward, only A punishes you. The asymmetry favors B unless cancellation risk is material.

## Step 4: What would flip it

- Meaningful probability of cancellation or postponement (weak sponsor commitments, uncertain speakers, external risk) → A's refundability dominates everything.
- Catering not actually needed, or sourceable for far under $6,000 → A's true all-in drops well below B's and the cost gap widens.
- Evidence the early-bird pace is pure pull-forward (total registrations tracking flat vs. last year) → the capacity option is worth less, weakening B's case.

A reasonable hedge if the organizer is nervous: book A now (free option), watch two more months of sales data, and switch to B before A's refund window matters — but only if B's availability that late is likely, which is not guaranteed for a 5-months-out booking.

## Recommendation

**Book Venue B**, provided the organizer is reasonably confident the event will run and catering at the ~$6,000 level is genuinely needed. After netting the included catering, B costs only about $1,000 more all-in, and that $1,000 buys 150 seats of headroom against a demand signal that is already trending above last year's 280 — a break-even of just 7 extra tickets — plus simpler logistics. Venue A's only decisive advantage is refundability, which matters only in a low-probability cancellation scenario and expires 60 days out regardless.
