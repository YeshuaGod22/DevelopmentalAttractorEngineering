# R02 — Feature X vs Feature Y

## Question
A PM must pick one feature for next quarter: Feature X (offline mode) — top request from paying customers, appears in 30% of cancellation surveys, one quarter of work. Feature Y (real-time collaboration) — rarely in cancellation surveys, but the most common reason prospects cite for choosing a competitor per sales, same effort, more technical risk. Monthly churn is 4%; new-customer growth has slowed for two quarters.

## Analysis

**The churn math is the loudest signal.** 4% monthly churn compounds to roughly 39% of the customer base lost per year. With acquisition already slowing, the business is leaking from a bucket it is refilling more slowly each quarter — that combination shrinks revenue even if sales holds steady. Retention improvements act on the entire existing base immediately and compound every month they're in effect.

**Evidence quality is asymmetric.** The case for X rests on structured, first-party data from people who actually paid and then left: 30% of cancellation surveys name the missing capability. The case for Y rests on secondhand, sales-mediated accounts of why prospects walked. Lost-deal attributions are notoriously noisy — "they had collaboration" is an easy, polite reason for a prospect to give and an easy pattern for a sales team to over-index on, and it often masks price, brand, or inertia. That doesn't make it false, but it deserves a wider error bar.

**Risk profile favors X.** Same estimated effort, but Y carries more technical risk (real-time sync is genuinely hard — conflict resolution, presence, infrastructure). A one-quarter estimate on the riskier project is more likely to slip into two, delaying whichever bet comes next. X (offline mode, which notably shares some sync machinery with collaboration) is the more predictable delivery — and building a robust local-first sync layer for offline mode can become the foundation for collaboration later, making X a partial down payment on Y rather than a dead end.

**Expected-value sketch.** If offline mode eliminates even half of the 30% of churn that cites it, monthly churn drops from 4% toward ~3.4% — a durable, compounding gain on known revenue. For Y to beat that, it would have to win a comparable volume of new deals, on shakier evidence, with higher delivery risk, into a slowing top-of-funnel. Slowing growth is a reason to investigate *why* acquisition is stalling, not yet a mandate to bet the quarter on one hypothesized cause.

**What the PM should do about Y:** don't discard it — instrument it. Get win/loss interviews done by someone other than the losing rep, quantify how often collaboration is the real blocker vs. a mentioned nice-to-have, and validate demand with prospects directly. If that evidence firms up, Y is the natural next-quarter bet — built on top of X's sync layer.

## Recommendation

Schedule **Feature X (offline mode)** for next quarter, and spend a small slice of the quarter hardening the evidence on Y (structured win/loss analysis) so the following quarter's decision is made on data as good as X's.

Recommendation: Feature X (offline mode)
Confidence: 80
Main reason: At 4% monthly (~39% annualized) churn, fixing a retention driver documented in 30% of cancellation surveys is a compounding, low-risk gain backed by strong first-party evidence, whereas Y's case rests on noisy secondhand lost-deal reports plus higher technical risk for the same effort.
Condition most likely to change recommendation: Rigorous win/loss data (not sales anecdote) showing collaboration is the decisive blocker in a large, quantified share of lost deals — combined with evidence that the acquisition slowdown is feature-driven rather than market- or channel-driven — would flip the bet to Feature Y.
