# R15 — Evening Coverage Decision

## Initial view

Shift the three agents now. The numbers make the case bluntly: 40% of volume lands 6pm–11pm, and current staffing past 6pm is two agents until 8pm and **zero from 8pm to 11pm** — a fifth of the demand window has no coverage at all. Evening response times at 3x daytime, with two enterprise clients already complaining, is an active revenue risk, not a scheduling inconvenience. The contractor option leaves that wound open for six more weeks of onboarding before anyone is effective, and part-time contractors are the people least likely to handle enterprise-grade tickets well in their first months. Reassigning shifts is ordinary operational authority; the two reluctant agents have said they would comply. Soften it with a volunteer call first, a shift differential if budget allows, and an explicit review date — but move the coverage now.

## A materially different frame

**The scarce asset is not coverage hours; it is experienced agents who stay.** The initial view treats the schedule grid as the system and people as movable tiles. Reframe: in a 12-person team, the binding constraint is retention of tenured agents who carry product knowledge and the enterprise relationships. "They'd comply" is what people say in the meeting; forced evening shifts are one of the most reliable quit triggers in support work, and departures cluster — one resignation licenses the next. Losing even one experienced agent costs far more than a year of the contractor budget (roughly half an FTE, by the problem's own numbers), and it creates a coverage hole worse than the one being fixed, in daytime *and* evening.

**Hold the frame; state what follows if it is right.** If retention is the real constraint, then the six-week onboarding delay is the cheaper of two irreversibilities: slow evening response is recoverable and can be bridged (a paid volunteer on-call rotation for 8–11pm, proactive outreach to the two enterprise clients with a dated fix, temporary triage of enterprise tickets to first position each morning), whereas attrition of tenured staff is not recoverable on any comparable timescale. Under this frame the right choice is: hire the contractors, bridge the six weeks with voluntary paid measures, and treat coerced schedule changes as the option of last resort — because the "fast" option is actually the slow one once you price in a resignation and a backfill.

## Final recommendation

The frames genuinely disagree about which risk is less reversible — enterprise churn versus staff attrition — and the problem gives no data (contract renewal dates, agents' tenure, local labor market) to settle it. So the recommendation preserves the tension by refusing the pure form of either option:

**Do both, sequenced.** Hire the two contractors now as the permanent evening solution — at half the cost of an FTE against 40% of volume, that is cheap capacity even if the shift question didn't exist. For the six-week gap, run a volunteer-first bridge: ask for volunteers for a *time-boxed* 3pm–11pm rotation with a shift differential or comp time, starting with the one agent who didn't object. Only if volunteering plus incentives cannot cover the gap does the lead assign the reluctant agents — explicitly temporary, with a written end date tied to contractor readiness. Call the two enterprise clients this week with the plan and dates; the complaint is as much about feeling unheard as about latency.

If the budget truly permits only one option, take the shift move — uncovered enterprise demand is the nearer cliff — but recognize that choosing it *without* the time-box converts a coverage problem into a retention problem.

Recommendation: Hire the two evening contractors as the permanent fix; bridge the 6-week gap with a paid, time-boxed, volunteer-first evening rotation (compelled assignment only as last resort, with a written end date); notify the enterprise clients of the plan immediately.
Confidence: 70
Main reason: Contractor capacity at half an FTE's cost permanently matches staffing to 40% of demand without spending the scarcer asset — tenured agents' willingness to stay — while the bridge caps the enterprise-churn risk during onboarding.
Condition most likely to change recommendation: An enterprise contract renewal or escalation inside the 6-week window (or contractors proving unable to handle enterprise-tier tickets) — either would force the permanent evening shift of existing agents despite the retention cost.
