# R07 — Roof vs. Lobby Renovation ($40,000, 60-unit building)

## Framing the two options

**Option A (roof):** The contractor says 2–4 years of remaining life, so this is a deferrable-but-hard deadline, not an emergency. Cost escalation at ~8%/year means waiting two years raises the price roughly 17%. The real risk is tail risk: a failure causes cascading damage (interiors, tenant claims, emergency premiums) far beyond the roof itself.

**Option B (lobby/hallways):** The building is bleeding **$7,200/month = $86,400/year** in lost rent across 6 vacancies (~$1,200/unit/month). Two prospective tenants explicitly named the common areas as the reason they walked — that is unusually direct causal evidence, not a guess. Comparable renovated buildings run 95% occupancy, which for 60 units means ~3 vacancies. Closing even half the gap (6 → 3 vacancies) recovers ~$3,600/month = **$43,200/year** — the $40,000 renovation pays back in roughly 11 months, and in ~6 months if occupancy reaches the comp level.

## The consideration that decides it

**$40,000 is very likely not enough to replace the roof on a 60-unit building.** Full replacements on buildings this size typically run well into six figures depending on roof area and system. So Option A, in practice, is probably not "replace the roof" — it is either (a) a partial repair/restoration that spends the entire budget without eliminating the failure risk, or (b) a down payment that still requires supplemental funds the manager doesn't have. That asymmetry matters enormously:

- Spending $40k on Option A likely buys an *incomplete* solution to the roof **and** forfeits ~$43–86k/year of recoverable revenue.
- Spending $40k on Option B buys a *complete* solution to the vacancy problem, and the recovered cash flow (~$43k+/year) is precisely what funds the roof replacement — through a dedicated reserve, or by improving NOI enough to support financing — well inside the 2–4 year window.

Even pricing in the 8% escalation: on a hypothetical $150k roof, waiting two years costs ~$25k extra. The renovation's first-year recovered rent alone exceeds that, and every year after is pure gain. The escalation penalty is smaller than the vacancy penalty.

## The recommendation, with guardrails

**Fund Option B — renovate the lobby and hallways — but treat the roof as an active, managed liability, not a deferred one:**

1. **Immediately get 2–3 firm roof replacement quotes.** This is nearly free and converts "2–4 years, cost unknown" into a real number and a real deadline. It is the single highest-value information purchase available.
2. **Commission a roof inspection now and semi-annually**, with a small maintenance budget (a few thousand dollars) for patching, flashing, and drainage upkeep to push actual life toward the 4-year end and cut failure risk.
3. **Earmark the recovered rent into a roof reserve** (e.g., $3,000–4,000/month once vacancies fill). At the comp occupancy level, the reserve plus next year's capital budget covers a six-figure roof within the window.
4. **Verify insurance coverage** for interior damage from roof failure, so the tail risk is at least partially transferred while the reserve builds.

The logic that flips this: if the firm quotes or inspection reveal the roof is actually at the short end (active leaks, deck deterioration, credible failure within ~1–2 years), the cascading-damage risk dominates everything — a roof failure would empty units far faster than a nice lobby fills them, and the manager should put the $40k toward the roof (as repair or financing down payment) instead. Similarly, if it turns out $40k genuinely covers a full replacement or restoration on this building (possible for some single-story-footprint or coating-eligible roofs), the option comparison changes: a complete roof fix at $40k versus a renovation becomes a much closer call, and eliminating the catastrophic tail for the same money is defensible.

But on the facts as given — a roof with runway, a renovation with a sub-one-year payback backed by direct tenant evidence, and a budget that probably can't complete the roof anyway — the renovation is the move that makes the roof affordable.

**Recommendation:** Fund Option B (lobby/hallway renovation) now; simultaneously get firm roof quotes, fund minor roof maintenance/inspections, and channel recovered rent into a dedicated roof reserve so the roof is fully replaced within its 2–4 year window.
