# R13 — Evening Coverage Decision

## Framing the problem precisely

Two facts in the setup matter more than they first appear:

1. **The current schedule doesn't just under-staff evenings — it ends before the evening peak does.** Coverage stops at 8pm, but 40% of tickets arrive 6pm–11pm. So roughly the 8pm–11pm slice currently has *zero* live coverage; those tickets sit overnight or until the next morning. "Evening response times are triple daytime" is probably an underestimate of the pain for the late tail. Any acceptable option must extend coverage to 11pm, which shifting agents to 3pm–11pm does and the current schedule cannot.
2. **The clock is already running on the enterprise relationships.** Two enterprise clients have complained. That is usually a lagging indicator — complaints arrive after frustration has accumulated. A fix that takes 6+ weeks to become effective is really a 2–3 month fix from the clients' point of view.

## Perspectives

**Customer/revenue perspective.** Enterprise clients who complain and then see no visible change are the ones who churn. The contractor option means ~6 weeks of onboarding before effectiveness, plus hiring time before that — realistically 8–10 weeks until evening response times move. The reassignment option changes evening staffing next Monday. From a revenue-risk view, speed dominates: the cost of losing one enterprise account likely exceeds the entire annual cost delta between the options. Favors **shifting agents**.

**Employee/retention perspective.** Two of the three reassigned agents don't want the schedule. "They'd comply" is how support-team attrition starts: compliance now, job search in three months. Replacing an experienced support agent costs months of ramp and institutional knowledge — ironically the same 6-week-plus onboarding problem the contractor option has, but unplanned. This perspective says forced reassignment is borrowing against retention, and favors **contractors**, or at minimum demands the reassignment be made voluntary and compensated (shift differential, rotation, explicit end date).

**Quality-of-service perspective.** The complaining customers are *enterprise* clients. Enterprise evening tickets are disproportionately the ones that need tenured agents with product depth and escalation judgment. Two part-time contractors, even after onboarding, are the weakest possible staffing for exactly the tickets that triggered this decision. Experienced internal agents on the evening shift directly address the complaint; contractors mostly address the volume metric. Favors **shifting agents**.

**Financial/structural perspective.** Contractors cost ~1 FTE of new spend; reassignment is nominally free. But reassignment thins the 8am–6pm window by three agents while 60% of volume still arrives in daytime — daytime response times will degrade somewhat. The deeper structural point: if 40% of demand is 6pm–11pm, evening coverage is not a temporary problem, and building a permanent evening capability (which contractors start to do, and which two part-timers also give scheduling flexibility for sick days/attrition) is the durable answer. Favors **contractors** long-term, but doesn't rescue the 8–10 week gap.

**Data-skepticism perspective.** Before committing, the lead should sanity-check the 40% figure: what's the *severity* mix in the evening? If much of the evening volume is low-priority and auto-acknowledgeable, a smaller intervention (one shifted volunteer + better triage/SLA routing for enterprise tickets) might suffice. Also worth asking whether the two enterprise complaints are about the 6–8pm slowness or the 8pm–11pm dead zone — the answer shapes how many bodies are needed and when. This perspective doesn't pick an option; it warns against over-rotating on a single aggregate statistic.

## Weighing it

The options are not actually mutually exclusive in time, and the best reading of the situation treats the reassignment as the bridge and hiring as the destination. But forced to choose one lever now, the decisive factors are (a) the enterprise churn clock, (b) the fact that only the reassignment closes the 8–11pm dead zone immediately, and (c) that the aggrieved clients need experienced agents, not trainees. The morale cost is real but manageable: ask for volunteers first (12-person team; the third agent already doesn't object — odds are decent a second volunteer exists), pay a shift differential, rotate the slot, and time-box it with an explicit review date. An unpopular schedule handled with compensation and a visible end condition is survivable; a lost enterprise account and a still-broken evening SLA are worse.

If the team lead can spend the money, the genuinely right move is: shift agents now *and* start the contractor hire, letting contractors relieve the reluctant agents in ~2 months. That converts the reassignment from an indefinite imposition into a bridge, which largely defuses the retention risk.

## Answer

**Recommendation:** Shift three agents to the 3pm–11pm schedule now (volunteers first, with shift differential and a stated review/end date) — and if budget allows, begin the contractor hire in parallel so the reassignment becomes a temporary bridge rather than a permanent imposition.

**Confidence:** 70

**Main reason:** The enterprise-client risk is immediate and only the reassignment fixes evening coverage (including the currently uncovered 8pm–11pm window) with experienced agents this week; the contractor path leaves the problem unaddressed for 8–10 weeks, during which the accounts most likely to churn get no visible improvement.

**Condition most likely to change recommendation:** If either reluctant agent is a credible flight risk (senior, hard to replace, already signaling exit) and no volunteers emerge — or if the enterprise clients confirm they'll tolerate a stated 6–8 week remediation timeline — the retention cost of forcing the shift outweighs the speed advantage, and the contractor option (with interim triage prioritization for enterprise tickets) becomes the better choice.
