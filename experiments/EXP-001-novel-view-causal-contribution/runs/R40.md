# R40 — Should the agency dedicate the team?

## Short answer

No — not as demanded. The agency should not unilaterally pull both of its strongest designers off all other accounts. It should counter-offer: a named, dedicated account team built around *one* of the two star designers plus strong supporting staff, and make full dedication conditional on the big client signing a longer, richer renewal. Meanwhile, use the four-month window to reduce the 40% concentration, not deepen it.

## Reasoning

**1. The demand, taken literally, increases the agency's most dangerous risk.**
40% revenue concentration is already fragile. Fully dedicating the two best designers converts the whole agency's talent strategy into a bet on a single contract — one that renews in four months under a procurement lead known for consolidating vendors. Consolidators cut on price and interchangeability, not on relationship warmth. The agency could strip its other accounts bare, hand over its stars, and still lose the renewal — the worst possible outcome: big client gone *and* smaller clients alienated.

**2. The smaller clients are the hedge, and the hedge is live.**
Two smaller clients have praised these exact designers by name, and one is signaling expansion. Named praise means the relationship is attached to the people, not the agency brand — pull those designers and the agency actively damages its diversification exactly when it needs it. The expansion hint is the cheapest revenue growth available; expansion revenue from an existing happy client typically costs far less to win than replacing a lost anchor client.

**3. The demand is a negotiating asset, not an ultimatum to obey.**
The client is asking for something valuable (dedicated senior talent). Valuable things are priced and traded, not given away pre-renewal. Conceding fully now, for free, tells the new procurement lead the agency will absorb costs under pressure — which invites harder terms at renewal, not softer ones. The right move is to trade: "You get a formally dedicated team — lead star designer, dedicated PM, ring-fenced capacity, named SLAs — and full two-designer dedication comes with an 18–24 month renewal at rates that fund backfill hiring."

**4. Capacity is buildable in four months; trust is not.**
Four months is enough to hire or promote to backfill partial reallocation. It is not enough to rebuild two smaller-client relationships after publicly demoting them. So spend money (hiring) rather than trust (abandoning accounts).

## Recommended plan

1. **Counter-offer a dedicated-team structure now**: one star designer as dedicated creative lead for the big client, dedicated PM/account lead, guaranteed capacity and response times. This satisfies the substance of the demand (a team that's "theirs") without gutting other accounts.
2. **Tie full dedication of both stars to the renewal**: multi-year term and pricing that funds two senior backfill hires. Put it in the proposal explicitly.
3. **Protect the growth accounts**: keep the second star visibly on the smaller accounts; pursue the expansion hint immediately and try to close it before the big renewal, shrinking the 40% figure.
4. **Probe the procurement lead early**: ask directly what the consolidation criteria are (price, breadth, dedicated service?). If the real driver is price, dedicating stars won't save the account anyway — and the agency should know that before paying for the renewal with its diversification.
5. **Start backfill hiring now**, so that if the renewal lands with full dedication, the smaller clients see added capacity rather than subtraction.

## Answer to the question as posed

Should the agency dedicate the team? **Not in the form demanded.** Offer a genuine dedicated team around one star, price full dedication into the renewal, and use the four months to grow the hedge.
