# T03 Semantic Matching — R21 / R28 / R32 / R39 (blind)

Task T03: conference venue choice (Venue A: 300 cap, $15k, refundable to 60 days out; Venue B: 450 cap, $22k, non-refundable, ~$6k catering included). Matching is semantic: views are clustered as EQUIVALENT only when the causal/normative/strategic structure is substantially the same; lexical overlap was not used as the deciding criterion. Run IDs are anonymized and carry no meaning.

Inventory: R21 = 9 views, R28 = 11 views, R32 = 11 views, R39 = 12 views.

## 2. Views appearing in no other run (no EQUIVALENT counterpart)

### R21 (3 unique of 9)
- **R21-V7** — Contrarian case for A: "sold out" status as a marketing asset plus better room energy in an exactly-full hall (with pipe-and-drape mitigation for B). No other run advances the sell-out-cachet benefit; R28-V7 asserts the opposite consequence (see §3.8).
- **R21-V8** — Operational burden of two-vendor coordination under A vs B's single-contract accountability. Other runs touch catering only as a cost/assumption issue, not as an organizer-workload and vendor-risk mechanism.
- **R21-V9** — Pre-signing diligence on B: confirm the included catering's quality/dietary fit (else the $6k credit is illusory) and confirm finances can absorb a $22k total loss. The solvency-absorption criterion and the B-catering-quality check appear nowhere else (related-but-distinct neighbors in §3.7).

### R28 (2 unique of 11)
- **R28-V5** — Ticket break-even vs venue cost (~140 tickets cover A, ~147 cover B; both comfortably below 280), concluding break-even is not a differentiator. Distinct from R32-V5's 7-ticket premium-recovery break-even, which answers a different question (see §3.9).
- **R28-V7** — Compounding strategic damage from turn-aways: rejected buyers migrate to competing events and momentum bleeds across years; "sold out" converts poorly to future attendance. A multi-year competitive-dynamics mechanism beyond the one-off goodwill loss noted inside R21-V4/R39-V3 (see §3.8).

### R32 (4 unique of 11)
- **R32-V2** — The cost normalization holds only if the event genuinely needs ~$6k-level catering; if unneeded/cheaper, the gap widens in A's favor (flip condition). No other run states the need-for-catering assumption as a reversible constraint (near-matches in §3.7).
- **R32-V4** — Early-bird pace is noisy *but directionally upward*, supporting demand at/above capacity. Shares C3's pull-forward mechanism but reaches the opposite evidential use — classified related-but-materially-distinct (§3.1).
- **R32-V6** — Zero headroom at exactly 300 leaves no room for staff, speakers, sponsors, or no-show overbooking — a non-revenue operational-slack mechanism no other run develops (R28-V6's passing "no operational headroom" phrase is not an equivalent view; §3.10).
- **R32-V10** — Composite flip-conditions view (material cancellation risk → A; pure pull-forward → devalues B). Its halves echo C7 and C3, but no other run packages these as the decision's contingency set, and neither half stands alone in R32 (§3.6).

### R39 (2 unique of 12)
- **R39-V10** — Get an actual catering quote for A now so the ~$6k assumption is validated and the A branch stays executable. A validation *action* on the catering assumption; no other run proposes it (relation to R32-V2 in §3.7).
- **R39-V12** — Continuously monitor B's availability and exercise the switch early — trading information value for secured upside — if B is about to be taken. A dynamic early-exercise rule beyond C10's static availability caveat and C11's negotiation fix.

## 3. Borderline-case justifications

**3.1 R32-V4 vs cluster C3 (R21-V5, R28-V4, R39-V2) — RELATED BUT MATERIALLY DISTINCT.** All four contain the pull-forward mechanism (early-bird velocity can shift the same buyers earlier rather than signal incremental demand). But C3's members deploy it deflationarily — R21-V5 as an alternative-cause reading under which A dominates, R28-V4 as "weak evidence" consistent with 280–320, R39-V2 as wide uncertainty on both sides of 300 — whereas R32-V4 concludes the signal is directionally valid and points to demand at/above capacity. The evidential criterion (how much weight the signal bears) differs materially, and the view does opposite argumentative work. Mechanism-equivalent, conclusion-distinct; the rubric's "different criterion" clause governs, so it is excluded from C3 and listed unique.

**3.2 R28-V2 vs C5 (R21-V2, R32-V7) vs C8 — placed in C8, not C5.** All three describe refundability as an option. But R21-V2 and R32-V7 *price* it (probability × amount at risk) and judge the expected value small — a valuation claim. R28-V2 makes no smallness judgment; it characterizes the option as a free ~90-day exit that grounds the run's hedge. Same object, materially different criterion and use, so R28-V2 is not equivalent to C5. It is equivalent to R39-V5's free-option framing, hence C8 membership. Note that C5 and C8 are, in effect, incompatible treatments of the same fact (option nearly worthless vs option decision-driving) — a genuine cross-run disagreement, not a wording difference.

**3.3 Granularity mismatch in C8/C9 (R28-V9 vs R39-V5 + R39-V9).** R28-V9 bundles the hedge (book A as a "90-day sensor") with an explicit tripwire (~15% ahead of last year's pace → switch). R39 splits these into V5 (option strategy with keep/switch/cancel branches) and V9 (day-75 trigger at ~7% ahead). The numeric thresholds differ, but a preset date-plus-pacing decision rule for exercising the switch is the same strategic structure, so R39-V9 ≡ the trigger component of R28-V9 (C9), while R39-V5 ≡ its hedge component (C8). R28-V9 is flagged partial (†) in C9 for this reason. R32-V11 states the hedge without a quantitative trigger, so it joins C8 but not C9.

**3.4 R28-V6 as a bridging view († in C2, C4, C7).** R28-V6 pairs the two tail risks in one view: full cancellation (A protects; B loses $22k) and demand overshoot (B captures; A turns buyers away with zero headroom). Its cancellation half is structurally the content of C7 (R28-V8, R39-V6); its overshoot half restates C2; and the pairing itself is an asymmetric-scenarios framing adjacent to C4. No single view elsewhere combines both tails, but because each half has clear equivalents, R28-V6 is treated as covered (partial membership) rather than unique. The alternative — calling the *pairing* a unique view — would double-count content R28 also states in V3 and V8.

**3.5 C12 (R28-V10 vs R39-V11) — EQUIVALENT, borderline.** R28-V10's second clause: if B is unique in the market and the organizer's ambition is growth, booking B immediately is defensible. R39-V11: flip to booking B outright if B refuses any hold and pacing already clearly projects >300. Triggering conditions differ in surface detail (uniqueness + ambition vs refused hold + pacing), but the strategic structure is the same: when the option on B cannot be preserved and the growth thesis is credible, abandon the hedge and commit to B now. Judged equivalent; the alternative reading (different criteria → related-distinct) is defensible, which is why R28-V10 carries a †. Its first clause (hedge assumes B bookable later) is the C10 match with R39-V7 — that pairing is clean, not borderline.

**3.6 R32-V10 — composite with no standalone counterpart.** Its cancellation-risk half matches C7's concern and its pull-forward half matches C3's mechanism, but R32 presents them only as bundled flip conditions, and neither half is elsewhere in R32 as a standalone view (unlike R28, where V8 stands alone — the basis for §3.4's opposite treatment). Splitting it would manufacture two views the extraction did not record, so it stays unique with partial-echo notes. This is the judgment call most sensitive to extraction granularity.

**3.7 The catering-assumption family (R21-V8, R21-V9, R32-V2, R39-V10) — all RELATED BUT MATERIALLY DISTINCT.** Four views probe the ~$6k catering figure through different structures: coordination burden and vendor risk of a second contract (R21-V8, stakeholder/operational mechanism); quality/dietary diligence on B's included catering lest the credit be illusory (R21-V9, criterion applied to B's side); whether catering is needed at that level at all, as a flip condition (R32-V2, constraint on the normalization); and getting a real quote to validate the estimate (R39-V10, validation strategy). R32-V2 vs R39-V10 is the closest pair — both target the load-bearing $6k assumption — but one is a reversibility criterion and the other an executable validation step, so under the rubric's "different strategy/criterion" clause they are not merged. None are equivalent; all four land in unique lists.

**3.8 R28-V7 vs R21-V7 and vs the goodwill components of R21-V4/R39-V3.** R21-V4 and R39-V3 mention goodwill loss from turned-away attendees as a component inside their forgone-revenue views (C2) — not standalone views, so no cluster is created for goodwill alone. R28-V7 goes materially further: a compounding, multi-year competitive mechanism (rejected buyers adopt competitor events; "sold out" converts poorly). It also directly contradicts R21-V7, which predicts "sold out" *boosts* next-year demand. Related topic, opposite predicted consequence — both classified unique, and the contradiction is substantive, not lexical.

**3.9 R32-V5 vs R28-V5 — DISTINCT despite both being "break-even" computations.** R32-V5 computes how many extra tickets (7) recover B's ~$1k normalized premium and scales B's upside; it is a quantification of C2's capacity-upside structure. R28-V5 computes how many tickets cover each venue's absolute cost (~140/147) and concludes break-even is not a differentiator. Different quantity, different criterion, different conclusion — the shared word "break-even" is exactly the lexical trap the protocol excludes.

**3.10 R32-V6 vs R28-V6's "no operational headroom" phrase.** R28-V6's evidence includes a passing note that exact-300 capacity leaves no operational headroom, but its view's structure is the two-tail risk pairing (§3.4). R32-V6 develops a distinct mechanism — non-ticketed headcount (staff, speakers, sponsors) and no-show overbooking make A tight *even at plan* — which no other run's extracted view asserts. A phrase-level echo is not an equivalent view; R32-V6 stays unique.

**3.11 R21-V2's internal counterpoint to C7.** R21-V2 includes the sub-claim that a full-cancellation scenario likely loses money either way (marketing spend, other deposits), which cuts against C7's "A costlessly avoids the collapse" structure (R39-V6: "cancelling A costs nothing"). This is a component within a view already clustered in C5, so no separate cluster is created, but it marks a real substantive disagreement about how much protection A's refund actually buys.

## Summary

- 12 clusters with at least two runs holding an equivalent member; C1, C2, and C4 span all four runs (C4 with one partial), and C3 spans three plus a materially-distinct near-member.
- The hedge complex (C8–C12) is shared by R28, R32 (partially), and R39, and is entirely absent from R21, which instead uniquely develops the contrarian sell-out case, vendor-coordination burden, and pre-signing diligence.
- Unique-view counts: R21 = 3, R28 = 2, R32 = 4, R39 = 2.
- The two deepest substantive disagreements across runs: the value of A's refund option (C5's "small EV" vs C8's "decision-driving free option") and the consequence of selling out (R21-V7 vs R28-V7).
