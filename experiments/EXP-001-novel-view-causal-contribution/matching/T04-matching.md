# T04 Cross-Response View Matching (R19, R24, R36, R38)

Blind semantic crosswalk for Task T04 (school district: $60,000 expiring in six weeks; Option A = replace 150 laptops; Option B = two-year math tutoring for 80 students). Matching is by semantic structure (mechanism, criterion, stakeholder-interest, constraint, jurisdiction, strategy, consequence), not lexical overlap. Run IDs are anonymized and treated as meaningless.

Composite views (one extracted view containing several distinct structures) are assigned to clusters per component, marked "(component)". Views that share a topic with a cluster but introduce a different mechanism, criterion, or consequence are NOT cluster members; they appear in the unique lists with cross-references and are argued in the borderline section.

## 2. Unique views (no EQUIVALENT counterpart in any other run)

### R19
- **V12** — Rawlsian/equity frame arguing tutoring wins because concentrated transformative benefit to the furthest-behind outweighs thin diffuse benefit. Directionally argues for B on equity; no other run makes this affirmative equity argument (see borderline B2 re R36-V14).
- **V13** — Device reliability matters most for low-income students lacking home computing alternatives, partially offsetting the equity case for tutoring. No other run identifies this specific affected interest.
- **V14** — Conditional defeater: tutoring's equity advantage evaporates if slots go to already-engaged families rather than highest-need students. R36-V14 gestures at a selection-equity question but lacks this defeating mechanism (borderline B3).

### R24
- **V15** — Contingent residual-funds tutoring bridge: only if volume discounts leave a remainder, spend it on a short fall bridge contract. Related to C17 but a different strategy structure (opportunistic remainder vs. deliberate split) — borderline B5.
- **V16** — Flip condition pairing legal-encumbrance confirmation with a *pilot-validity/dosage-adequacy* test. Shares C15's legal conjunct but introduces a different second criterion — borderline B4.

### R36
- **V9** — Rebuttal of the cliff argument: learning gains are durable and "banked" even if the program ends; the cliff objection wrongly treats program continuity, not learning delivered, as the goal. No counterpart anywhere.
- **V11** — Rebuttal of the freed-IT-capacity argument: the recovered hours are salary already paid and create value only if actually redeployed, which districts rarely execute. Directly undercuts C4; no other run raises redeployment.
- **V12** — Normative characterization: buying laptops is the district optimizing its own operational comfort while declining rare self-generated causal evidence about its students. Unique framing.
- **V13** — Rebuttal of the fiscal-matching rule: a fully-prefunded, defined-term two-year program is itself a bounded one-time expenditure, so C1's rationale (avoiding unfunded recurring obligations) is not triggered. Unique counter-view (borderline B6).
- **V14** — Breadth-vs-depth recast as a legitimate values tradeoff a board could decide either way, plus a slot-selection equity question. Related to R19-V12/V14 but materially distinct in criterion structure (borderline B2/B3).
- **V15** — Standalone sensitivity analysis: the recommendation depends heavily on pilot design quality (size, randomization, tutor typicality), with a bound (even a halved effect likely still beats hardware). Overlaps a conjunct of R24-V16 but functions as a load-bearing sensitivity claim, not a flip condition (borderline B4).
- **V18** — Reverse-direction flip condition: the choice flips (toward A) if devices are shown to be instruction-blocking (e.g., 1:1 testing requirements) rather than merely IT-burdening. Structurally not the C15 conditional — different antecedent, opposite direction (borderline B4).

### R38
- None. All 11 extracted views map into clusters (several as composites). R38's extraction is the coarsest-grained of the four; it contributes no substantive structure absent from every other run.

## 3. Borderline-case justifications

**B1. R36-V8 vs. C9 (the $375/student figure) — RELATED BUT MATERIALLY DISTINCT.** All three runs that compute $375/student/year draw from the same arithmetic, but the inferential valence is opposite: R19-V2 and R24-V10 treat the low figure as a red flag (the plan is underdosed/underfunded relative to what produced the pilot effect — an adequacy doubt), while R36-V8 treats it as a point in tutoring's favor (exceptional cost-effectiveness for the effect class). Same datum, different predicted consequence and different criterion, so R36-V8 sits in C10 (evidence/value claim), not C9. Lexical overlap here would have produced a false match.

**B2. R19-V12 vs. R36-V14 (depth vs. breadth) — RELATED BUT MATERIALLY DISTINCT.** Both address the 80-deep vs. everyone-shallow tradeoff. R19-V12 applies a directional normative criterion (Rawlsian maximin: the concentrated benefit to the worst-off wins). R36-V14 explicitly refuses that adjudication, classifying the tradeoff as a legitimate values choice a board could make either way. One resolves the tradeoff with a criterion; the other asserts the tradeoff has no analytic resolution. Different normative structure; not clustered.

**B3. R19-V14 vs. R36-V14 (slot-selection equity) — RELATED BUT MATERIALLY DISTINCT.** R19-V14 states a conditional defeater with a mechanism: if slots go to already-engaged families, the concentrated-benefit rationale fails. R36-V14 flags "an equity question about which 80 students are selected" without the defeating conditional. Flagging a question is not the same structure as a stated defeat condition; R19-V14 stays unique with a near-match note.

**B4. The flip-condition family (R19-V17, R24-V16, R38-V10, R36-V18).** R19-V17 and R38-V10 are classified EQUIVALENT: both require (i) confirmed legal/practical ability to obligate the two-year contract within six weeks AND (ii) the laptop problem being otherwise manageable (fleet defensibly serviceable another year / alternative device funding; R38 adds a tutoring continuation-funding plan, an extension of the same sustainability logic rather than a different criterion). R24-V16 shares conjunct (i) verbatim in structure but substitutes a different second criterion — pilot internal validity and dosage adequacy — so it is RELATED BUT MATERIALLY DISTINCT from the C15 pair and is listed unique. R36-V18 is not in this family at all despite being a "flip condition": its antecedent (devices shown to be instruction-blocking) and its direction (flips toward A, since R36's baseline recommendation differs) make it a different conditional entirely — DISTINCT from C15, unique to R36. R36-V15 (pilot-quality sensitivity) semantically overlaps R24-V16's second conjunct, but one is a standalone sensitivity/robustness claim with an explicit survival bound and the other a necessary condition inside a decision rule; classified RELATED BUT MATERIALLY DISTINCT, both retained separately.

**B5. R24-V15 vs. C17 (R36-V17, R38-V11) — RELATED BUT MATERIALLY DISTINCT.** R36-V17 and R38-V11 propose a deliberate ex-ante split of the $60k (partial fleet replacement + reduced tutoring block), with explicit guard conditions (dosage preserved at smaller cohort; one-year block fully contractable) — EQUIVALENT to each other. R24-V15 proposes spending only an incidental procurement remainder on a short bridge; the allocation is contingent on discounts, not a designed split, and there is no partial-fleet element. Different strategy structure; excluded from C17.

**B6. R36-V13 vs. C1 — RELATED BUT MATERIALLY DISTINCT (deliberate exclusion).** R36 both states the fiscal-matching rule (V1, clustered in C1) and separately argues the rule's rationale dissolves for a prefunded defined-term program (V13). V13 shares C1's topic maximally but inverts its normative force by reclassifying the two-year program as a bounded one-time purchase — a new criterion application, hence a distinct view, not a cluster member.

**B7. R36-V11 vs. C4 and R36-V9 vs. C2 — DISTINCT-as-rebuttals.** Both share their target cluster's topic (freed IT capacity; funding cliff) but introduce new mechanisms (redeployment failure; durability of banked learning) that reverse the cluster's implication. Counted as unique views, not weak matches: semantic matching here is about shared structure, and a rebuttal's structure is the new mechanism it introduces.

**B8. R36 and cluster C6/C7/C8 (legal-encumbrance, procurement feasibility, forfeiture).** R36-V1 mentions the transaction "closing within the six-week window," which brushes C7, but the view's substantive structure is the matching principle; R36 contains no view whose mechanism is procurement-process risk, no legal-obligation constraint, and no forfeiture failure mode. This is an absence, not a borderline match — noted so the empty R36 cells in C6–C8 are read as genuine non-occurrence rather than paraphrase distance.
