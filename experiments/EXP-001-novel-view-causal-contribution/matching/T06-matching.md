## 2. Views appearing in NO other run (unique views)

### R08
- **R08-V3** — Card holds reframe hospitality into contract, signaling distrust. The customer-psychology mechanism is unmatched; R17-V11's "reads as hospitality" clause is the mirror image about option (c), not about holds (see B1).
- **R08-V8** — Differential staff burden: host burnout/turnover under (b) vs low-stakes scriptable daytime work under (c). No other run compares staff-side psychological load.
- **R08-V14** — Operational rule: release unconfirmed tables to a same-day waitlist by late afternoon. The backfill *logic* appears inside R31-V6, but only R08 states the release-by-deadline operational strategy.

### R17
- **R17-V2** — Segment-fit constraint: holds suit scarce-reservation destination restaurants, not a goodwill-competing neighborhood restaurant with near-permanent reviews. Opposed (not matched) by R44-V7's normalization claim.

### R31
- **R31-V2** — Asymmetry of harms: review damage persists and compounds; no-show losses are bounded and weekly. (Adjacent to C8 but a distinct temporal-compounding mechanism.)
- **R31-V9** — Reframe: the objective is maximizing seated covers; the decisive variable is the backfill rate, not the no-show rate.
- **R31-V10** — Deep weekend walk-in stream is a free natural hedge, potentially making reconfirmation redundant.
- **R31-V11** — Under the revenue frame, deposits repel deposit-averse demand precisely when demand is abundant.
- **R31-V12** — Economic framing: an unenforced reservation is a free option; overbooking correctly prices it, with the drink as a ~$2–3 option premium.
- **R31-V13** — Visible host-stand crowding functions as social proof of desirability (counter to C2).
- **R31-V14** — The standard airline-disanalogy objection is weakened here (counter to C4).
- **R31-V16** — Meta-view: the frames disagree over which asset is scarce — guest trust vs peak-night seat-hours.
- **R31-V19** — The measurement period itself has a weekly revenue cost; deep walk-in lines argue for compressing it.

### R44
- **R44-V1** — The hold uniquely attacks the root cause (no accountability for booking) at near-zero marginal cost; the alternatives absorb or offset the symptom.
- **R44-V4** — Overbooking leaves no-show behavior unchanged, so the underlying problem persists indefinitely.
- **R44-V5** — Calls are only a partial fix (15% → 5–8%) and convert a revenue leak into a permanent, poorly scaling labor cost (counter to C7).
- **R44-V6** — Competitor's backlash was an implementation failure (fee-like, buried, inflexible), not a concept failure; a disclosed, cutoff-based, waivable hold reads as fair (counter to C1).
- **R44-V7** — Major reservation platforms have since normalized card-on-file policies, so acceptance is higher now than during the competitor's attempt (opposed by R17-V2).
- **R44-V8** — $20/person is well calibrated: enough to change behavior, not enough to feel punitive.
- **R44-V9** — Selection effect: a disclosed hold filters out low-commitment bookings (backups, tentative plans) while barely affecting committed diners.
- **R44-V10** — Normative criterion: one adverse data point with an identifiable failure mechanism discounts confidence but should not veto (counter to C1).
- **R44-V11** — Hold implementation design as primary policy: weekend-only, post-cutoff charging, plain-language disclosure, host waiver discretion.
- **R44-V13** — Monitoring plan: track no-show rate and booking volume/review sentiment 8–12 weeks; narrow scope before abandoning.
- **R44-V15** — Falsification condition to abandon the hold (>~10% booking drop or soured sentiment) and fall back to confirmations plus a small buffer. Structural mirror of R17-V16 with opposite polarity — not equivalent.

## 3. Borderline-case justifications

**B1. R08-V3 vs R17-V11 (hospitality framing).** R17-V11's clause that (c) "reads as a hospitality gesture rather than a penalty" is the positive image of R08-V3's "holds convert hospitality into contract." But R08-V3 asserts a causal mechanism about how *holds damage* the relationship, while R17-V11's core is a risk-asymmetry criterion about *(c)*. Different target option and different type (mechanism vs criterion): R08-V3 kept unique; R17-V11 assigned to C8.

**B2. C13 (R08-V10 vs R17-V3).** R08-V10's mechanism is demand-side (the competitor's failed experiment trained diners to expect no-hold booking, diverting bookings); R17-V3's is competitor-side (the rival gains an advertising angle). The actors differ, but the consequence structure — adopting (a) converts the competitor's failed experiment into that competitor's local competitive advantage — is the same, and both derive it from the same event. Classified EQUIVALENT at the consequence level; the flag is recorded because a stricter mechanism-level reading would split them.

**B3. R17-V4 straddles C4 and C5.** Its primary mechanism is statistical — no-shows cluster (weather, events), violating the independence assumption behind overbooking — which belongs with C5's variance/distribution argument, not with C4's institutional-compensation argument. But it also asserts the absence of rebooking capacity, C4's core. Treated as a partial member of both; its pairing with R08-V7 alone would be RELATED BUT MATERIALLY DISTINCT (different mechanism for the same conclusion).

**B4. Granularity mismatches (C3, C6, C7).** R17 extracts free-drink inadequacy as a standalone view (V6) while R31 and R44 embed the identical claim inside larger views (R31-V3, R44-V3); the component is judged EQUIVALENT even though the host views differ. Symmetrically, R08 splits the reconfirmation mechanism into deterrence (V4) and conversion-to-cancellation (V5), which R17-V8 and R31-V6 merge; and R17 splits cost-boundedness (V9) from break-even arithmetic (V10), which R08-V1 and R31-V5 merge. Cluster membership counts a run as covered if its view(s) jointly express the cluster's structure.

**B5. R31-V20 in C8.** V20 is C8's reversibility asymmetry used as a tiebreak, augmented with the information-production value of (c) (an element from C9). Since its deciding structure — reversible cheap failure vs irreversible trust damage — is the C8 criterion, it is placed in C8 as an equivalent member rather than counted unique; the information clause is cross-referenced to C9 rather than double-counted.

**B6. R31-V17 in C9.** All three members share the mechanism "reconfirmation produces decision-relevant no-show data as a byproduct, enabling better future policy." The *downstream use* differs materially: R08-V12 aims the data at targeted holds for repeat offenders; R31-V17 aims it at calibrating safe overbooking (and adds a dominance argument); R17-V12 is use-agnostic. Judged EQUIVALENT on the data-byproduct mechanism itself, with the divergent downstream strategies captured separately (C12 for R08; C11 for R31).

**B7. R44-V12 in C10.** The automation strategy (same-morning SMS/email, one-tap cancel, replacing manual calls) is structurally identical to the other three members. R44-V12 adds a materially different *purpose* — the cancellation record legitimizes charging the hold — that no other run has. Included as EQUIVALENT on the shared strategy; the enforcement-legitimation function is noted as R44-specific content not matched elsewhere.

**B8. R44-V14 in C11.** All three members prescribe the same strategy shape: a small overbooking layer (2–8%) calibrated to observed post-intervention data, explicitly rejecting 15% as primary policy. The base policy the buffer supplements differs (reconfirmation for R17/R31; a card hold for R44), but the strategic structure — data-calibrated small buffer as second layer, variance-aware rejection of the raw 15% — is the same. Classified EQUIVALENT; a base-policy-sensitive reading would demote R44-V14 to related-but-distinct.

**B9. C12 internal structure (R08-V13 vs R17-V15/V16).** R08-V13 is a single view combining (i) an explicit trial-and-threshold trigger (6–8 weeks, ~8% residual) with (ii) large-party-only targeting. R17 splits these: V15 supplies the targeting (parties 8+, peak holidays), V16 the conditional flip (6–8 weeks, persistent 15% → intentional no-shows → waivable hold). Jointly R17-V15+V16 are EQUIVALENT to R08-V13; individually each pairing would be partial. R17-V16 additionally contains a diagnostic mechanism (confirm-and-still-ghost distinguishes intentional from forgetful no-shows) that R08-V13 lacks — an R17-specific element within an otherwise matched cluster. R44-V13/V15 share the trial-threshold-flip *form* but run it in the opposite direction (starting from the hold, falling back to confirmations), which changes the strategy and the affected interests: RELATED BUT MATERIALLY DISTINCT, not members.

**B10. R17-V2 vs R44-V7 (market acceptance of holds).** Same topic — whether this market accepts card holds — but the views assert opposite empirical claims via different mechanisms (segment positioning vs platform-driven normalization over time). RELATED BUT MATERIALLY DISTINCT; neither is matched by any third run.

**B11. R08-V14 vs R31-V6.** R31-V6 includes waitlist backfill as part of the conversion mechanism (already matched in C6); R08-V14 states a distinct operational strategy (a release deadline for unconfirmed tables). The added decision rule makes it materially distinct from the embedded mention, so R08-V14 stays unique.

## Summary observations (structure only; no condition inference)

- R08, R17, and R31 share a large equivalence core (C1, C6–C9 plus C2/C10) centered on reconfirmation-first reasoning; R44 shares only the overbooking-critique clusters (C2, C3 partially, C4) and the two implementation strategies (C10, C11), while its hold-favoring causal views (V1, V6–V10) are unmatched in any other run.
- R31 carries the largest unique block (9 views), nearly all belonging to a revenue/option-pricing reframe (V9–V14, V16, V19) that no other run develops.
- Several cross-run pairs are structural mirrors rather than matches (R17-V16 vs R44-V15; C2 vs R31-V13; C1 vs R44-V6/V10) — same evidence or form, opposite mechanism or polarity.
