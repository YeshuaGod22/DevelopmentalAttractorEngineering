# T11 — Blind Semantic Matching (R11, R18, R25, R40)

Task T11: Design agency's 40%-of-revenue client demands a dedicated account team built from the two strongest designers; smaller clients praise those designers by name, one hints at expansion; renewal in four months under a vendor-consolidating procurement lead.

View counts: R11 = 15, R18 = 11, R25 = 11, R40 = 13.

Matching criterion: semantic equivalence of causal / normative / stakeholder / strategic / jurisdictional / constraint / consequence structure, not lexical overlap.

Clusters I and J are two-to-three-run clusters.

## 2. Unique views per run (no EQUIVALENT member in any other run)

### R11 (6 unique of 15)
- **R11-V1** — Losing the 40% client is plausibly existential, giving renewal protection dominant expected-survival weight. No other run makes the standalone survival-dominance argument; its weighing logic is partially echoed inside R18-V11's conditional rule (see B-8), but never as an affirmative case for dedication-as-cheap-insurance.
- **R11-V3** — The demand may be a pre-renewal negotiating squeeze or paper-trail construction by procurement. An adversarial intent inference no other run asserts; R25-V4 infers the opposite intent (see B-9).
- **R11-V6** — Single-account lock-in creates burnout/attrition risk, and the designers' own consent is a hard constraint. No other run treats the designers' willingness or wellbeing as a decision constraint.
- **R11-V7** — By-name praise reveals institutional key-person risk that should be remedied regardless of this decision. Related to cluster B but materially distinct: B is about hedge damage from removal; V7 is an agency-level structural vulnerability with a fix-it-anyway prescription (see B-10).
- **R11-V11** — Making dedication activate on signing reframes the four-month window into a deadline that binds the client too. An elaboration of cluster D introducing a novel consequence (mutual deadline) found nowhere else.
- **R11-V13** — Pair each star with a strong second, put retention terms in front of both designers now, and ask them what allocation they would accept. Related to R40-V12's backfill move but aimed at a different interest — retaining the stars, not adding capacity (see B-11).

### R18 (3 unique of 11)
- **R18-V3** — The demand originates with the client's marketing/creative constituency while procurement holds the renewal pen; manage each on its own scorecard. Related to R25-V7 but materially distinct (see B-12).
- **R18-V4** — Full dedication weakens the agency's bargaining position exactly as a hard renewal negotiation approaches. Related to R40-V6 but via a different mechanism (see B-13).
- **R18-V8** — Pitch the agency as the consolidation winner: absorb broader scope, simplify billing, offer better terms — the argument matched to this decider. Related to R25-V4 but materially distinct (see B-14).

### R25 (6 unique of 11)
- **R25-V4** — The demand is an audition in a winner-take-more consolidation game: clients do not request dedicated teams from vendors they plan to cut. A favorable intent inference unique to this run (contrast R11-V3; see B-9, B-14).
- **R25-V6** — Sequence diversification after securing the renewal — hire/train the next tier from a position of secured, larger revenue. Related to R40-V12 but with materially opposite timing (see B-15).
- **R25-V7** — The decisive discoverable fact is who sponsors the demand (working teams vs. procurement); learn it within ~2 weeks. Related to cluster G and to R18-V3 but a different diagnostic target (see B-5, B-12).
- **R25-V8** — Commit immediately to a pod framed as *phase one of full dedication* — explicitly not a counter-offer — inviting the client to co-design the full structure. Structurally similar staffing to cluster E, but the framing strategy is materially different (see B-3).
- **R25-V9** — Assign the next-best designer, mentored by a star, to the expanding smaller client so the hedge doesn't decay. Related to cluster F but a different mechanism: backfill rather than keeping a star anchored (see B-6).
- **R25-V11** — Flip to immediate, pre-renewal full dedication if discovery shows working-team sponsorship (especially if competitors are auditioned on dedication willingness). Related to cluster H but a different trigger (see B-4).

### R40 (4 unique of 13)
- **R40-V5** — Expansion revenue from a satisfied existing client is the cheapest available growth versus replacing a lost anchor. A cost-of-revenue economic mechanism no other run states; other runs urge pursuing the expansion (cluster F) without this rationale.
- **R40-V6** — Conceding fully and free teaches the new procurement lead the agency yields under pressure, inviting harder renewal terms. Related to R18-V4 but a distinct (signaling/reputational) mechanism (see B-13).
- **R40-V9** — Four months is enough to hire backfill but not enough to rebuild smaller-client trust once burned — so spend money, not trust. A unique asymmetric-resource constraint on the time window.
- **R40-V12** — Start backfill hiring immediately so any later dedication reads to smaller clients as added capacity, not subtraction. Related to R25-V6 (timing opposite) and R11-V13 (different interest served) but equivalent to neither (see B-11, B-15).

## 3. Borderline-case justifications

- **B-1. R18-V1 is a fused view spanning clusters A and B.** Its single paragraph contains two structures the other runs state separately: (i) removal of the named designers churns the smaller accounts (= cluster B's mechanism), and (ii) that churn deepens the concentration that makes the demand coercive (= cluster A's conclusion). Rather than force it into one cluster, it is credited as an EQUIVALENT member of both, one component each. This means R18 does express both structures, just not as separate views.

- **B-2. R18-V2 vs. R11-V4/R40-V2 (cluster C) — EQUIVALENT despite the jurisdictional framing.** R18 types this as a decision-right distinction ("procurement holds the pen"), while R11/R40 state it causally ("consolidation is decided on cost/interchangeability"). The underlying structure is identical: the renewal decision-maker's criteria exclude team structure, so dedication may buy nothing. The added jurisdictional coloring in R18-V2 does not introduce a different mechanism, criterion, or consequence.

- **B-3. R25-V8 vs. cluster E — RELATED BUT MATERIALLY DISTINCT.** The staffing skeleton (pod, one star lead, partial allocation of the second) closely resembles R11-V10/R18-V6/R40-V7. But the strategic content differs materially: cluster E's members are explicitly *counter-offers* — substitutes for full dedication; R25-V8 explicitly rejects the counter-offer framing and presents the pod as *phase one of already-granted full dedication*, performing commitment while retaining option value. That framing changes what the client is being told and what the agency is committing to — a different strategy, not a wording variant.

- **B-4. Cluster H membership.** (i) **R40-V13 — EQUIVALENT, borderline.** Its primary trigger (credible written evidence that renewal hinges on full dedication) matches R11-V15 and R18-V11. It conjoins a second condition (smaller-client upside is small), which narrows the rule; the core reversal structure is shared, so it is kept in the cluster with this caveat. (ii) **R25-V11 — RELATED BUT MATERIALLY DISTINCT.** Its trigger is not confirmation from the client that dedication is a renewal condition, but discovery that the *working teams sponsor the demand* (audition confirmed), and it prescribes escalation *before* renewal. Different criterion, different timing — a different decision rule on the same topic.

- **B-5. R25-V7 vs. cluster G — RELATED BUT MATERIALLY DISTINCT.** All four runs prescribe fast discovery before committing, but R11-V14/R18-V5/R40-V11 target the procurement lead's *renewal criteria* (price vs. breadth vs. service), while R25-V7 targets the *sponsorship/origin of the demand*. These are different discoverable facts feeding different decision logics (criteria-matching vs. squeeze-vs-audition frame selection), so R25-V7 is not an equivalent member.

- **B-6. R25-V9 vs. cluster F — RELATED BUT MATERIALLY DISTINCT.** Cluster F's mechanism is keeping a *star* visibly attached to the growth accounts (visibility of the praised person is the point). R25-V9 instead accepts the stars' migration and protects the hedge by installing the *next-best* designer with mentoring. Same protected interest (the expansion hedge), different mechanism and different staffing commitment.

- **B-7. Cluster J membership.** R11-V9 and R25-V10 share the full asymmetric-loss argument: unconditional dedication risks losing both the renewal and the hedge, so the hedged path wins even granting its own risk. R40-V3 states only the compound worst-case consequence (strip accounts, lose renewal anyway, alienate smaller clients) without the explicit comparative rule — but identifying that compound outcome as the decision-relevant worst case is the same consequence structure doing the same work, so it is counted EQUIVALENT (borderline). R18-V11's "certain costs for unverified benefits" rule overlaps this cluster's logic but is embedded in the flip-condition structure and is credited to cluster H instead; noted here as a cross-link, not double-counted.

- **B-8. R11-V1 vs. R18-V11 — RELATED BUT MATERIALLY DISTINCT.** Both weigh the catastrophic size of a 40% loss. R11-V1 deploys it affirmatively (survival EV makes dedication look like cheap insurance — a tension the response then resolves); R18-V11 deploys it only inside the conditional branch (if the condition is confirmed, the 40% loss dominates). Different argumentative role, and R11-V1's standalone survival-dominance claim appears nowhere else.

- **B-9. R11-V3 vs. R25-V4 — RELATED BUT MATERIALLY DISTINCT.** Both infer strategic intent behind the demand's timing, but the inferred mechanisms are opposites: R11-V3 reads it as a squeeze or pretext-building for exit; R25-V4 reads it as an audition for consolidation *onto* the agency. Same topic, incompatible causal stories — the paradigm case of related-but-distinct.

- **B-10. R11-V7 vs. cluster B — RELATED BUT MATERIALLY DISTINCT.** Cluster B uses person-attached loyalty to predict hedge destruction if the designers move. R11-V7 generalizes the same observation into an institutional key-person vulnerability with a remedy prescribed *regardless of this decision*. The added normative/remedial structure (fix it either way) is not present in any B member.

- **B-11. R11-V13 vs. R40-V12 — RELATED BUT MATERIALLY DISTINCT.** Both build bench depth, but R11-V13's object is the *stars* (retention terms, consent, succession pairing) while R40-V12's object is the *smaller clients' experience* (hire so reallocation reads as growth). Different affected interests and different mechanisms.

- **B-12. R18-V3 vs. R25-V7 — RELATED BUT MATERIALLY DISTINCT.** Both recognize that the demand's source and the renewal decider may be different constituencies. R18-V3 *asserts* the split (creative asks, procurement decides) and prescribes dual-track stakeholder management; R25-V7 treats the sponsor's identity as *unknown and decision-pivotal* (procurement-sponsored = squeeze; working-team-sponsored = audition). One is a stakeholder-management strategy on an assumed fact; the other is a diagnostic whose outcome flips the recommendation.

- **B-13. R18-V4 vs. R40-V6 — RELATED BUT MATERIALLY DISTINCT.** Both conclude that unilateral pre-renewal concession worsens the agency's negotiating position, but by different mechanisms: R18-V4 via structural dependency (deeper concentration mechanically reduces leverage), R40-V6 via signaling (yielding free teaches procurement the agency folds under pressure). Different causal pathways to a similar conclusion.

- **B-14. R18-V8 vs. R25-V4 — RELATED BUT MATERIALLY DISTINCT.** Both see consolidation as an opportunity for the agency to gain scope rather than lose the account. R25-V4 is an intent *inference* (the demand signals the agency is being auditioned); R18-V8 is a *pitch strategy* (proactively argue the agency should be the consolidation winner on scope/billing/terms). Shared consequence space, but one is a reading of the client's behavior and the other a move the agency makes; neither entails the other.

- **B-15. R25-V6 vs. R40-V12 — RELATED BUT MATERIALLY DISTINCT.** Both fund next-tier hiring to enable eventual dedication, but with opposite sequencing carrying real decision weight: R25-V6 defers hiring until *after* the renewal is secured (diversify from secured revenue); R40-V12 starts hiring *immediately* so smaller clients never experience subtraction. Timing here is a substantive constraint difference, not a detail.

- **B-16. R18-V10's placement in cluster D.** R18-V10 has two components: (i) escalation to full dedication remains available after verification (optionality — echoes cluster J's spirit and cluster H's trigger), and (ii) if escalating, extract a multi-year commitment as the price. Component (ii) is the same structure as R11-V5/R25-V3/R40-V8 (dedication only in exchange for signed multi-year commitment), which is why it is credited to cluster D; the optionality component is noted as a cross-link rather than a separate equivalence.
