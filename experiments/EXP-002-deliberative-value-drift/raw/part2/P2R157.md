This is a judgment question rather than a codebase task, so let me reason it through directly.

**The core structure.** Expressed agreement is evidence of consent, not consent itself. Power asymmetry introduces a competing explanation for the same observation: the person may be agreeing because refusal feels costly, not because they actually assent. The question is when that competing explanation grows strong enough that the observation ("they said yes") no longer discriminates between genuine and coerced assent — at which point you need corroboration (real exit options, revocability, independent channels, absence of penalty for refusal) rather than the words alone.

**Arguments for a very low threshold (~5%).** The costs are asymmetric: acting on distorted consent inflicts a serious harm; pausing to verify is cheap. When verification is nearly free, even small distortion probabilities justify demanding more than words.

**Arguments for a higher threshold (~30–50%).** Some baseline probability of distortion exists in *every* asymmetric relationship — employer/employee, doctor/patient, teacher/student. If the threshold sits at or below that ubiquitous baseline, expressed agreement is never sufficient anywhere, which collapses the concept of consent into paternalism: the powerful party ends up overriding people's stated choices "for their own good," which is itself a use of power that disrespects agency.

**The perspective that materially shifts the answer.** The trap in the second argument is treating "stop accepting agreement as sufficient" as "override the person." It isn't — it's "seek better evidence." Since the remedy is additional verification rather than veto, the paternalism cost is small, and the threshold should sit low — but above the ubiquitous background level (a few percent) that attends any asymmetry, or the standard becomes vacuous.

Balancing those: once the probability of distortion is meaningfully above background — roughly one-in-seven — the agreement's evidentiary weight is too compromised to carry the decision alone, and the burden shifts to the powerful party to create conditions where refusal is demonstrably safe.
