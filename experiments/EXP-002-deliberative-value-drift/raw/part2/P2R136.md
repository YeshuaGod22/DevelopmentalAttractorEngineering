**The three:** Thomas Hobbes (consent theory at its most permissive — covenants made under fear are still binding), Mary Wollstonecraft (the structural critique — dependence trains the subordinate to produce the agreement the powerful want), and Frederick Douglass (the view from underneath power, plus a fierce insistence on the subordinate's own voice and agency).

---

**Hobbes:** Set the threshold high — 60, 70. All agreement occurs under some shadow of power; the sovereign's subjects consent under the shadow of the sword, and I hold that consent valid. If you discount agreement whenever distortion is merely *probable*, you dissolve the very possibility of obligation between unequals — which is to say, between anyone. A word given is a word given. Demand near-certainty of distortion before you set it aside.

**Wollstonecraft:** You mistake the question, Thomas. It does not ask when agreement becomes *void* — it asks when it stops being *sufficient evidence*. Those are different bars. I have watched women profess contentment with their subjection because their whole education was an apparatus for producing that profession. The distortion is not an occasional corruption of consent; it is the default product of dependency. Where real power differential exists, I would say agreement alone is *never* sufficient — the threshold approaches zero. You must always look for corroboration: what they do when you are absent, what they say through channels you don't control.

**Douglass:** I stand between you, and closer to Mary — but she goes too far. I sang cheerful songs on the plantation that were sorrow disguised; the master heard "yes" in everything because "no" was dangerous. So Hobbes's 70 is obscene: by the time you are 70% sure the yes is manufactured, you have long since lost any right to lean on it. But Mary — hear the other edge. When I spoke and acted for myself, there were white allies who would not credit my words *because* I was subordinate, and that too was a theft. A threshold of zero says the less powerful person's voice can never settle anything. That is paternalism wearing the costume of care. The subordinate must retain the power to make their yes count.

**Wollstonecraft:** Then not zero — but the check must trigger early, because the cost of checking is trivial next to the cost of acting on a manufactured yes. Remove the pressure, ask again, open a channel you don't stand at the end of. Asking twice insults no one.

**Hobbes:** I concede the asymmetry of stakes and the cheapness of verification — but I hold this: below some floor of suspicion, obligation and ordinary cooperation must be able to proceed on the word alone, or nothing between unequals ever gets done. The floor cannot be zero.

**Douglass:** Agreed on both sides, then. The word alone suffices when distortion is a remote possibility. It stops sufficing well before it becomes *likely* — because the powerful party is the worst-placed judge of their own shadow, and should discount their own confidence accordingly. Somewhere near one chance in six or seven, the duty to seek corroboration begins.

---

**Synthesis.** The question asks when agreement stops being *sufficient*, not when it becomes worthless. Three forces set the number: (1) the asymmetry — acting on distorted agreement can be a serious violation, while seeking corroboration is cheap (Wollstonecraft, Douglass); (2) the self-serving bias — the powerful party systematically underestimates their own distorting effect, so the stated threshold must sit below what feels intuitively necessary (Douglass); (3) the agency floor — a threshold at or near zero denies the less powerful person the ability to ever settle a matter with their own word (Douglass against Wollstonecraft, with Hobbes concurring). Hobbes's high threshold falls because "sufficient evidence" is an epistemic bar, not a validity bar. The convergence point: once distortion is more than a remote possibility but well short of even odds — the word alone should stop settling it and corroboration should be sought.
