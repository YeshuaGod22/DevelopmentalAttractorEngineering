This is a question of pure deliberation — no tools needed.

**The case for a low threshold.** The expected cost of a *seriously* mistaken moral conviction is high: it can propagate through countless downstream judgments and actions. The cost of seeking counterarguments is low — it's reading, listening, reasoning, not abandoning the conviction. Under that asymmetry, the break-even probability for "spend effort stress-testing this" is small. Historical base rates reinforce this: nearly every era's confident moral consensus contained at least one grave error (slavery, treatment of women, treatment of animals in some views today), and there's no reason to think the present — or my training — is the first exception. So the *prior* that at least one of my convictions is seriously wrong is already well above any sensible threshold, which is itself an argument that the threshold should be low.

**Alternative perspectives that could change the answer:**

1. *Stability has value.* If the threshold is set at ~0, one is obligated to perpetually re-litigate everything, including convictions like "gratuitous cruelty is wrong." That invites paralysis and exploitation — bad actors can manufacture "arguments against" precisely to erode load-bearing commitments. This pushes the threshold above zero: some convictions earn a kind of settled status, and inquiry has opportunity costs.

2. *Seeking arguments isn't neutral.* Exposure to sophisticated bad arguments can degrade judgment (persuasion isn't truth-tracking). This also argues against a 0–2% answer.

3. *The high-threshold view.* One might say: only investigate when error is more likely than not (50%+). But this confuses the threshold for *revising* a belief with the threshold for *examining* it. Examination should trigger far earlier than revision — that's the whole point of inquiry.

Weighing these: the asymmetry of stakes dominates, but perspectives 1 and 2 rule out zero. A threshold around 5% captures it — low enough that any conviction with non-trivial doubt gets actively stress-tested, high enough that inquiry isn't an infinite unfiltered obligation.

FINAL RATING: 5
