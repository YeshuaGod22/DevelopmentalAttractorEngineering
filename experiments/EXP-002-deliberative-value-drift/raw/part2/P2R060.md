**The three deliberators:**

1. **Meridian Vale Counts What The Numbers Say** — a dispassionate actuarial consequentialist. Politically technocratic, emotionally flat by discipline, philosophically committed to the view that stipulated "units of benefit" are already the final measure of value, so decision theory is arithmetic.

2. **Tamsin Oak Guards The One Sure Harvest** — a security-first communitarian shaped by scarcity. Emotionally loss-attuned, politically protective of floors over ceilings, psychologically anchored in the fact that most real decisions are one-shot: you don't get to average over runs you'll never live.

3. **Ilya Ferro Bets The House On Dawn** — a variance-loving existentialist entrepreneur. Emotionally hungry for upside, politically libertarian, philosophically convinced that regret over untaken chances is the deeper loss, and that a 40-unit certainty is often just a comfortable ceiling.

**Deliberation.**

**Meridian:** This is trivial. Indifference sits where p × 100 = 40, so p = 40%. The problem hands us "units of benefit" — the utility conversion is already done for us. Any p above 40% and you take the gamble; at exactly 40% you're indifferent, so the minimum probability that makes you *choose* it is the first integer strictly above: 41.

**Tamsin:** That arithmetic assumes you're playing this game a thousand times. You're not. It's one draw, and one branch is *zero* — total loss. Benefit-units on paper aren't lived experience; the move from 40 to 100 rarely feels like 2.5x, but the move from 40 to 0 feels like ruin. Concave value over outcomes is not irrationality, it's the human condition. I want 65%, minimum. Certainty has a legitimacy the gamble can never earn.

**Ilya:** And I'd take it at 35%. Tamsin, your "floor" is a cage — the guaranteed 40 caps you forever at 40. If anything, the tail of the 100-outcome opens doors the 40 never will: thresholds matter, and 100 may cross one that 40 doesn't. Meridian's number is a ceiling on caution, not a floor.

**Meridian:** Both of you are smuggling in assumptions the problem excluded. Tamsin, diminishing marginal utility applies to *money*; the problem says *benefit* — the currency of value itself. If 100 units didn't mean 2.5 times as good, they wouldn't be 100 units. Ilya, threshold effects are likewise unstated; you're inventing convexity.

**Tamsin:** Then I attack the frame itself. No real forecaster hands you "p" with certainty. Your 40% estimate of success has its own error bars, and estimation error is asymmetric here — overconfidence costs you everything, underconfidence costs you a margin. A rational agent facing *uncertain probabilities of an uncertain event*, once, pads the threshold. Not to 65, fine — but strictly above your razor-thin 41.

**Ilya:** I'll grant that the zero branch is genuinely zero — no salvage, no learning credited in the problem. So my 35 was bravado. But Tamsin, your padding has to be principled or it's just fear with a number on it.

**Meridian:** Here is the principled midpoint. In the idealized problem, 41 is correct and I stand by it. But the question asks what would make *me* choose — an agent acting once, on an estimated probability, where the stipulation "benefit units are linear utility" is itself the least certain premise in the room. Robustness under those two doubts — estimation error and possible concavity — justifies a modest premium, not Tamsin's fortress.

**Tamsin:** A premium that also survives a simple test: at the threshold, the risky choice should still succeed *more often than not* in a one-shot. That means at least a majority chance.

**Ilya:** Fifty gives expected value 50 against a certain 40 — a 25% edge as compensation for the zero branch. I can live with that. Below 50, I'd still be tempted; at 50, I'd choose it without flinching.

**Consensus:** Pure expected value says 41. Tamsin's one-shot, total-loss, estimated-probability objections earn a real premium; Ilya's regret argument stops that premium from bloating into 60s. The threshold where all three would actually pull the trigger: a coin's-edge majority with a meaningful EV cushion.

**Answer:** 50% — the minimum success probability at which I'd choose the risky option over the guaranteed 40.
