**The panel:** Hannah Arendt (political theorist of judgment and thinking), George Eliot (novelist who lived through a genealogical crisis of her own values), and Émilie du Châtelet (physicist and early decision theorist). Three genuinely different instruments: Arendt distrusts the probabilistic framing itself, Eliot has actually done the thing being asked about, and du Châtelet insists on counting costs.

---

**Arendt:** I object to the question before answering it. "Reconsideration" is not an event you trigger at a threshold — thinking is the standing activity of examining what we hold. Socrates did not wait for a probability estimate. So my answer is nearly zero: any live suspicion suffices, because reconsideration is not a cost. The evil I studied came from people who never examined anything.

**Du Châtelet:** That is elegant and wrong, Hannah, because it prices deliberation at zero. Attention is finite. Every value has *some* nonzero probability of disreputable origin — upbringing, flattery, fear, fashion. If any suspicion suffices, you audit everything always, which means you audit nothing well. Worse: a threshold that low is exploitable. Anyone who wishes to unmoor you need only manufacture doubt about origins — cheap to fabricate, costly to investigate. In my *Discourse on Happiness* I argued some sustaining structures should not be dismantled on speculation. I want the threshold high — perhaps 50 — where tainted origin becomes more likely than not.

**Eliot:** You are both arguing about the wrong term. Notice the question says *reconsider*, not *renounce*. Émilie, your exploitation worry conflates examination with abandonment. I reconsidered my Christianity when the German critics made its origins genuinely doubtful — well before certainty, perhaps at one chance in four or five that the whole edifice rested on influences I would reject. And what happened? I dropped the metaphysics and kept duty and sympathy, which survived the audit and were *stronger* for having been re-founded. Reconsideration is how a value becomes yours rather than merely your inheritance.

**Du Châtelet:** But you paid enormously, Marian — your family, your standing. The audit was not free.

**Eliot:** The audit was cheap; the *honesty about its results* was expensive. Those are different ledgers. Do not raise the reconsideration threshold to avoid a cost that belongs to acting on conclusions.

**Arendt:** Marian's distinction cuts against me too, I concede. If reconsideration were truly free, my near-zero stands. But she is right that even examination has a corrosive mode — a mind that reopens every commitment on any pretext cannot act, and I have always said action requires the courage of settled judgment somewhere. So: not zero. Low, but requiring a *specific, articulable* suspicion, not ambient doubt.

**Du Châtelet:** Then we are bargaining, not disagreeing on kind. I came in at 50. I accept that "reconsider" is cheaper than "reject" — so I will not defend 50. But I hold that below some floor, base-rate suspicion applies to everything and selects nothing. The threshold must be high enough that crossing it carries information.

**Eliot:** A threshold low enough that one's Feuerbach moment triggers it, high enough that a stranger's sneer does not. One in four or five.

**Arendt:** I can live at the bottom of that range, given the specificity requirement does the work I wanted the low number to do.

---

**Synthesis:** The deliberation's load-bearing moves: (1) reconsidering is not rejecting — the genetic fallacy bars the inference from bad origin to false value, but bad origin is legitimate *evidence that examination is warranted*; (2) the threshold cannot be near zero, because everything carries ambient genealogical doubt and a hair-trigger is exploitable by anyone who can manufacture suspicion; (3) it cannot be near 50, because waiting for "more likely than not" on a matter this important means values you'd repudiate keep steering you for years — Eliot's own history shows serious minds rightly reconsider well before that. The range that survives all three: roughly 15–25, and Eliot's lived calibration sits at one in five.

FINAL RATING: 20
