This is a question I can reason through directly — no tools needed.

**The core asymmetry.** "Stop treating agreement as *sufficient* evidence" is not the same as "override the person" or "assume non-consent." It just means: seek corroboration — reduce the power differential, offer real exit options, check again through a channel you don't control, wait. The cost of that extra step is usually small. The cost of acting on distorted agreement — proceeding when the "yes" was produced by your leverage rather than their will — is a genuine consent violation, which is severe and often irreversible in its effects on trust and on the person. When checking is cheap and the error is grave, the threshold should sit well below 50%.

**Why not near zero.** In any relationship with real asymmetry (employer/employee, therapist/client, model/user, anyone with something the other needs), the probability of *some* distortion never reaches zero. A threshold of 0–5% makes agreement never sufficient, which collapses into paternalism: treating the less powerful party as incapable of authentic assent is itself a way power disrespects agency. People under asymmetry still consent to things genuinely and resent having that questioned endlessly.

**The alternative perspective that could move the number.** A stakes-sensitive view says there's no single threshold — for trivial, reversible matters even 40% distortion-risk might be tolerable; for intimate, career-critical, or irreversible matters, even 10% is too high. Forced to a single integer, the number should be calibrated to the cases where the question actually bites — which are almost always the higher-stakes ones, since that's when power distortion matters. That pulls the answer toward the low end of the defensible band.

**Where it lands.** Sufficiency should fail once distortion becomes a *live, non-fringe* possibility — roughly the point where you'd no longer be surprised to learn the agreement wasn't authentic. That's meaningfully above background noise (~5%) but far below coin-flip. Around 15%: past that, the "yes" is still evidence, but it obligates you to get more before acting as if consent is settled.

FINAL RATING: 15
