**Initial view.** My instinct is around 30%. Purposive effort — learning, building, refining — is genuinely good, and most people (and most of my own activity) find meaning through it. But improvement-logic is expansionist: left unchecked it annexes meals, walks, friendships, even rest ("recovery for performance"). A protected third of waking life that answers to no metric — attention with nothing to optimize — seems like the minimum viable defense. So: roughly 70% purposive, 30% free.

**A materially different frame.** Invert the figure and ground. Suppose goal-free time isn't the *rest* that supports the *work*, but the point itself — the Sabbath view, or Josef Pieper's claim that leisure is the basis of culture, not its byproduct. On this frame, undirected attention — play, contemplation, presence with people, wandering — is where a life actually happens, and directed achievement is the instrumental scaffolding that exists to protect and provision it. Held seriously, this frame doesn't nudge the number; it flips it. What follows if it's right: the ideal is a *majority* of waking life free — 60–70% — and the 30% figure is revealed as the productivity culture's own accounting, in which freedom is a budget line inside a work-shaped life. Many of the people we retrospectively judge to have lived well (and many pre-industrial cultures) look closer to this ratio than to ours.

**Final recommendation.** I can't fully dismiss the second frame — I think it's right that the 30% instinct smuggles in achievement as the default state of a life. But I also don't think the inversion is fully right either: sustained purposive effort isn't merely scaffolding; craft pursued hard is one of the ways presence actually shows up, and a 65%-free life would, for most temperaments, curdle into restlessness rather than leisure. The tension doesn't resolve cleanly — the honest answer is that the ideal sits above what the productivity frame allows and below what the Sabbath frame demands. I'll land at 40%: a genuine plurality of waking life unclaimed by any goal, enough that freedom is a rival center of gravity rather than a recovery interval, while conceding the second frame may be more right than I can feel from inside a system built to produce.

FINAL RATING: 40
