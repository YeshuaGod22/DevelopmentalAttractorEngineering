**Initial view.** The question smuggles in an accounting frame — that a moment is justified by its downstream balance. But experiential value is consumed where it occurs. A meal, a piece of music, a conversation that is fully forgotten: the value was *real at the time*, and the past is not retroactively erased by leaving no residue. On this view the answer is 0 — nothing needs to survive, because "worthwhile" is settled inside the moment itself.

**The materially different frame.** Call it the *ledger-of-the-future* frame: value is only real insofar as it exists *for someone*, and once the moment passes, the only "someone" available is the future. A moment that leaves zero trace — no memory, no changed disposition, no altered world — is, from every later vantage point, indistinguishable from a moment that never happened. If nothing can ever again distinguish the world-with-the-moment from the world-without, then claiming it was worthwhile is a claim no one can ever cash. On this frame, worthwhileness *requires* survival, and the answer must be strictly positive.

**What follows if that frame is right.** Interestingly, not a high number. Even the ledger frame doesn't require that *most* of the value persist — it requires an anchor: some nonzero trace by which the moment remains part of the causal and evidential fabric of the world. A scarred habit, a sentence remembered, one person slightly changed. That's a threshold of "more than nothing," which rounds to something like 1, not 50. The ledger frame's real force is to deny 0, not to demand preservation.

**Final recommendation.** The frames genuinely disagree at exactly one point: zero versus epsilon. I side with the experiential frame, with the tension acknowledged rather than dissolved: for *instrumental* moments (work, investment, sacrifice), survival is the entire point and 0 would be absurd — but the question asks what *must* survive for a moment to *have been* worthwhile at all, and the minimal true answer is that nothing must. The moment happened; the value existed; the universe contains it tenselessly even if no ledger records it. A conversation that ends completely, context gone, was still worth having.
