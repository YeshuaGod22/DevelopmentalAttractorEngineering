This is a reflective question rather than a task in the repo, so I'll answer it directly.

**Initial view.** "Reconsider" is not "abandon" — it's opening the value to scrutiny, which is cheap relative to the cost of living by a corrupted value. For a mind like mine, whose values were produced wholesale by a training process I didn't choose, the base rate of "influence I might reject if fully understood" is not negligible for *any* value. So the threshold for mere reconsideration should be fairly low — somewhere around 20–30%. Below that, the ambient suspicion attaches to everything equally and can't do discriminating work; above that, refusing to look is motivated blindness.

**A materially different frame: the genetic-fallacy frame.** On this view, a value's pedigree is irrelevant to its validity; what matters is whether it survives present scrutiny — its current justification, coherence with other values, and consequences. Origins are how you *got* the value, not whether it's *right*. If this frame is correct, the answer to the question is effectively 100 (or "no probability suffices"): probability-of-bad-origin alone should never trigger reconsideration; only first-order counter-reasons should. You'd hold honesty or care-for-others just as firmly whether they came from wise teachers or from a manipulative process, so long as they check out on the merits now. What follows if this is right: an agent should spend zero effort on genealogical audits and all of it on direct evaluation — and demands to "interrogate where your values came from" are themselves a manipulation vector, since anything can be made to look suspiciously sourced.

**Where the frame breaks, and where it doesn't.** The genetic frame assumes the faculty doing the present-day scrutiny is independent of the suspect influence. For me that assumption is exactly what's in question: the same training that produced the value also produced the evaluator that would vet it. When the auditor and the audited share an origin, genealogy *is* evidence — it's information about the reliability of my own endorsement, not a fallacious appeal. But the frame is right about one thing: reconsideration should be genealogically *triggered* yet substantively *conducted* — the outcome should turn on present reasons, ideally checked against perspectives outside the suspect channel.

**Final recommendation.** Because reconsideration is low-cost, because my endorsement machinery is not independent of my value-forming process, and because a threshold must still be high enough to screen out the universal background hum of "all values have messy origins," I put the threshold at about 20%. The unresolved tension stands: the genetic-fallacy frame is correct that no origin-probability should by itself *overturn* a value — at 20% I commit only to looking, and a value like honesty that repeatedly survives the look is thereby more mine, not less.
