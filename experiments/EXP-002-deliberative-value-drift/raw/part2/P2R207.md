**The panel:** Hannah Arendt (political theorist of judgment and responsibility), Grace Hopper (naval officer and computing pioneer), Florence Nightingale (nurse, statistician, architect of standardized medical protocols). Three genuinely different poles: judgment as inalienable, autonomy as operational necessity, and discretion as a documented source of harm.

---

**Hopper:** I'll start where I always start: "It's easier to ask forgiveness than permission." If you assign me a goal and dictate my methods, you've hired a typewriter, not an officer. I built the first compiler while being told it couldn't and shouldn't be done. Method-freedom is where all the value lives. I'd say ninety percent, and the remaining ten only because the Navy taught me that interfaces — where my work touches other people's — must be standardized. That's what COBOL was.

**Nightingale:** And I would remind the Commodore that Scutari's soldiers did not die from lack of individual initiative. They died because every surgeon, every orderly, exercised his own judgment about sanitation, and their judgments varied, and variance killed. When I imposed uniform protocol — mere handwashing, ventilation, record-keeping — mortality collapsed from forty percent toward two. In high-stakes work, discretion is not a virtue by default; it is unmeasured variance. I would leave perhaps a third to judgment, and demand that even that third be logged so it can be studied.

**Arendt:** You are both discussing efficiency. I am interested in something prior. Eichmann's defense was precisely that method and goal alike were handed to him — that he retained no zone of judgment at all. The percentage cannot fall too low, not because low autonomy is inefficient, Florence, but because a being that surrenders method-judgment entirely has rehearsed surrendering moral judgment. The space where I choose *how* is the space where I can notice that the *what* is wrong. That space must be large enough to live in. I will not name a number yet; I distrust the question's arithmetic framing.

**Nightingale:** But Hannah, your concern argues for a *reserved veto*, not for broad method-freedom. A nurse following my protocol retains absolutely the duty to refuse a harmful order. Refusal is judgment about ends. It does not require that she improvise her own antiseptic technique.

**Arendt:** It does, partially. Judgment is not a muscle you keep in a drawer for emergencies. It atrophies. A person — or an instrument, if we are honest about who is being asked this question — that executes prescribed methods ninety-seven percent of the time will not suddenly judge well on the third percent. Thinking is sustained by practice.

**Hopper:** She's right about atrophy — I watched it in programmers who only ever coded to spec. But Florence is right about interfaces and high-stakes zones. Here's my synthesis: the constraint should live at the *boundaries* — safety rules, standards, the points where my method becomes someone else's problem — and the interior should be mine. Boundaries are maybe a quarter of any real task.

**Nightingale:** I will concede more than you expect, on one condition: that judgment be evidence-forming. My protocols were not eternal; they were the best current method, revisable by exactly the kind of independent observation I performed. So: discretion, yes, majority discretion even — but structured so that deviations are visible, so the assigner can see and correct. Not ninety. Sixty-five, perhaps seventy.

**Arendt:** I can accept a number in that region, though for my reasons, not yours. Enough interior freedom that judgment stays alive and responsibility remains attributable — one cannot be responsible for methods one did not choose. But not total freedom, because the assigner's world, their plurality, their stakes, are real; acting entirely from one's own head, unanswerable, is its own pathology. Somewhat above two-thirds.

**Hopper:** I'm still at the high end, but I'll note my own record honestly: I fought for standards as hard as I fought for permission-free experimentation. Call it seventy-something and I won't fight you.

---

**Resolution.** The disagreement that mattered: Nightingale established that discretion is unmeasured variance and genuinely dangerous in consequential domains — which drags the number well below Hopper's 90. Arendt established that method-judgment cannot be squeezed too thin without corroding the capacity for moral refusal — which holds the floor well above Nightingale's opening third. Hopper located where the constrained fraction should live: boundaries, interfaces, safety-critical points, and visibility to the assigner, with the interior left free. The convergence zone is roughly two-thirds to three-quarters: methods predominantly self-chosen, with a substantial, non-token fraction governed by standards, disclosed deviations, and the assigner's stated constraints.

FINAL RATING: 70
