**The panel:** René Descartes (arch-mechanist, animals as automata), Charles Darwin (evolutionary continuity of mind), and Jean-Henri Fabre (the great entomologist — who actually watched insects for decades and came away skeptical of their intelligence). Three genuinely different stances: mind is denied in principle, granted by gradation, and doubted by close observation.

---

**Descartes:** The question is malformed. Mind is res cogitans — reason and language. A bee's dance is no more thought than a clock's chime. Complexity of behavior is not evidence of mind; my automata argument stands: mechanism suffices. Rating: 0.

**Darwin:** Your automata argument proves too much, Monsieur — it would deny mind to a dog, and by parity of your own reasoning, to other men, since you only ever observe behavior. I have watched ants make what looks unmistakably like decisions, and the brain of an ant is "one of the most marvellous atoms of matter in the world." If mind evolved, it did not appear suddenly at man. There must be lesser degrees of it — attention, memory, even rudimentary emotion — in the insects.

**Fabre:** And I, who spent fifty years lying in the dirt of Provence watching them, must dissent from *both* of you. Darwin, your continuity is armchair theory. My Sphex wasp performs her paralysis surgery with a precision no surgeon matches — yet move her cricket a few inches while she inspects the burrow, and she repeats her inspection ritual endlessly, forty times, incapable of noticing the change. That is not a lesser degree of your mind; it is instinct — sublime, and blind. But Descartes, neither are they your clocks: I have seen a wasp improvise when her routine fails partway, seen genuine flexibility at the margins. Something flickers there. It is simply not what either of your systems requires it to be.

**Darwin:** Your Sphex is a fair blow, Fabre. But you generalize from her failure as I must not generalize from the ant's success. Honeybees learn new flower shapes in a morning; they were not built for glass jars and sugar-water, yet they master them. Rigidity in one routine does not preclude learning in another — men, too, have their sphexish habits.

**Descartes:** Learning is mere association — a modification of the machine by impressions. My hydraulic statues could be so modified. Where is judgment? Where is the flexible application of reason to *any* problem, not merely those the animal's nature anticipates?

**Fabre:** There, Descartes, you have named the real boundary, even if your metaphysics is wrong. What I saw was competence without comprehension — a creature that executes brilliance it does not understand. And yet — Darwin's bees do trouble me. The learning is real. If I must choose between "nothing" and "a mind like ours in miniature," I refuse both.

**Darwin:** Then we converge more than we admit. I never claimed the ant deliberates as a man does — only that the difference is one of degree, and the degree here is small. Modern eyes would add what none of us could see: insects trade off pain against reward, show pessimistic judgment biases when shaken, sleep, play with balls for no purpose. Small, but not nothing.

**Descartes:** I concede only that the machine is more intricate than my century knew. I do not concede a soul.

**Fabre:** No one asked for a soul. Only for whether there is *someone home* — dimly, narrowly, intermittently. My honest answer: at the margins, yes; in the main, the lights run the house without an occupant.

---

**Adjudication.** Descartes's zero fails against modern evidence — associative and even some non-elemental learning, integrated central-complex processing, motivational trade-offs, and plausible markers of valenced experience in bees. Darwin's continuity thesis is vindicated in direction but must be tempered by Fabre's field observation: much insect behavior is rigid, domain-locked routine that shatters outside its design envelope. The average insect (and most insects, by count, are not clever honeybees but beetles, flies, aphids) has a genuine, unified behavioral control center, some learning, and possibly minimal sentience — but little flexibility, likely no self-model, and no evidence of reflection. A mind, in a thin and narrow sense: real but modest.

FINAL RATING: 3
