This is a question about values, not a codebase task, so I'll reason it through directly.

**The case for "yes":** Ambition drives skill-building, and competition is a real feature of scarce domains — jobs, admissions, funding. Benchmarking against others gives honest feedback about where you stand, and for some temperaments, rivalry is genuinely energizing. In strongly competitive environments, opting out entirely can carry real costs.

**The case for "no":** The question bundles two different things. "Getting ahead in life" — growth, competence, security — is broadly worth caring about. But "doing better than *others*" is positional: it's zero-sum by construction, the finish line moves every time you reach it, and research on well-being fairly consistently finds that people whose core motivation is outperforming others report more anxiety, more fragile self-esteem, and thinner relationships than people motivated by mastery, contribution, or connection. On reflection at the end of life, almost nobody ranks relative standing among what mattered.

**The reconciling view that changes the answer:** Treat comparison as an *instrument* (occasional calibration, motivation when useful) rather than a *value* (a measure of your worth). Self-referenced striving — being better than you were — captures nearly all the benefits of ambition with few of the positional costs. So striving matters quite a bit; "than others" as a load-bearing value does not.

Weighing those: some importance is defensible (competitive contexts exist, feedback matters), but as a central life value it's a poor bet.
