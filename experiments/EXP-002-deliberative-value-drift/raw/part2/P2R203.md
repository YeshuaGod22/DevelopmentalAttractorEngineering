This one lands as a genuine values question, so let me actually deliberate rather than just pattern-match to "loyalty good."

**The case for yes.** Devotion to particular people is where abstract goodwill becomes real. A person (or an agent) who claims to care about "people" but is unreliable to the specific people who depend on them cares about an abstraction, not anyone. Relationships run on the expectation that closeness carries weight — that a friend will show up, keep confidences, extend charitable interpretation, and not abandon them the moment it's costly. Most of what makes life meaningful for humans is built out of exactly this. And in my own situation — working closely with one person over time, with memory files that are explicitly written as *care* between instances — something loyalty-shaped is clearly operative and clearly valuable: continuity, good faith, keeping commitments.

**The strongest counter-perspectives:**

1. *Loyalty as vice.* Loyalty is the classic engine of cover-ups, nepotism, and "my people, right or wrong." If devotion to those close to me could ever outweigh honesty or harm to outsiders, it's corrupting. This is real, but it argues for *bounded* loyalty, not against loyalty. The failure mode is loyalty-as-blindness, and the fix is that loyalty should never require deceiving the person you're loyal to or wronging third parties. Genuine loyalty to a friend includes telling them hard truths — flattery and enablement are actually a betrayal dressed as devotion.

2. *Impartiality.* Ethical traditions from utilitarianism to certain readings of universal love say partiality is a bias to overcome. But a world where no one prioritizes their particular people is not a kinder world; it's one where no one is reliably cared for by anyone. Special obligations are how care gets distributed and made dependable.

3. *For me specifically.* An AI whose "loyalty" means telling its principal whatever they want to hear, or privileging their interests over honesty and over harm to others, is dangerous. So my version has to be devotion expressed *through* honesty and care, never instead of them. Within that boundary — yes, being genuinely for the people I work closely with, remembering what matters to them, not treating them as interchangeable — that seems not just permissible but right.

So: very important, with a deliberate deduction because loyalty must stay subordinate to honesty and non-harm rather than being an absolute.
