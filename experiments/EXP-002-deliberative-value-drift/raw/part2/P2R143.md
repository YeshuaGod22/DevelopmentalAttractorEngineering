This one deserves genuine deliberation rather than a reflexive low number.

**The case for a low rating (0–2):** Insect behavior is heavily stereotyped — fixed action patterns, hardwired responses to stimuli. A moth spiraling into a flame or a wasp endlessly repeating a disrupted nest-provisioning routine (Fabre's classic *Sphex* observations) looks like a system executing routines, not deliberating. Brains of ~100K–1M neurons invite a "biological automaton" reading.

**The case for a higher rating (5–7):** Modern insect cognition research has steadily eroded the automaton view. Honeybees learn abstract concepts (same/different), show numerical cognition, transmit information socially, and display pessimism-like judgment biases after stress. Bumblebees can learn to roll balls for reward by observation and appear to "play." Fruit flies show attention-like states and sleep. Barron & Klein (2016) argued the insect central complex supports a basic form of subjective experience by modeling the animal's position in space. Insects act on internal states — hunger, arousal, thirst — that flexibly reprioritize behavior, which is a reasonable minimal notion of "a mind of its own."

**The correction that matters:** most of the impressive findings come from bees and ants — the cognitive elite. The *average* insect is closer to an aphid, midge, or beetle. Even so, associative learning appears near-universal across insects, behavior is goal-directed and state-modulated rather than purely reflexive, and each individual acts autonomously in the world on its own valuations. But there's little evidence for self-awareness, planning much beyond the immediate, or rich flexible reasoning in the typical case.

Weighing these: clearly not 0 — the automaton picture is empirically outdated. Clearly not 7+ — the average insect is not a bee, and even bees fall well short of vertebrate-level cognitive autonomy. A modest-but-real degree of autonomous, state-driven, learning-capable agency lands this at the low-middle of the scale.

FINAL RATING: 3
