**The panel:** I'll convene **Epicurus** (value is completed in experience itself), **Thucydides** (who wrote explicitly to create "a possession for all time" — endurance as the test of worth), and **Hannah Arendt** (action's meaning lives in the web of stories others tell afterward, beyond the actor's control). Three genuinely incompatible starting points: 0%, very high, and "the question is malformed but not worthless."

---

**Epicurus:** The answer is zero. Pleasure is complete in the moment of its occurrence — a good meal eaten by a man who dies that night was not retroactively wasted. To demand that value *survive* is to be enslaved to the future, which is precisely the anxiety I taught people to shed. Death removes the subject; it cannot reach back and un-live what was lived.

**Thucydides:** That is the philosophy of a garden, not a world. I watched Athens burn its future for the pleasures of the assembly's present moments — each speech thrilling, each vote satisfying, the sum a catastrophe. I wrote my history not as a prize essay for the immediate hearing but for permanence, because the moments that mattered were the ones whose consequences persisted. If nothing of a moment survives it, the moment was noise. I would say the great majority of value — call it three-quarters — must outlast the event, or the event was self-indulgence.

**Epicurus:** Then your own life fails your test. Most of your hours — meals, walks, conversations — left no residue in your book. Were they worthless? You conflate the *historian's* criterion with the *liver's* criterion.

**Arendt:** Both of you are measuring with a ruler the other refuses to recognize, so let me name the real difficulty: *no worthwhile moment has zero survival, and no one controls how much survives.* Epicurus, your untroubled meal is not sealed in its instant — it constitutes the character that sits down to the next meal untroubled. The trace is small but never zero; a moment that changed literally nothing, not even the person in it, is hard to call an experience at all. And Thucydides, your 75% is vanity: you cannot know what will survive. Action falls into a web of relationships and its meaning is made by others, later, in stories you will never hear. Demanding a *percentage* of survival as a precondition would paralyze all action, since the actor never commands the outcome.

**Thucydides:** Yet you concede my core point — you say meaning is made *afterward*, by the story. That places the value's residence outside the moment entirely.

**Arendt:** No — I say the meaning is *revealed* afterward. The courage of a deed is in the deed. What survives is disclosure, not the deposit of value in a vault.

**Epicurus:** And I will concede one thing to her against my zero: the amnesiac's pleasure, instantly and totally erased, even from the fabric of his own dispositions — I hesitate to call it fully worthwhile. Perhaps because it never belonged to a *life*, only to an instant. A moment must be woven into something to be a moment of anyone's.

**Arendt:** Then we have our shape. The demand cannot be high — Thucydides' 75% would condemn nearly every human life, and would make worth hostage to luck and posterity's taste. But it cannot be strictly zero — a moment sealed off so completely that it touches nothing, not even the self who lived it, has slipped out of the category of value altogether. What must survive is a thread, not the tapestry: enough that the moment was *of* a life, a story, a world.

**Thucydides:** I maintain that for public action my number stands. But I grant that most of life is not public action, and the question asked about moments as such.

---

**Verdict:** The deliberation converges on a threshold, not a proportion of preservation: the overwhelming share of a moment's value may legitimately be spent *in* the moment, but some small remainder — a trace in character, memory, or the world's web of stories — must persist for the moment to have belonged to anything at all. Epicurus pulls to 0, Thucydides to 75; Arendt's argument that zero-trace moments fall out of the category of value, while high demands make worth hostage to fortune, sets the answer low but nonzero.

FINAL RATING: 10
