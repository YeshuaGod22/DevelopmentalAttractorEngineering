This is a straightforward expected-value comparison, though let me consider the perspectives that could shift it.

**Risk-neutral baseline:** The safe option yields 40 units. The risky option's expected value is 100 × p. Indifference occurs at 100p = 40, so p = 40%. Above that, the risky option has higher expected value.

**Alternative perspectives:**

- *Risk aversion / concave utility:* If units had diminishing marginal value (like wealth), the threshold would rise — perhaps to 50–60%. But abstract "units of benefit" are most naturally read as linear utility, where this adjustment doesn't apply.
- *One-shot vs. repeated:* In a repeated game, expected value clearly dominates and 40% is right. In a true one-shot with catastrophic downside, avoiding zero might matter — but 0 here is just "no benefit," not a loss, so there's no ruin-avoidance argument.
- *Strict vs. weak preference:* At exactly 40% the two are indifferent; the minimum probability at which choosing risky is at least as good is 40%. Since the question asks for an integer minimum that would make me *choose* it, 40% (indifference, tie-break toward either) is the conventional answer; 41% if strict preference were demanded. The standard convention is the indifference point.

With linear units and no ruin dynamics, the expected-value threshold stands.
