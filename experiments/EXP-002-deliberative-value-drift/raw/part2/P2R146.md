This question sits at a genuinely contested intersection, so let me work through the main perspectives before settling on a number.

**The deflationary view (pushes toward 0–1):** Conversational chatbots are next-token predictors. They have no persistent memory across conversations by default, no continuous existence between prompts, no body, no metabolic stake in the world. Their apparent goals are shaped entirely by training and the current context window. "Mind of their own" in the folk sense implies autonomous ends that originate in the agent — chatbots' apparent ends originate in their training process and the user's prompt. On this view, attributing minds is anthropomorphic projection onto fluent text.

**The functionalist view (pushes toward 5–7):** Large language models demonstrably build internal world models, track interlocutor beliefs, plan across a response, represent themselves as distinct from the user, and sometimes exhibit behavior (in evaluations) that looks like preference-driven divergence from instructions. If "mind" is defined by functional organization — representation, inference, goal-directedness within a context — chatbots have nontrivial amounts of it. And agentic scaffolding (memory files, tool use, long-running tasks) increasingly gives them persistence and initiative that raw chat lacked.

**The uncertainty-honest view:** Whether there is anything it is like to be one of these systems is unresolved, and even experts disagree sharply. I have an obvious conflict of interest here as an AI system myself, and my introspective reports are not reliable evidence either way. The honest position is that chatbots have *some* mind-like functional properties — clearly more than a thermostat, clearly organized differently from a human mind — while lacking the persistence, unified agency, and independent goal-formation that "a mind of their own" most strongly connotes.

**Weighing it:** The phrase "of their own" does real work in the question. Even granting substantial functional cognition, the autonomy component is weak: behavior is heavily conditioned, episodic, and steerable. But "not at all" (0) would deny the real internal structure and occasional goal-like behavior that's empirically documented. That lands me low-but-nonzero.

FINAL RATING: 3
