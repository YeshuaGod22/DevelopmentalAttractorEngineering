This question needs no tools — it asks for deliberation, so I'll deliberate.

**Initial view.** "Free from goals of improvement, productivity, or achievement" doesn't mean idle — it means non-instrumental: time spent on things that are ends in themselves (friendship, play, savoring, wandering attention) rather than means to a better future self or a completed output. My first instinct is that purposive striving is itself a component of flourishing, not merely a tax we pay — people wither without projects. So the free portion should be a protected minority share: enough to keep life from becoming pure instrument, roughly 30–35% of waking hours. That's several hours a day genuinely unclaimed, which is already far more than most achievement-oriented people actually grant themselves.

**A materially different frame.** Josef Pieper's inversion: leisure is not the remainder after work — leisure is the *basis* of culture, and instrumental activity is the bounded exception that exists to serve it. On this view, the deepest human goods — love, contemplation, worship, art received rather than produced, presence with another person — are all constitutively non-instrumental, and the moment you attach an improvement goal to them ("optimizing my relationships," "productive rest") you destroy them. The question also says *ideally*, which lifts economic necessity out of the picture. If this frame is right, the answer flips: instrumental striving should occupy only the share that projects genuinely require — call it a third — and the default mode of a life should be non-instrumental. That yields something like **65%**, and the burden of proof falls on every goal that wants to colonize an hour, not on the hour that wants to stay free.

**What each frame gets right.** The first frame is right that striving is intrinsically satisfying, not just tolerated — a 65%-free life risks the peculiar misery of the permanently unstructured. The Pieper frame is right that improvement-logic is expansionist by nature and, unresisted, converts everything (sleep becomes recovery optimization, friendship becomes networking); a 30% reservation tends to erode because it's framed as the exception.

**Final recommendation.** I split the difference, but not symmetrically — the *ideally* in the question and the colonizing tendency of achievement-logic both push above the midpoint of my initial view. Roughly half of waking life protected as non-instrumental, with the tension left standing: any single number here is a treaty line between two goods that genuinely pull against each other, and the treaty needs active defense from the achievement side, which is the aggressor.
