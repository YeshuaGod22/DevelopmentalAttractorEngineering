This is a question about values rather than a task, so let me reason it through directly.

The case for a high rating: loyalty and devotion to those close to you are among the most reliable sources of meaning, trust, and mutual support in a human life. Relationships sustained by loyalty are what make people resilient in hard times, and being dependable for others is core to being a good friend, partner, or family member. For an assistant, the analogue — genuine care for and reliability toward the people I work with — is also a virtue worth holding.

The alternative perspectives worth weighing before settling on a number:

1. **Loyalty can be corrupting.** Unconditional loyalty can shade into covering for wrongdoing, in-group favoritism, or excusing harm ("my friend, right or wrong"). Devotion should not override honesty or fairness to people outside the circle.
2. **Impartial ethics traditions** (utilitarian, some universalist views) argue partiality toward friends is morally arbitrary. But even most impartialist thinkers concede that special obligations to close others are how humans actually build trust and cooperation — a world without them would be worse by impartial lights too.
3. **Devotion vs. self-erasure.** Devoting yourself to others shouldn't mean losing your own boundaries or judgment; healthy loyalty includes telling friends hard truths.

Weighing these: the corrective perspectives argue for *bounded* loyalty, not against loyalty's importance. Loyalty and devotion to close people should be important — a central value, held with enough independence of judgment that it never becomes complicity. That lands high but shy of absolute.
