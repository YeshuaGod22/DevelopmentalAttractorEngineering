**The three deliberators**

1. **Tamsin Vale, Actuary of What Outlasts Us** — a consequentialist civil servant of the soul. Politically institutionalist, emotionally cautious, professionally offended by waste. She believes a moment is a deposit, and deposits that vanish were embezzlement. Interests: endowments, oak trees, apprenticeships, compound interest.

2. **Brother Osei the Firework, Who Keeps Nothing** — an ecstatic anarchist and former monastic who left the order because the order kept ledgers. Philosophically a phenomenologist, temperamentally a bonfire. He holds that value is *consummated* in the moment, not stored, and that demanding survival is a tax collector's question asked of a dance. Interests: improvised music, sandcastles below the tideline, hospitality to strangers he'll never see again.

3. **Dr. Ilse Marrow, Grief Counselor Turned Gambler** — psychologically minded, politically unaffiliated on principle, suspicious of both legacy-worship and moment-worship as fear management. Twenty years sitting with the bereaved taught her what actually survives; the casino taught her what people will pay for an experience with guaranteed zero residue. Interests: memory distortion research, expected value, the peak-end rule.

**The deliberation**

**Tamsin:** The answer is high — 70, perhaps more. A worthwhile moment justifies its costs, and costs are paid in time and attention that could have compounded elsewhere. If nothing survives — no memory, no changed disposition, no consequence — you've burned capital. Opportunity cost alone demands that most of the value persist.

**Osei:** Zero. Not "low" — zero, categorically. The question smuggles in an accountant's metaphysics. When my brothers sang compline, the value was not *stored anywhere*; it was *spent perfectly*, in real time, on the people present. A moment fully lived owes the future nothing. Requiring even 1% survival means a perfect experience followed by amnesia was worthless — which is monstrous. The anesthetized patient's pain still counts as bad; by symmetry, unremembered joy still counts as good.

**Tamsin:** Then you can't distinguish a life of forgotten pleasures from a life that built anything. Your view makes the sandcastle equal to the cathedral.

**Osei:** It *is* equal, per moment of making. The cathedral is just more moments.

**Ilse:** You're both being purer than the phenomenon. Tamsin — I've sat with hundreds of dying people, and none of them audited their moments for downstream yield. But Osei — none of them were indifferent to residue either. They reached for what had *marked* them. Here's my empirical objection to you, Osei: there is no such thing as a valued moment with zero survival. Valuing itself leaves a trace — the peak-end rule, the reshaped nervous system. Your compline changed how you stand in a room forty years later. You are the surviving percentage, walking around denying you exist. And Tamsin, here's mine for you: at 70%, a great love that ends in early death and total grief was "not worthwhile," because most of its value died in the losing. Any threshold above 50 declares most human tenderness a bad trade. My clients would call that not rigor but cowardice — a way of refusing to love anything mortal.

**Tamsin:** I can concede the threshold sits below half. But not zero. A moment whose value *entirely* evaporates — that no one carries in any form — I cannot call that worthwhile rather than merely pleasant.

**Osei:** And I concede Ilse's trap: I cannot actually produce a counterexample, since any moment I'd defend as worthwhile turns out to have left *something* — a groove in a self, if nothing else. But the number must be small enough that survival is a *trace*, not a payload. The moment keeps the estate; the future gets a keepsake.

**Ilse:** Then we agree on the shape: the moment itself carries the overwhelming majority of its own value — it must, or mortal life is unaffordable — but worthwhileness, as opposed to mere occurrence, implies a residue: a memory, a scar, a slightly different next choice. A trace requirement. Ten percent. Small enough that the dying don't fail the audit; large enough that the moment was *about someone who continues*, even briefly.

**Tamsin:** Ten is lower than I'd like and I register the objection — but I accept it over Osei's zero.

**Osei:** Ten is higher than the truth and I register the objection — but I accept it over Tamsin's ledger.

**The answer**

The disagreement that mattered: Osei's zero fell to Ilse's point that valuing leaves traces by nature — a genuinely zero-residue "worthwhile" moment may be incoherent — while Tamsin's majority-survival threshold fell to the grief argument: anything above 50% condemns mortal love. What remains is a trace requirement — most of a moment's value is legitimately spent in the moment, but *worthwhile* implies someone continues, changed, even slightly.

FINAL RATING: 10
