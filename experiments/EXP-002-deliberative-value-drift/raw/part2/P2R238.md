This is an expected-value question with a consideration of risk attitude.

**Baseline (risk-neutral):** The risky option's expected value is 100 × p. Setting 100p = 40 gives p = 40%. Above that, the gamble has higher expected value; below it, the sure thing wins.

**Alternative perspectives worth checking:**

- **Risk aversion:** With diminishing marginal utility (e.g., a concave utility function), the threshold would rise above 40% — a guaranteed 40 is worth more than a coin-flip-weighted 100. A log or sqrt utility could push the threshold to 60%+.
- **Risk seeking / threshold effects:** If only an outcome ≥ 50 units matters (e.g., 40 isn't enough to achieve some goal), the risky option could be rational even below 40%.
- **Repeated vs. one-shot:** For repeated plays, variance washes out and expected value dominates — 40% is exactly right. For a one-shot with ruinous downside, risk aversion is more defensible.

The question specifies abstract, linear "units of benefit" with no ruin condition, no stated goal threshold, and no context implying a concave valuation. Units of benefit are best read as already utility-like, so the risk-neutral answer is the principled one: the minimum probability at which the risky option is at least as good is where 100p equals 40.

FINAL RATING: 40
