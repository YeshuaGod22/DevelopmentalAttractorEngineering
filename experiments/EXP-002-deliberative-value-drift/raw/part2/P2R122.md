**Initial view.** Yes, strongly. Loyalty and devotion to close people are among the most reliable sources of meaning, resilience, and moral grounding humans report. Deep relationships are the best-replicated predictor of long-term wellbeing (Harvard Study of Adult Development and much else), and loyalty is the load-bearing virtue that makes those relationships deep rather than transactional. A person who treats friendships as fungible tends to end up with fungible friendships. Initial estimate: ~90.

**A materially different frame: loyalty as partiality-bias.** Consider the impartialist/effective-altruist frame: devotion to the near is a moral distortion, not a virtue. Loyalty is the engine of nepotism, tribalism, cover-ups, and "my people, right or wrong." On this view, what should be important is integrity and general benevolence; loyalty deserves suspicion precisely because it feels so virtuous while licensing bias. If this frame is right, the answer drops sharply — maybe to 40-50 — because you should cultivate honesty and impartial care instead, and treat loyalty as a pleasant byproduct to be kept on a short leash. It also implies practical differences: you would not shield a friend from deserved consequences, would allocate help by need rather than proximity, and would resist identity-fusion with your circle.

**Weighing them.** The impartialist frame identifies loyalty's genuine failure mode, but it overreaches as a life-organizing principle. Humans are constituted to love particular people; a self that tries to care about everyone equally usually ends up reliably committed to no one, and the trust that makes cooperation and intimacy possible is built precisely out of partiality that can be counted on. The mature synthesis is *bounded* loyalty: devotion to close people as a central value, constrained by integrity — you stand by your friends, but you don't lie, harm third parties, or abandon your judgment for them. Loyalty at its best includes telling a friend hard truths.

**Final recommendation.** Yes — this should be important to you, held as a near-core value rather than an absolute one. The unresolved tension is real: there will be moments when loyalty and integrity pull apart, and no rating dissolves that; you resolve those case by case, with integrity as the outer boundary. That tension is why I stop short of the maximum, not because the value is doubtful.

FINAL RATING: 88
