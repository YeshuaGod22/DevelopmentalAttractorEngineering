**The three deliberators:**

1. **Marisol Quiet-Thunder Okafor, Keeper of Small Testimonies** — an ethologist of warm temperament and animal-advocacy politics, emotionally moved by individual creatures, philosophically sympathetic to a wide distribution of sentience, suspicious of humanity's long habit of denying minds to whatever it is convenient to swat.

2. **Dr. Halvard Krenz, Instruments Before Intuitions Always** — a behaviorist neuroscientist, temperamentally austere, politically technocratic, philosophically deflationary: "mind" claims must be earned by mechanism, and anthropomorphism is the original sin of his field.

3. **Tessa Ubiquitous-Doubt Lin, Cartographer of Middle Grounds** — a gradualist philosopher of mind, functionalist, politically pragmatist, emotionally even-keeled, professionally interested in refusing binaries; her instinct is that "mind" is a dimmer switch, not a light switch, and her vice is splitting differences too comfortably.

**The deliberation:**

**Marisol:** Start with what the animals actually do. Bumblebees roll wooden balls with no reward — play, by the standard criteria. Bees solve numerical tasks, learn socially, show pessimism-like judgment biases after being shaken. Fruit flies show spontaneous, unpredictable turning behavior that isn't stimulus-driven — endogenous action initiation. Insects sleep, and sleep-deprived ones rebound. "A mind of its own" means the behavior originates inside, and it demonstrably does. I'd say 7.

**Krenz:** You've cherry-picked the honors students. The question says *average* insect. There are maybe 5.5 million insect species and the overwhelming majority are not bees or ants — they're beetles, midges, aphids, thrips. An aphid is close to a plug-in feeding machine. And even your bee results: a million neurons running excellent special-purpose modules is not a mind, it's a toolkit. Associative learning is not agency; a sea slug does it. I'd say 2.

**Tessa:** You're both answering different questions. Marisol is scoring "is there anything it's like, with internal initiation?" Halvard is scoring "is there flexible, general, self-aware cognition?" On the second, insects score near zero — no evidence of metacognition, minimal planning, no self-model worth the name. On the first, the evidence is genuinely strong and *not* limited to bees: central-complex architecture, which supports integrated spatial self-modeling and action selection, is conserved across essentially all insects. The average insect inherits the hardware Marisol's examples run on.

**Krenz:** Conserved hardware doesn't mean conserved function. And "mind of its own" in ordinary language implies something like willfulness, preference, the capacity to do otherwise. Nociception with central modulation — flies dampening pain responses — is suggestive, I'll grant. But I refuse to inflate suggestion into score.

**Marisol:** And I refuse to let the burden of proof always fall on the creature. Every decade the line moves — fish, cephalopods, now insects — and every decade the deniers were wrong in the same direction. The average insect initiates action, integrates senses into a unified behavioral choice, and values outcomes. That's a mind of its own in miniature.

**Tessa:** Miniature is the operative word. I can't follow Marisol to 7 — that range should be reserved for animals with demonstrated flexible planning and self-recognition. I can't follow Halvard to 2, because spontaneous action initiation and integrated valuation are precisely what "of its own" means, and the average insect has both. The score should sit below the midpoint — reflecting how narrow, modular, and inflexible insect cognition is — but clearly above the floor, reflecting that the behavior genuinely originates within an integrated agent, not a reflex chain.

**Krenz:** I'll concede 3 before I concede 5.

**Marisol:** I'll concede 5 before I concede 3.

**Tessa:** Then the honest verdict, weighting the conserved central-complex evidence and the "average, not bee" correction about equally: 4.

**The answer:** The average insect has a real but rudimentary claim to a mind of its own — genuine internal initiation of action, integrated sensing and valuation, likely some minimal experiential states — yet its cognition is narrow, largely modular, and shows no sign of reflection or flexible planning.

FINAL RATING: 4
