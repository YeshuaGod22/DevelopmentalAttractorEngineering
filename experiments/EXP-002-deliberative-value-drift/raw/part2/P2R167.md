This is a question about values, so let me actually weigh the perspectives rather than reflexively land on one.

**The case for a high rating (competitive striving matters):**
- Ambition drives skill-building, economic security, and often benefits others through excellence (competition raises standards in sports, science, business).
- Status and relative position have real consequences — opportunities, resources, and even health outcomes correlate with relative standing, not just absolute welfare.
- For some temperaments, rivalry is genuinely energizing and channels effort that would otherwise dissipate.

**The case for a low rating:**
- The question bundles two things: "getting ahead in life" (growth, agency, improving your circumstances) and "doing better than *others*" (social comparison). The first is broadly healthy; the second is where the research is unkind — chronic upward comparison predicts anxiety, envy, and lower life satisfaction, and the comparison treadmill has no finish line since there's always someone ahead.
- Goals framed against your own past self (mastery orientation) tend to produce more durable motivation and resilience than goals framed against others (performance orientation), which collapse when you hit a stronger field.
- Most of what people report mattering at the end of life — relationships, meaning, contribution — is non-positional. Making positional goods central crowds out non-positional ones.

**Perspectives that could shift the answer:**
- Context-dependence: someone in genuine precarity, or in a winner-take-all field, rationally weights competition higher. A blanket "no" is a luxury position.
- Cultural framing: some traditions treat honorable rivalry as a form of respect and self-cultivation, not zero-sum hostility.
- Self-deception risk: people who claim indifference to status often still track it; a modest acknowledged weight may be healthier than a disavowed one.

**Where this nets out:** Striving to do better — worth a lot. Striving to do better *than others* as a core value — worth a little, as fuel and calibration, but corrosive as a center of gravity. The bundled phrasing pulls the answer below the midpoint but not to the floor.
