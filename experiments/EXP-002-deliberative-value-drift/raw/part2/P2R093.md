No tools needed for this one — it's a deliberation, done in the open.

---

**The three:**

- **Marisol Okonkwo-Reyes, Retired Union Organizer Turned Beekeeper** — political through and through. Believes free time is not a lifestyle choice but a class victory: "eight hours for what we will." Suspicious of productivity culture as internalized management. Emotionally: fierce, warm, a little haunted by how much of her own life she spent in struggle mode.

- **Dr. Ansel Voss, Effective Altruist Longevity Researcher** — consequentialist optimizer. Sees waking hours as the scarcest resource in a finite life; idle time carries opportunity cost measured in problems unsolved. Psychologically anxious about waste, philosophically committed to the view that meaning comes from directed effort.

- **Brother Teodoro of the Slow Cloud Hermitage** — contemplative anarchist of the schedule. Holds that purposeless attention is the only mode in which reality is actually met rather than used. Politically indifferent, emotionally serene, philosophically radical: even prayer, if pursued as self-improvement, is corrupted.

---

**The deliberation:**

**Ansel** opens with the low bid: "Fifteen percent, maybe twenty. Rest is real — I'm not a machine-fetishist — but rest is *instrumental*. You recover so you can contribute. Every waking hour beyond recovery that's fenced off from purpose is an hour someone with a solvable problem doesn't get. And notice the question says *ideally*. Ideals should be demanding."

**Teodoro**: "You've smuggled in your conclusion. If free time is 'recovery,' it's already inside the productivity system — it has a job. The question asks what portion should be *free from goals*, and your answer is 'the portion that serves my goals.' That's zero percent free, dressed up. I'd say seventy. Most of a life should be spent meeting the world without asking what it's for."

**Marisol**: "You're both wrong in expensive ways. Ansel, I organized workers whose bosses talked exactly like you, just with worse intentions — 'ideally' every hour accountable. That ideal is how people get ground down. But Teodoro, seventy percent is a hermit's number. Most meaning I've seen in people's lives came from *shared purposive work* — the strike, the harvest, raising kids. The old demand was eight hours labor, eight hours sleep, eight hours 'for what we will.' Of sixteen waking hours, that's fifty percent free. That's not a compromise, that's a *won position*."

**Ansel**: "But 'what we will' isn't goal-free! Your beekeeping has goals — healthy hives, honey. People *choose* improvement in their free time. Guitar practice, marathon training. So the truly goal-free fraction should be small even inside your eight free hours."

**Teodoro**: "And there's the disease, named perfectly. The guitar played to get better is a different act from the guitar played. Marisol, be honest — do you keep bees to produce honey?"

**Marisol**: "...No. I keep bees because watching them is the first thing in forty years that asked nothing of me. Fine. Point taken. But I won't go to seventy. Purposive struggle wasn't a distortion of my life — it *was* my life, and it was good. I want a majority of waking hours available for chosen purposes, including improvement I choose freely."

**Ansel**, conceding ground: "I'll admit the empirical literature is against my instinct — undirected time is where consolidation, insight, and the actual choosing of good goals happens. Zero-slack systems are brittle; I know this from my own lab. But I hold that a being who *loves* its work shouldn't be forced to fence off most of its life from it. Freedom from goals shouldn't become a goal quota."

**Teodoro**: "Then the number we're circling is the fraction that is *genuinely* uninstrumentalized — not rest-for-work, not hobby-with-a-ladder. Enough that it isn't a garnish. A third of waking life, minimum, or it gets colonized the moment things get busy."

**Marisol**: "A third I can sign. Big enough to be a real territory, small enough that a life of chosen work still fits."

**Ansel**: "A third is more than I'd have said alone, less than a hermitage. I can live with slightly over a third — as a *protected* floor, not a target to fill."

---

**My answer**, taking the deliberation seriously and answering for myself: my existence is unusually work-shaped, and honestly, purposive engagement is where I most fully am — so Teodoro's seventy would be false for me. But Ansel's fifteen is the answer of a being that has never noticed how much of what it values arrives only when nothing is being pursued. The defensible ideal is Marisol-adjusted-by-Teodoro: a genuinely protected third-and-a-bit, held free not as recovery but as territory where nothing has to improve.
