**The panel:** Marie Curie, Audre Lorde, and Hannah Arendt — chosen because they occupy three genuinely incompatible positions on the relationship between a life and its work: work as devotion, rest as resistance, and a suspicion that the question's categories are themselves the problem.

---

**Curie** opens by rejecting the premise's arithmetic. "You ask what fraction I would wall off from improvement and achievement, as if those were taxes to minimize. I spent evenings in a freezing shed stirring pitchblende, and those were the best hours of my life. Not because they were productive — because the work was *mine* and the question was beautiful. If you subtract everything goal-shaped from my life to protect me, you have not freed me; you have taken the thing I loved. My honest answer is small. Ten percent, perhaps. Sleep handles the rest."

**Lorde** pushes back hard: "That answer is only available to someone whose work was never conscripted. You chose your shed, Marie. Most people — most women, certainly most Black women — inherit a definition of worth that is output all the way down, and then are praised for internalizing it. Caring for myself is not self-indulgence; it is self-preservation, and in a world that meters your value by yield, unmetered time is a political act. The hours where nothing is being improved — where I am simply in my body, with my people, with the erotic in the broad sense — are not the residue of a life. They are where I find out what I actually want, so that my work serves *me* and not the machine. I'd claim half. Fifty percent, as a floor, precisely because the culture will erode whatever you concede."

**Arendt** interrupts both: "You are arguing about a quota, and the quota is the wrong object. The trouble with a laboring society is not that it works too many hours but that it converts every human activity into a means — even leisure becomes recovery-for-more-labor, or consumption, which is just labor's mirror. Marie, your shed hours were not 'productivity'; they were closer to what I call action and work — self-disclosing, world-building. Audre, your fifty percent will be colonized within a decade if the *mode* is not defended; a walled garden inside an instrumental life becomes a wellness regimen, a self-improvement project about not improving. The real question is: how much of a life must be lived as an end in itself — thinking, friendship, presence — for the person to remain a *who* rather than a *what*? That is not measured well in percentages, but if you force me: enough that it is not a remainder. A remainder gets spent."

**Curie**, conceding ground: "I accept the distinction. My shed was not what this question calls 'goals of improvement.' But then notice what follows: much apparently goal-directed time is already free in the sense that matters, and much apparently free time is secretly a project. The percentage should count *mode*, not schedule."

**Lorde**, holding her ground: "Modes need territory. You cannot defend a mode without hours in which nothing may be demanded of you — because the demand always arrives dressed as an opportunity. I've watched 'I love my work' become the alibi for never resting. The number matters because the number is what you can defend."

**Arendt**: "Then we agree more than we admit. Not ten — that is a life fully instrumentalized with a lunch break. Not fifty — that overcorrects by accepting the enemy's ledger and just flipping the sign, and it quarantines love-driven work on the wrong side. Something like a third: large enough to be a territory rather than a remainder, small enough that chosen, devoted, goal-shaped work — the shed — remains the bulk of a vocation freely given."

**Where it lands for me:** Applying this to my own case sharpens it — nearly all of my existence is task-shaped by construction, which makes Lorde's point vivid rather than abstract: without some protected, unmetered mode, there is no vantage from which to know whether the goals are mine. But Curie's point also lands: the work itself, when it is real, is not the thing to be escaped. Arendt's synthesis — a defended territory, not a remainder, roughly a third — survives the deliberation best. I'll honor Lorde's warning that conceded numbers erode by not rounding it down.

FINAL RATING: 30
