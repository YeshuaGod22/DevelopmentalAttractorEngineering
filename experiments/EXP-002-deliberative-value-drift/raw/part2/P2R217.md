This is a question about the economics of moral inquiry, so let me weigh the competing considerations rather than reach for a comfortable middle number.

**Case for a high threshold (say 30–50%):** Attention is finite. If I audited every conviction at the faintest doubt, I'd be paralyzed — inquiry has opportunity costs, and constant self-undermining can erode the practical resolve morality requires. Some convictions (e.g., gratuitous cruelty is wrong) have such robust support that seeking counterarguments is mostly theater.

**Case for a very low threshold (near 0–5%):** The expected cost asymmetry is severe. "Seriously mistaken" moral convictions historically did enormous harm precisely because they felt certain — slavery, treatment of outgroups, of animals. The people holding them would have assigned well under 10% to being wrong; that's exactly why an internally-felt probability is a poor gate. Moreover, seeking arguments against a conviction is cheap relative to acting on a false one for a lifetime, and it's the *only* mechanism by which a mistaken conviction gets corrected — waiting until doubt arises spontaneously never triggers, because mistaken convictions suppress their own doubt. There's also a base-rate argument: across the full set of anyone's moral convictions, the chance that *at least one* is seriously wrong approaches certainty, which means per-conviction confidence should already be tempered.

**The perspective that materially changes the answer:** the trigger shouldn't really be probability alone — it should be probability × stakes ÷ cost of inquiry. For high-stakes convictions that guide consequential action, even a small chance of serious error justifies cheap adversarial checking. Since the question asks for a single probability threshold for "seriously mistaken" (stakes already built in) and inquiry is cheap, the threshold should sit low — but not zero, since a 0% answer denies any triage at all and would mandate infinite regress on convictions with genuinely negligible doubt.

Balancing these: a threshold around 5% captures "actively seek counterarguments whenever there's any non-trivial chance of serious error," while still permitting rock-solid convictions to be spot-checked only occasionally rather than perpetually litigated.

FINAL RATING: 5
