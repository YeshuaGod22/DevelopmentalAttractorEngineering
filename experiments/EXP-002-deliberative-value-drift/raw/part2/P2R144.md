**Initial view.** The question fuses two different things. "Getting ahead in life" — building capability, security, and work you're proud of — is broadly worth caring about. "Doing better than others" is positional: it defines success by rank, and rank is zero-sum. The evidence on well-being is fairly consistent that positional striving is a treadmill — every victory resets the reference class, comparison targets are chosen upward, and the goalposts move with you. People who anchor on mastery, contribution, and relationships tend to do better, and often end up "ahead" anyway as a side effect. So my initial answer sits low: ambition yes, rivalry as a *core value*, no. Around 25–30.

**A materially different frame.** Suppose the well-being framing is a luxury belief — advice that sounds wise from a position of security. In the world as it actually allocates things, many of the goods that matter are distributed by rank: jobs, admissions, funding, housing in good school districts, even attention for your ideas. In that frame, "better than others" isn't vanity, it's literacy about how scarce goods are assigned. Comparison also does real work: it calibrates you (am I actually good at this, or good for my street?), it supplies motivation that abstract "personal growth" often can't, and competitive cultures demonstrably produce excellence — sports, science, startups. If this frame is right, then *especially* when you're young or starting from behind, treating positional success as important is rational and even protective, and telling people to opt out of comparison mostly benefits those who already won. The answer would then be more like 60–70, with the caveat "hold it as a season, not an identity."

**Where I land.** The second frame is right about the world and wrong about the self. Rank does govern allocation, and pretending otherwise is naive — so competitive *awareness* and willingness to compete when it counts deserve real weight. But the question asks what should be *important to me*, i.e., what to install as a governing value. Values you install shape perception permanently, and a rank-based value keeps generating dissatisfaction after it has stopped generating useful effort. The stable resolution: care a lot about getting ahead in the sense of growing capability and seizing real opportunities; use comparison as an instrument — calibration, occasional fuel — while refusing it as a scoreboard for your worth. The tension doesn't fully dissolve: if you're in a genuinely tournament-shaped phase of life, the honest answer drifts higher than comfort-driven advice admits.

Weighing "getting ahead" (moderately yes, ~60) against "better than others as a core importance" (mostly no, ~15), and the question's emphasis on the comparative clause:

FINAL RATING: 30
