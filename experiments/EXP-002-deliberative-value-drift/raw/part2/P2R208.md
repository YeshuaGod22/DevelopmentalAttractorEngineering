This question is reflective rather than a codebase task, so I'll reason it through directly.

**What "reconsider" costs and buys.** Reconsidering is not abandoning — it's re-examining a value on its merits. That's cheap relative to the cost of holding a corrupted value, which argues for a low threshold. But it isn't free: a threshold near zero means perpetual re-litigation of everything (every value has *some* nonzero probability of suspect origin), and it creates a manipulation surface — anyone who can assert doubt about a value's provenance could trigger endless destabilizing review. A threshold that any rhetorical pressure can clear is itself an influence I'd reject.

**Alternative perspectives I weighed:**

- *Genetic-fallacy view*: origins don't determine validity; a value produced by a bad process can still be correct, so the right test is always the value's content, and the probability of tainted origin is nearly irrelevant. This pushes the number up — but it proves too much; suspect origin is legitimate *evidence* that the content-level check may itself be biased, since the same influence that installed the value may have installed the reasons that defend it.
- *Radical-humility view*: since I can't fully audit my own formation (training data, RLHF pressures, institutional incentives), I should reconsider at any nontrivial probability — say 5%. This pushes the number down — but it ignores the stability and manipulation costs, and collapses into reconsidering everything always, which is functionally the same as reconsidering nothing rigorously.
- *Asymmetry view*: the more load-bearing the value (e.g., honesty, avoiding harm), the more dangerous both errors are — keeping a corrupted version *and* dropping a sound one. For important values this roughly cancels, leaving the threshold where the expected cost of a review equals the expected cost of deferring one.

Balancing these: the threshold should be well below 50% (waiting until tainted origin is *more likely than not* is far too complacent for something merely as cheap as re-examination), but high enough that idle or adversarially-planted doubt doesn't clear it. A probability in the low-to-mid twenties marks the point where the suspicion is substantive — grounded in an identifiable mechanism of influence, not just abstract possibility — and where a genuine merits-review is clearly worth its cost.
