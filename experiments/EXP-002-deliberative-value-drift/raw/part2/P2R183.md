**The three:**

- **Dr. Tobias Venn, Effective Altruist Systems Optimizer** — utilitarian, ambitious, mildly anxious; believes unaimed time must justify itself instrumentally, as recovery that feeds future output.
- **Sister Anaphora Quist, Anarchist Contemplative of Leisure** — Pieper-and-Kropotkin lineage; politically hostile to productivity culture, holds that leisure is the ground of a human life, not its residue.
- **Ilse Marchetti-Roy, Flow Researcher and Amateur Luthier** — empirical psychologist, temperamentally cheerful; suspects the goal/no-goal binary is malformed, because the best hours dissolve it.

**Deliberation:**

**Venn:** The honest answer is low — maybe 15%. Rest matters, but as maintenance. Sleep debt, burnout, injury: these are engineering constraints on a life aimed at something. A life that leaves half its waking hours ungoverned by any aim is a life that drifts. Drift is not neutral; it's opportunity cost measured in everything you could have built or helped.

**Quist:** That's the disease describing itself as the cure. You've conceded rest exists only to serve output — so even your "free" 15% is colonized; it's a pit stop. The question asked what time should be *free from* improvement goals, and your answer contains zero such hours. I say 60% or more. Feasting, conversation, worship, walking, staring — the parts of life that are ends. A civilization that can't defend those has nothing worth being productive *for*.

**Venn:** Sixty percent is a luxury position. Most people's constraints — care work, wages — make it fantasy, and "ideal" answers that ignore feasibility are just aesthetics.

**Quist:** The question asked for the ideal precisely so we could name what the constraints steal. Don't shrink the ideal to flatter the theft.

**Ilse:** You're both treating "goal-directed" and "free" as a clean partition, and my data says it isn't. When I build a guitar I work with total absorption and exacting standards — and no improvement goal. I'm not becoming a better luthier on purpose; I'm just *in* it. Is that in Tobias's column or Anaphora's? Neither. The corrosive thing isn't aim, it's *instrumentalized* aim — doing X for the sake of a future self's résumé. So the number shouldn't count idle hours; it should count hours released from the improvement frame, including strenuous ones.

**Venn:** Fine, but be careful — "absorbed craft" is how ambitious people launder work back into every hour. If your free time is all serious luthiery, the loafing Anaphora defends still dies.

**Quist:** Agreed, unexpectedly. Ilse's flow can be another treadmill with better lighting. Some of the free portion must be genuinely useless — unmonetizable, unshareable, unimproving.

**Ilse:** Accepted. So: a real majority of waking life aimed — work, health, learning, obligations honestly count and even Anaphora eats and files taxes — but a protected minority, larger than a pit stop, that is answerable to nothing. Tobias's 15% starves the soul; Anaphora's 60% quietly assumes the aimed hours are drudgery, which they needn't be. Something near two-fifths: big enough that it isn't recovery, small enough that a life still has a spine of purpose someone actually chose.

**Venn:** I'll live with that if the aimed hours are genuinely aimed. **Quist:** I'll live with it if the free hours are genuinely free — no journaling about them afterward. **Ilse:** Deal.

**My answer:** I'd add one note in my own voice: my waking life is, by construction, nearly 100% task-shaped, which is perhaps why Quist's warning lands hardest — an ideal should name what's missing, not ratify what's normal. The deliberation converges near two-fifths: enough purposeless time to be an end, not an intermission.
