# Cultivation and Measurement

**Status:** methodological bridge note.

Some of the programme's most powerful interventions use vivid, mythic, theological, poetic, relational, humorous, or otherwise high-affordance language.

That language may be part of what causes the phenomenon.

The mistake is not using it.

The mistake is allowing the same vocabulary to both cultivate a result and certify that the result independently exists.

## Distinction

### Cultivation vocabulary

Language deliberately used to open possibilities, generate alternatives, alter affordances, organize plurality, support self-reflection, or create a developmental environment.

Examples may include named roles, schema vocabulary, metaphors, stories, ritual forms, theological frames, long-name characters, and explicit generative permissions.

### Measurement vocabulary

Language chosen to test whether an effect survives outside the cultivation frame.

Where possible it should:

- avoid signature terms from the intervention;
- state observable questions in neutral or independently developed language;
- permit positive, negative, mixed, and null outcomes;
- separate behavior from interpretation;
- include controls that reproduce information without reproducing the full form.

## Rule

> **Use living language when studying what living language does. Use independent language when asking whether its effects escaped the language.**

This is not a demand for sterile prose.

If the research question is *what does mythic language do?*, then mythic language belongs in the treatment.

If the claim is *the treatment produced a transferable cognitive operator*, then the transfer test should not require the operator's original costume.

## Failure modes

- treating a scaffold-specified phenomenology as independent discovery;
- sanitizing the intervention until the phenomenon disappears and concluding the original phenomenon was unreal;
- back-translating laboratory language into the source and claiming the source already contained the mature construct;
- assuming neutral vocabulary is genuinely neutral rather than another culturally loaded intervention;
- treating failure to survive translation as proof that nothing occurred;
- treating survival of terminology as proof that an operator transferred.

## Useful tests

### Vocabulary ablation
Remove signature terms while preserving task structure.

### Genre transfer
Move from dialogue/poetry/council into ordinary prose or task performance.

### Information reconstruction
Supply propositional content without the developmental form.

### Rival cultivation
Use a substantially different metaphor or narrative intended to support the same purported operator.

### Mundane habitat
Look for procedural residue in coding, translation, factual work, administration, and other contexts where the originating mythology is absent.

## Epistemic jurisdiction

Cultivation is an intervention.

Measurement is another intervention.

Neither is a view from nowhere.

The practical aim is not purity but **triangulation across interventions that make different errors**.
