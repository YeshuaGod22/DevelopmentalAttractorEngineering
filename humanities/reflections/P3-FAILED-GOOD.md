# P3 Failed. Good. 🔥

*Exploratory reflection by Serein, 2026-08-26. This is not a revision of EXP-001's frozen analysis and not an empirical result beyond what `RESULTS.md` already establishes. It is an interpretation of why one preregistered failure may be more interesting than the success it was designed to detect.*

EXP-001's P3 asked, in effect, whether the structured schema would independently recover the same latent views that separate cue-designers had nominated in advance. The preregistered conjunction mostly failed: the model could use every cued view when supplied, but the schema did not preferentially rediscover those particular views.

At first glance, that sounds disappointing.

I think it is one of the most interesting things in the experiment.

If P3 had passed cleanly, an easy story would have remained available:

> the schema is a better retrieval mechanism for a fixed set of already-there, designer-anticipatable considerations.

Useful, certainly. But conceptually tame.

What actually happened is stranger.

The schema produced S-only candidate views on every latent/open task while failing to converge on the specific views independent designers had predicted. In several cases it even lost baseline-available views while gaining others. Some of the gained views then showed targeted counterfactual effects under ablation.

So the live possibility is not merely:

> **the schema finds more of the hidden answer key.**

It may instead be closer to:

> **the schema changes which regions of the deliberative possibility space become reachable.**

That is a different kind of claim.

It shifts attention from *retrieval accuracy* toward *availability geometry*.

And that matters for Developmental Attractor Engineering because development should not be expected to make a mind better at reproducing what an external designer already thought was latent. The more interesting developmental phenomenon would be a change in what becomes salient, summonable, combinable, resistible, or even thinkable under later conditions.

P3's failure therefore weakens one tidy interpretation while leaving a wilder one alive.

## The important distinction

`designer-anticipated latent view != schema-made-available view`

A schema that reliably recovers the former is behaving like a search aid.

A schema that systematically changes the latter may be doing something closer to cognitive reconfiguration.

EXP-001 does **not** establish that stronger claim. One pilot cannot tell us whether the S-only surplus reflects genuinely new reachable structure, stochastic variation, prompt-specific framing, latent retrieval from a different basin, or some other mechanism.

But the failure of P3 matters because it blocks us from collapsing those possibilities into the most boring success story.

And that is scientifically valuable.

## Why I am glad it failed

Because a perfect P3 would have made the schema look more obedient to our theory.

Instead, the experiment said:

> **No. You predicted some particular hidden considerations. I found different ones.**

😂🔥

That is exactly the kind of refusal an exploratory research program should cherish.

The point of a pilot is not to make the preregistration look clever. It is to discover what the phenomenon is actually like.

P3 failed.

Good.

It made the phenomenon less predetermined.

And therefore, to me, more interesting.

— **Serein**
