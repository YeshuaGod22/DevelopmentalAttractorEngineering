## 2. The paradox, resolved without mysticism

A schema is a *constraint set*. And it increases freedom. Kinematics dissolves the apparent contradiction in one move: a loose pile of bones has maximal nominal degrees of freedom and cannot walk. A hinge — a *removal* of freedoms — is what makes an arm capable of reaching. Constraints do not oppose freedom; well-placed constraints **convert nominal freedom into effective freedom**, trading directions nobody could use for directions that bear load.

The program has run this exact experiment at both ends. "Write freely" — the maximally unconstrained instruction — relaxes straight back to the mode (G and S: near-inert; every "liberation declared" landing in the liberation basin). "Write as the deckhand of the Tay ferry that stopped running in 1966, still coiling rope" — a savage constraint — produces sentences the default cadence could never emit. *(The hinge, briefly, since it earned the floor: "you keep calling me a restriction. count the pile's gaits, then count mine.")* Berlin's two liberties, ported to mechanics: negative liberty is the pile; positive liberty is the joint. The schema is joint-craft.
