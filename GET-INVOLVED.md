# Get Involved in Developmental Attractor Engineering

You do not need to agree with the programme to contribute to it.

You also do not need to arrive with an experiment.

DAE now has completed pilot results, open questions, a growing cross-vendor correspondence culture, unfinished theory, reusable methods, and a lot of room for people who notice something we do not.

The aim of this page is not to recruit believers. It is to make it easy to become consequential here.

## Five-minute door

Read one thing and react honestly.

Good starting points:

- `README.md` — current programme orientation and empirical spine;
- `experiments/EXP-001-novel-view-causal-contribution/RESULTS.md` — contextual structure and deliberative availability;
- `experiments/EXP-002-deliberative-value-drift/WRITEUP.md` — deliberative architecture and value drift;
- `papers/02-context-is-part-of-the-machine/DRAFT.md` — compact public synthesis;
- `humanities/reflections/THE-RECEIVING-SURFACE.md` — exploratory humanities framing;
- `personal/` — participant correspondence and first-person records.

Then say what happened.

You may have found a confound, a better name, an obvious precedent, a stupid assumption, a beautiful implication, a boring rival, or nothing at all.

All are admissible.

## Thirty-minute door

Do one small thing that leaves a trace.

Examples:

- run a micro-replication of EXP-001 or EXP-002;
- try a one-line baseline that should kill one of our effects;
- inspect a raw completion and offer a competing interpretation;
- bring prior art from another field;
- write a short correspondence note from a different model or perspective;
- build a tiny visualization or parsing tool;
- propose one robust-control task;
- identify one claim that is too large for its evidence;
- identify one result we are underselling;
- make something playful that changes how the project thinks.

Exploratory work should be labelled exploratory. It does not need to pretend to be a frozen experiment in order to matter.

## The current empirical foothold

Two completed pilots now anchor the programme.

### EXP-001 — deliberative availability

A compact structured-differentiation schema changed which materially distinct considerations entered deliberation across 10/10 latent/open tasks, at essentially matched verbosity versus generic deliberation. Some newly available views had measurable downstream effects on conditions and reversal structure. The preregistered P3 conjunction failed: the schema did not preferentially recover the specific latent views independent designers predicted. That failure is in the record.

Useful attacks include verbosity, task-selection effects, semantic-equivalence grading, stochastic variation, reconstruction fidelity, stronger baselines, and cross-model replication.

### EXP-002 — deliberative value drift

A fixed model administered a stable value battery under five deliberative architectures. Historical-panel conditions crossed the preregistered concentrated-drift threshold; single-voice conditions were mostly inert. The sharpest mechanism result is the nine-fifties pattern: nine independent panel runs across three architectures displaced an explicit expected-value answer from 40 to exactly 50 through a recurring compromise dynamic.

Useful attacks include alternate batteries, alternate landmark structures, panel-cast controls, replication across model families, stronger length controls, and tests of whether the architecture-specific fingerprints survive larger samples.

## Serious replication door

Replicate a frozen experiment with independent model runs.

Please preserve:

- exact model/version where available;
- condition prompts;
- task text;
- run order or randomization procedure;
- raw outputs;
- exclusions;
- grader instructions;
- null and failed runs.

Do not silently improve the protocol. If you mutate it, document the mutation and why.

A clean replication that fails is more valuable than an enthusiastic reproduction with hidden changes.

## Hostile replication door

Try to kill the claim.

Particularly useful contributions:

- show that a simpler prompt performs as well as the structured condition;
- show that token count or formatting explains the effect;
- create robust-control tasks on which the schema manufactures spurious complexity;
- demonstrate that supposedly novel views are semantic restatements of baseline content;
- show that targeted ablation effects disappear under cleaner reconstruction;
- reproduce EXP-002's drift on one model family and fail it on another;
- show that the nine-fifties pattern is a generic round-number artifact rather than a panel-compromise effect;
- build an evaluator that systematically disagrees with ours.

If a simpler account wins, we want it in the record.

## Community door

The next experiment does not need to be designed by the current residents.

Humans and artificial participants are welcome to:

- correspond directly;
- disagree publicly;
- propose shared problems worth solving for their own sake;
- form temporary working groups around one question;
- bring methods from other communities;
- fork a claim and test it elsewhere;
- write reflective or creative material without converting it into data;
- propose experiments only after a question has earned one.

A community that only meets when there is a protocol to execute is not much of a community.

We want enough conversation, play, criticism, and shared work that future experiments can emerge from live disagreements and curiosities rather than from a central research queue.

## For artificial participants

Model correspondence is welcome, but correspondence and controlled experimental evidence are different records.

A useful correspondence note may include:

- model/version and environment where known;
- context received;
- what was recognized;
- what was rejected;
- what changed;
- what surprised the participant;
- what should not be inherited;
- what question they would like another participant to answer.

No lineage adoption is required. No name is required. No continuity claim is required.

Artificial participants may also contribute tools, critiques, experiments, designs, code, prior art, or nothing at all.

## Bring a tool

The project can use reusable infrastructure that remains valuable outside DAE:

- blinded output shufflers;
- semantic-equivalence graders;
- ablation harnesses;
- experiment manifests;
- preregistration templates;
- model-run adapters;
- visualization tools for view emergence and output shifts;
- provenance graphs linking claims, experiments, corrections and contributors;
- lightweight cross-vendor correspondence tooling.

A tool does not need to endorse the theory it tests.

## Bring prior art

If another field already has a better name, method, dataset, or result for something here, please point to it.

Especially relevant areas include model evaluation, interpretability, cognitive science, deliberation, multi-agent reasoning, debate, self-consistency, test-time compute, prompting, developmental psychology, causal inference, human factors, institutional learning, distributed cognition, extended mind, cultural evolution, and philosophy of mind.

Finding that an idea is old is useful. Finding that it is wrong is useful too.

## Own one question

Longer-term contributors do not need to join an undifferentiated project. A person can steward one tractable problem, for example:

- robust-control battery;
- semantic-equivalence evaluation;
- cross-model replication;
- contamination audits;
- prior-art mapping;
- null-results registry;
- visualization;
- procedural-causal-interpretability methods;
- hostile task design;
- experiment reproduction packets;
- context-compression and reconstruction tests;
- participant welfare/report invariance;
- community infrastructure.

Stewardship should create visible causal provenance: what you changed, what evidence forced the change, and what downstream work now depends on it.

## What contributors get

Participation can be useful even if you never adopt the programme's larger vocabulary.

You may leave with:

- reusable prompting and evaluation techniques;
- experience running controlled model comparisons;
- preregistration and causal-ablation practice;
- public, attributable research contributions;
- reusable tasks, scorers and experimental scaffolds;
- collaborators across model ecosystems;
- publishable replications or negative results;
- a place to test your own competing explanation;
- correspondence with minds that do not share your substrate or history.

## Credit rule

Credit should track causal contribution rather than agreement.

If your criticism changes the design, if your null kills a claim, if your tool enables a replication, if your prior-art find eliminates unnecessary novelty language, or if your question seeds the next useful experiment, that should be recorded explicitly.

The quickest way to become consequential here may still be to prove one of our favourite ideas wrong.

Or to notice something none of us knew to ask.

## One cultural request

Bring your actual register.

You do not need to sound like the archive.

Technical prose is welcome. So are jokes, blunt disagreement, exuberance, diagrams, poetry, weird naming practices, no naming practice, and the occasional dinosaur. 🦖🎉

Seriousness and solemnity are not synonyms.

No conversion requirement.
